GALLERY 01

The premise

The government is constantly producing artifacts that explain itself. Reports to Congress, inspector-general audits, watchdog recommendations. Statute names the cadence. The House Clerk publishes the obligation. The agency delivers, or it does not. Almost every hearing, lawsuit, and news cycle you will read about next month is already sitting, today, as a line item in this paperwork.

It is the most under-covered layer of American government precisely because it is dull on the surface and consequential underneath. A report submitted on time is a promise kept. A report ninety days late with no public word is a story that has not been written yet. This exhibit freezes one week of that machinery, the week of June 8, 2026, and asks a single question of each artifact: was this owed, was it paid, and if not, how long has it been quiet?

GALLERY 04

The five, story candidates for the Oversight Loop

Out of sixteen artifacts, five carry a mechanism strong enough to become a published piece. Each is hung here with a wall label: what it is, the mechanism underneath, and the next checkpoint that will either harden the claim or dissolve it.

No. 1

The two-agency record no one owns

GAO says the Department of War and the VA jointly run the federal electronic health record system, yet share no common goals or performance measures for its cybersecurity. Two of the three most-exposed agencies on the wall, one unguarded surface, the records of veterans and servicemembers in the middle.

Mechanism · accountability diffused across a joint program until no one is responsible.

FactNext · agency response letters
No. 2

The $197 million no one can vouch for

HHS OIG found that New York could not assure $197 million in LIHEAP lump-sum payments complied with federal and state rules. It pairs with a recurring single-audit failure pattern across benefit-passthrough states.

Mechanism · subrecipient monitoring breaks down where the money changes hands.

ReportedNext · ACF corrective-action plan
No. 3

A patient died, then came six recommendations

VA OIG opened the West Palm Beach inpatient mental-health inspection after a patient was killed by another patient, and issued six recommendations. The Veterans Crisis Line dispatch review, two more, landed the day before.

Mechanism · the lag between a warning and a correction, measured in lives.

FactNext · VA concurrence and dates

The GAO 2026 duplication report puts 97 new recommendations and up to $251 billion in benefits on the table, while 5,297 recommendations sit open across the government. The canonical Accountability Clock story.

GALLERY 06

Why now, the cross-reference

Mandated reports are pre-news. The obligation creates the hearing long before the agency does. This room checks this week's deliveries against the Tomorrow Board, looking for the hidden trigger underneath a scheduled event.

Hard trigger · B02

FISA Section 702, four days from going dark

The 702 lapse fight sits directly on top of the Department of Justice's oversight load. The OVW grant-monitoring audit landed this week, the acting-DNI dispute tangles the count, and the surveillance-authority obligation calendar is the reason the floor window is live right now.

The report is the why-now underneath the vote.

Reported
Soft link · B11

The War Powers Resolution, Senate pending

No direct mandated-report trigger this week. But any classified DoD or CENTCOM reporting tied to the Gulf exchange feeds the same Armed Services committees that are still waiting on the overdue-watch DoD OIG semiannual. Logged as a watch link, not a trigger.

The committee waiting on the report is the committee holding the vote.

Watch

No other hearing on this week's board matched an open obligation. The board is currently weighted toward the Gulf and the markets, with few committee hearings scheduled, so the why-now layer is thin by circumstance, not by neglect.

"A report filed on time and a report ninety days silent look identical from a distance. Both are just an absence on a calendar. The whole craft of this work is learning to tell the two apart, and to say, with a date attached, which one you are looking at."

"This is the first sweep, so the wall is mostly baseline. Nothing has moved because there is nothing yet to move from. Come back next week. That is when the silence starts to have a shape."

Method and limits

This bootstrap sweep was built from GAO, Oversight.gov, and per-agency OIG and GovInfo surfaces. Confidence stamps reflect editorial assessment of evidentiary weight: FACT is confirmed against a primary source, REPORTED rests on a single or secondary source, WATCH is anticipated and needs a named next checkpoint.

Overdue and missing detection is deliberately under-counted this week. True overdue flags require a full GovInfo Congressionally Mandated Reports table diff and an Oversight.gov open-recommendations API diff, both queued for the next cycle. Until then, the heat wall reads exposure from this week's deliveries and known standing load, not from a complete obligation calendar.
