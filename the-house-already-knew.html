    The house already knew.

    A sportsbook's alarm caught a fixed bet almost at once. No alarm is on record for the three years one player bet about fifty million dollars.

    Jontay Porter was a backup forward for the Toronto Raptors. He is banned from the N.B.A. for life and has pleaded guilty to a federal crime. This exhibit is about the part of his story that happened before the crime, inside a legal app, and about a simple question: if a betting company can see enough to stop a cheat, what does it see when a customer is drowning?

    3 Two whistles

    One alarm is required. One is optional.

    Sportsbooks run two kinds of watch on their customers. An integrity watch looks for cheating: bets that suggest someone knows the outcome. A harm watch looks for a customer in trouble: someone chasing losses, betting more than they can afford. Start both clocks on the same customer.

    The whistle race

    Press start. Both clocks run on the same account. Watch which one blows.

    The integrity clock reflects reporting that DraftKings flagged Porter's 26 January 2024 prop as that evening's biggest winner among N.B.A. player props, and that regulators "quickly" spotted the bets. REPORTED The harm clock reflects the absence of any public record of an intervention between 2021 and 2023. INFERENCEabsence in the record is not proof nothing happened

    The integrity whistle blew because the law and the leagues require it. Suspicious bets cost the sportsbook money and threaten its license. The harm whistle is different. In most states the duty is soft: offer a way to set limits, offer self exclusion, post a helpline number. A customer who never asks for help can lose for years inside the rules.     4 One dataset, two models

    Sell or protect?

    This is where AI comes in, and the best evidence is about DraftKings, not FanDuel. In September 2026 the New York Times reported that DraftKings built two kinds of machine learning model from its own customers' betting records. One scored customers for promotions. The other tried to flag customers at risk. REPORTED Here are seven things those models looked at. For each, guess which model used it.

    Sort the data

    Tap SELL if you think it fed the promotions model, PROTECT if you think it fed the risk model.
    The promotions model
    Shipped

    It scored each customer's "elasticity": whether an offer would bring in more losses than it cost. An executive told investors AI was used to personalize "hundreds of millions" of promotional dollars. REPORTED

    The protection model
    Shelved

    The team found even simple rules beat guessing at who would need help. Its presentation to company officials in early 2025 was canceled. Two similar efforts were also shelved, former employees said. REPORTED

    0 of 7 sorted.

    DraftKings disputes this account. It says promotions go to customers with "sustained, engaged use," not on the basis of losses; that the promotions test was "preliminary and inconclusive"; that it watches more than two dozen risk indicators; and that flagged or self excluded customers get no promotions. Its chief responsible gaming officer said leaders made a "collective decision" not to use predictive technology for problem gambling because it "wasn't evidence-based." REPORTEDNew York Times via Boston Globe, 21 Sept 2026

    A class action filed on 30 September 2026 makes the same charge in court. ALLEGEDVest v. DraftKings Massachusetts gambling regulators directed staff to question the company about its AI practices. REPORTED

    Your question, answered as far as the record goes

    "If it is smart enough to advertise well, shouldn't it also advertise the right decisions?" By this reporting, the same data and the same methods could do both. One use was built into the business. The other was optional, and it was stopped. Nothing in the public record shows what FanDuel's models did with Porter's account. INFERENCE

      6 The new door

      Same game. Different bouncer.

      Sportsbooks are licensed by states. A newer kind of company, the prediction market, lets you trade on the outcome of a game instead of betting on it. Kalshi and Polymarket are the biggest. They are overseen by a federal agency that regulates financial markets, not by state gambling boards. Pick an age and see which doors open.

      A CNN analysis estimated that users aged 18 to 21 traded about $3.9 billion on sports events on Kalshi this year. Kalshi says that age group is 3.14 percent of its volume. REPORTEDFortune, 31 Aug 2026 Kalshi began blocking athletes and politicians from markets tied to them in March 2026, and added deposit limits and alerts for users under 21 in late October. REPORTED

      Kalshi's position is that it is a neutral marketplace: it "does not set odds, does not act as a counterparty, and does not profit from customer losses." REPORTEDits testimony in Connecticut States disagree about who gets to regulate it. By late June 2026 the federal agency had sued at least nine states to stop them from restricting these markets.
