Decided Before You Act

A store camera flags your face before you shop. A box in your car caps your speed before you press the pedal. Your driving data prices your insurance before you file a claim. A trading model guesses your next move before you make it. More and more systems act on a model of you, and American law was mostly built to judge what you did.

    02Why the law fits badly

    Built for acts, facing forecasts

    Due process, discrimination law and consumer protection mostly start from something that happened: an arrest, a denial, a deceptive sale. A forecast changes what you are offered before anything happens, and often no one tells you it was used. INFERENCE The one big exception is credit-style reporting, where the Fair Credit Reporting Act already makes companies tell you when a report hurt you and lets you see and dispute the file. FACT

    Who holds the file on you, and how to ask for it

    Pick the decision that affected you. Each panel names who likely holds the record, the request route Infera verified, the law behind it, and what to do if it is wrong. This is general information, not legal advice. INFERENCE

    The pattern across all eight: where a decision runs through a consumer reporting company, you have a right to see and dispute the file. Where it runs through a store, a vendor's model or an employer's own software, you often have nothing, unless your state wrote something in. INFERENCE

    04Counterexamples · where the law caught up

    It can be done. Here is where it was.

    The fixes that held share a shape: an old law with an enforcer willing to use it, and a choke point every decision has to pass through, such as a warrant, a data broker or a lender's notice. INFERENCE

    Detroit · police face recognition

    A match is a lead, not an arrest

    Robert Williams was wrongly arrested from a face match. His 2024 settlement, binding on the city, bars arrests or lineups based only on a match and required an audit of every warrant since 2017 that used one. FACT Violent-crime searches fell from about 100 in 2023 to 9 in 2025, and one produced a lead. REPORTED

    Limit: all nine 2025 searches targeted Black men, the audit's results have not been released, and Detroit can still ask State Police to run searches. REPORTED

    FTC · General Motors

    Every driver can ask for the data

    The FTC's final order of January 2026 runs 20 years. GM may not give driving-behavior or location data to consumer reporting agencies for five years, must get affirmative consent, and must let every U.S. consumer request a copy and deletion. FACT

    Limit: no money for drivers and no admission, and the reporting ban ends after five years. FACT

    Colorado and 28 others · insurance

    Insurers must govern their models

    Colorado extended its rule against unfair discrimination by insurer algorithms from life to auto and health insurance in 2025. It requires board oversight, fixes for unfair outcomes and enough information for consumers to act on an adverse decision. 29 jurisdictions have adopted the regulators' model AI bulletin. FACT

    Limit: the bulletin is guidance, and Colorado's quantitative bias test is still a draft. REPORTED

    Massachusetts · AI lending

    A state enforced what federal regulators dropped

    In May 2025 the CFPB withdrew its guidance that AI-driven denials still need specific reasons. FACT Two months later, Massachusetts settled with student lender Earnest for $2.5 million over a model variable that penalized Black and Hispanic applicants, a rule that auto-denied applicants by immigration status, and inaccurate denial notices. FACT

    Limit: a settlement, so nothing was decided in court. The federal reasons requirement in the Equal Credit Opportunity Act still stands. INFERENCE

    European Union · AI Act

    Some predictions are banned outright

    Since Feb. 2, 2025, the EU bans emotion recognition at work and in schools and predictive policing based only on profiling. The 2026 changes left the bans in place and added two more. FACT

    Limit: no confirmed fine under these bans yet, and the high-risk rules were pushed to December 2027. WATCH

    The data broker side · Verisk

    One seller walked away

    After a March 2024 report that GM driving data was reaching insurers, Verisk stopped providing its Driving Behavior Data History Report, telling a privacy group that GM, Honda and Hyundai had supplied data. FACT

    Limit: a business decision under public pressure, not a rule. LexisNexis still runs a telematics exchange that insurers can query. FACT

    05One case, every layer

    How GM drivers got protection in layers

    No single law covered it. Protection arrived in layers, from a newspaper, a regulator, a court, states and a competitor's marketing. That is what "the system catching up" looks like in practice: slow, overlapping and uneven by state. INFERENCE

    1. March 2024

      The report. A newspaper investigation shows GM driving data reaching insurers through brokers. FACT

    2. May to June 2024

      A seller exits. Verisk drops its driving-behavior report. FACT

    3. January 2026

      The regulator. FTC finalizes its 20-year order: consent, access and deletion for every U.S. consumer. FACT

    4. April 2026

      The court. Judge Thomas Thrash lets the consolidated class action go forward in Georgia, including wiretap, stored communications and FCRA claims against GM, OnStar, LexisNexis and Verisk; some claims were dismissed. REPORTED

    5. May 2026

      One state alone. California settles separately for $12.75 million. REPORTED

    6. Oct. 29, 2026

      The states. 35 states and D.C. settle for $220 million. GM must get affirmative consent, limit collection and oversee dealers. Nothing goes to drivers. FACT

    7. Nov. 2, 2026

      The market. Ford publishes a privacy promise saying it does not sell data about how customers drive to data brokers or insurers. A company pledge, not a legal duty. FACT

    8. Pending

      The next rules. California's AB 311 would let insurers use telematics only with consent and approved discounts; it was in a Senate committee in July. REPORTED A 2025 Maryland bill would have barred insurers from using telematics to cancel or non-renew policies; its outcome is not confirmed. WATCH

    06Both sides

    Smarter decisions, or guilt in advance?

    For prediction

    • Pricing on actual driving can reward careful drivers who used to pay for others; California's AB 311 is written around that idea. REPORTED
    • A small group of repeat speeders causes outsized harm, and a limiter lets them keep a license instead of driving suspended. REPORTED
    • Brokerages say behavioral forecasts help them manage margin and liquidity risk that can hurt all customers in a crash. REPORTED
    • Where rules exist, as in credit, a predicted decision comes with a notice and a dispute right that many human judgments never had. INFERENCE

    Against, or wary

    • Forecasts can repeat old patterns: Detroit's 2025 searches all targeted Black men, per police data reported by BridgeDetroit REPORTED, and Massachusetts alleged Earnest's model penalized Black and Hispanic applicants. FACT
    • Most people never learn a prediction was used, so they cannot use the rights they have. INFERENCE
    • 42AI's own CTO called it "a valid concern" that a brokerage could use forecasts against its customers; the founders did not explain what would stop it. REPORTED
    • Federal guidance is retreating while states move, so your rights depend on your ZIP code. FACT
