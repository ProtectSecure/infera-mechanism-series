Mechanism Brief № 014Nov. 8, 2026~15 minAI training · the loop that feeds itself

The Photocopy Problem

About half of new English web articles are now machine-written. AI trained on that output loses the rare things first: the odd phrase, the local fact, the minority view. Labs can stop that if they keep human data in the mix. The harder part to stop is the same narrowing in people, because more and more of us now write and think through the same few models.

~50%Share of new English web articles that are mainly AI-written, flat since early 2025 (Graphite, detector-based) REPORTED
50%+Share of new music uploads to Deezer that are AI-generated, June 2026; about 90,000 tracks a day REPORTED
25%Drop in Stack Overflow activity within six months of ChatGPT, against comparable sites FACT

02The viral version, checked

"Irreversible brain damage" for AI? Not quite.

A post spreading this month calls a "new" Oxford paper proof that AI is poisoning itself. The paper is real, but it came out in July 2024, and the research since has narrowed what it means. FACT

The claimWhat the evidence says
"A new paper in Nature"False
Shumailov et al. "AI models collapse when trained on recursively generated data," was published in July 2024 by an Oxford-led team. FACT
"ChatGPT has a big problem"Not shown
The paper tested a 125-million-parameter language model, image models and simple statistical models, not ChatGPT or any frontier system. FACT
The rare "tails" disappearTrue, in that setup
Each generation trained on the last one's output, and the rare cases vanished first. Image models in a similar loop lost quality or diversity unless each round got enough fresh real data (Alemohammad et al. 2024). FACT
"Irreversible," "collapses in on itself"Overstated
Collapse needs the human data to be replaced. When synthetic data is added to the original real data, error stays bounded (Gerstgrasser et al. 2024). A 2025 Stanford and Harvard review found the scariest results assume all past data is deleted each round, which no frontier lab does. Checking synthetic data before training on it also prevents collapse (Feng et al. 2025). FACT
But a little can hurtOpen
In a theoretical setting, synthetic data as small as 1% of training data can stop gains from more data (Dohmatob et al. 2025). A September 2026 preprint found some of the fastest collapse seen in lab pipelines came from a sampling setting that reused the same random draws each round. REPORTED
Human data becomes the valuable commoditySupported
Courts, licensing deals and a new industry of paid expert data all now put a price on human work. See section 06. FACT

Collapse is a risk of engineering choices, not a law of nature. Three levers decide it: keep the human data, check what the machine makes, and sample honestly. The danger rises wherever one of those is cheap to skip. INFERENCE

03The loop nobody controls

The machines can be fixed. We are the other loop.

In the machine loop, models learn from models, and labs control the recipe. In the human loop, people learn from models. Nobody controls that one, and it is already measurable. INFERENCE

Psychology · creativity

Better alone, more alike together

With AI story ideas, 300 writers scored up to 8.1% higher on novelty, and the least creative gained most. Their stories were also about 10% more similar to one another. FACT

Doshi and Hauser, Science Advances, July 2024. One task, one model generation.

Cognition · memory

Writers who could not quote themselves

Of 54 students writing with GPT-4o, more than 80% could not accurately quote their own essay after the first session. EEG showed the weakest brain connectivity of three groups, and graders called the essays generic. REPORTED

MIT Media Lab, 2025. Small sample, not peer reviewed.

Language · speech

Chatbot words in human mouths

Across 740,249 hours of academic talks and podcasts, "delve" and other words ChatGPT favors rose in spoken human language after its release. In biomedical abstracts, at least 13.5% of 2024 papers show signs of LLM processing. REPORTED

Max Planck Institute preprint; the abstracts study is peer reviewed (Kobak et al. Science Advances).

Culture · whose defaults

Diwali, written as Christmas

AI autocomplete suggested pizza and Christmas to Indian writers and made their Diwali essays more generic. Indian and American writing both drifted toward American style. FACT

Cornell study of 118 writers, CHI 2025.

The commons · who answers

The places the models learned from are thinning

Stack Overflow activity fell 25% within six months of ChatGPT. Wikipedia's human pageviews fell about 8% year over year from March to August 2025, which Wikimedia ties to AI answers in search. FACT

This is the replace scenario at the scale of society: fewer new human answers going into the pool. INFERENCE

Labs · inside the model

Variety is trained out, not just sampled out

In the open Olmo 3 models, most loss of semantic diversity happens during fine-tuning, so changing how answers are generated does not bring it back. One dominant model also loses knowledge faster than several models splitting the same data. REPORTED

Two 2026 preprints (Karouzos et al.; Hodel and West, University of Washington).

04The loop moves into the physical world

World models need real streets

The next wave of AI, "world models," learns physics and space from video and scans so robots and cars can move through real places. They face the same problem. Simulated worlds are cheap and endless, but the rare real moments, like a near miss or a strange intersection, are the tails a simulation tends to lose. That makes real-world capture the scarce human data of physical AI. INFERENCE

  1. Through 2025

    The capture. Pokémon Go and Ingress players who opted into AR scanning of PokéStops and Gyms helped build Niantic's map of public places. Normal play did not create scans. FACT

  2. May 2025

    The split. Niantic sells its games to Scopely for $3.5 billion (closed May 29) and spins out Niantic Spatial, led by John Hanke, with Google Earth co-creator Brian McClendon as CTO. FACT

  3. August 2025

    Synthetic worlds. Google DeepMind's Genie 3 generates interactive 3D worlds from text in real time. FACT

  4. December 2025

    The defense link. Niantic Spatial partners with Vantor, a spatial intelligence firm with defense and intelligence clients, on navigation for drones and ground vehicles where GPS fails. FACT

  5. February 2026

    Simulation for the road. Waymo builds a driving world model on Genie 3. REPORTED

  6. March 2026

    The payoff. Niantic Spatial's model, trained on 30 billion images from players, places a phone within centimeters at about a million locations. Coco's sidewalk delivery robots use it. Yann LeCun's AMI Labs raises $1.03 billion for world models. REPORTED

  7. June 2026

    The dispute. Dutch papers report player scans trained military drone navigation. Not confirmed: Vantor denies using Pokémon Go data, and Niantic Spatial says scans fed only an early model, scanning has ended, and game data no longer flows to it. Nvidia releases open Cosmos 3 world models. REPORTED

Players said yes to helping an AR game. A decade later, a model built partly on their scans serves robot delivery and a partner with defense clients. "Submitted under the terms in effect at the time" is the consent gap, and it is the same one Brief № 011 tracks. INFERENCE

05Use it · Your digital twin

Your twin learns from you. Then you write with your twin.

Personal AI "twins" now draft email, answer for people and stand in for them in research. Stanford built agents of 1,052 people from two-hour interviews that matched their survey answers 85% as well as the people matched their own two weeks later. FACT But language models standing in for an identity group give less varied answers than real people do, often reading like an outsider's stereotype. REPORTED Run our loop on one person and the rare parts of you fade the same way.

Rare traits your twin still uses after 10 updates, by how much of your new writing you do without it (our model)
All writing assisted0%
10% unassisted10%
30% unassisted36%
Twin keeps your full original archive43%
With the archive kept, nothing is deleted, but the rare traits are diluted. INFERENCE
  1. Seal a pre-AI archive.Keep the writing, voice memos and photos you made before AI tools, stored apart from anything a model drafted.
  2. Label what was assisted.Note which emails and documents a tool drafted. A twin, or a company, can then weight the unassisted ones.
  3. Refresh it from you, not from its drafts.Update a twin with new interviews or unassisted writing, not with mail it helped write.
  4. Write some things alone.The unassisted share is the floor. Even a small one keeps the rare parts of you in the record.
  5. Ask what it is trained on.Before you sign up for a voice clone or memorial bot, ask whether it keeps your originals, and whether it learns from its own output.

Stanford HAI (May 2025); Wang, Morgenstern and Dickerson (2025). The steps are general guidance, not product advice. INFERENCE

07Both sides

A manageable engineering risk, or a slow cultural one?

The worry is overblown

  • No frontier lab deletes its human data, and adding synthetic data to it does not cause collapse in the studies. FACT
  • Synthetic data has reliably improved models where answers can be checked, such as math and code. FACT
  • AI help lifts the people starting furthest behind: weaker writers, non-native speakers, people without an editor. FACT
  • On Deezer, listeners still overwhelmingly choose human music even as AI floods uploads. REPORTED

The worry is real

  • Labs do not publish their data mix, so no one outside can check how much synthetic data goes in. INFERENCE
  • Public human text could be fully used between 2026 and 2032, by Epoch AI's estimate, which raises pressure to lean on synthetic data. FACT
  • Studies already show writing, speech and culture converging, and no lab controls that loop. FACT
  • Even the 2025 skeptics' review calls the loss of rare data a genuine risk, especially for minority groups. FACT
