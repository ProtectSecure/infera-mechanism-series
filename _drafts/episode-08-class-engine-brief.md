## Why this is the load-bearing episode

EP 02 through EP 07 named the mechanisms. EP 07 named the cost. EP 08 has a different job: **it names the victims, by callback to the publication's own prior reporting, and proves the mechanisms produced the outcomes.**

This is the move that converts the publication from two separate seasons of journalism into a single argument. The Eviction Funnel was the same machine as The Trust Market. The Closed Hospital was the same machine as The Composite State. The Trickle was the same machine as Personhood Inc. plus The Trust Market. The Provisional Ballot was the same machine as The Influence War. Each Season I family the reader remembers — by name, by photograph, by location — appears in this episode as the **destination** of a specific Season II mechanism.

Once the reader has read EP 08, they cannot read any Season I episode the same way again. They cannot read any Season II episode the same way either. The bowtie is the publication's promise: every thread connects, and the connection has been documentable all along.

## Six-Act Chassis

### HERO · *Familiarity (with new weight)*

> *You've read these families before. You did not know what was sorting them.*

Open with five photographs — one from each Season I episode the chapter will trace — captioned only with first names and ZIP codes. The reader recognizes them. The image grid sits without commentary for a beat. Then the line: *"Each of these families appeared in a different story. They were not in different stories. They were in different parts of the same machine."*

### PRESSURE · *Unease*

The thesis stated cleanly: every outcome the reader watched in Season I — eviction, foreclosure, denial, displacement, ballot challenge, settlement underpayment — was produced by composite scoring of the kind EP 02 through EP 07 have just spent six episodes describing. No single act of sorting is dramatic. The compounding effect across years and across categories is what produces what social scientists call *cumulative disadvantage* and what this episode will call, more honestly, *engineered class*.

The machine does not intend to stratify. It just does. Compounding optimization, run at scale, produces a digital caste the optimizer would never have written down. That is the mechanism of the engine.

### PIPELINE · *Recognition · the five chapters*

The episode walks five outcomes. For each, it surfaces the Season I episode where that outcome happened to a specific family, then traces the Season II mechanism that produced it. Each chapter ends with a clickable breadcrumb to the original Season I story — which the reader can follow or note and keep going.

The composite plus the influence machine plus the trust score added up to a voter whose ability to vote was materially shaped by all three. Provisional-ballot challenges were not random; they targeted the composites the same architecture targets ads. Down-ballot anomalies — the runoff tells — were the surface trace of an influence operation, not the noise of inattention.

> Breadcrumb: *Read the original case — [S1·05 The Provisional Ballot] · [S1·10 The Runoff Tells]*

The chapter closes with: *"Your vote was not lost in chaos. Your vote was lost in pattern."*

### OUTCOME · *Implication*

The compounding effect across the five chapters is not additive. It is multiplicative. A household sorted disadvantageously in any one chapter is statistically more likely to be sorted disadvantageously in the others — because the same composite feeds all five. The class engine is the integration of all five sorting outcomes against the same composite, refreshed daily.

The honest social-science term is *cumulative disadvantage*. The honest editorial term is *engineered class*. The episode uses both, in that order, so the reader sees the documented language first and the editorial framing second.

### COMPETING · *Systems Realization (one half)*

Four defenses hosted fairly:

1. **No single actor's intent.** No housing platform, no scoring vendor, no insurance underwriter, no hospital system intended to produce a digital caste. The result is emergent from many separate optimization decisions, each rational in isolation.
2. **The mechanisms also produce gains for many users.** Lower friction, faster decisions, broader access in some categories than would have existed under the prior manual regime. The system is not pure harm.
3. **Some of the outcomes pre-existed the mechanism.** Inequality preceded composite scoring. The defenders will argue (correctly) that the machine accelerates and refines existing patterns more than it invents new ones.
4. **The data and the optimization are publicly accessible in principle.** Auditing is possible. Regulatory remedies exist (in some jurisdictions). The system is not a black box if anyone with standing chooses to look inside.

Each defense holds in part. The episode hosts each fairly and names the specific places where the defense breaks down.

### DISCIPLINE · *Systems Realization (other half)*

Three refusals:

1. **No conspiracy.** The machine is not coordinated. The result is not designed. Naming it a class engine is naming a *systemic pattern*, not a *plot*.
2. **No determinism.** Not every reader is sorted the same way; not every chapter applies equally to every household. The episode must label every modeled aggregate as modeled and every documented family-level outcome as documented.
3. **No despair.** EP 12 (The Governance Layer) will name the political instruments that could change any of this. EP 08 must hand the reader off to EP 12 with the conviction that the outcomes are produced, not given.

What we did NOT prove:
- We did not prove the five Season I families in this episode were ALL sorted by the same scoring vendor.
- We did not prove the Provisional Ballot challenges in S1·05 used the same composite as the targeting in EP 04 (we showed the architectures are compatible).
- We did not prove the Hospital Closes record dispersal goes to the same Composite State infrastructure described in EP 06 (we showed the architectures are compatible).

What we *did* prove:
- That the mechanisms described in EP 02–07 produce outcomes of the kind documented in S1·01–S1·10.
- That at least one of the five families in each chapter can be traced from a documented Season I outcome back to a specific Season II mechanism via a documented inference chain.
- That the compounding effect across categories is multiplicative, not additive, because the same composite feeds all five.
- That this pattern matches the definition of cumulative disadvantage in the published literature.

## Interactive Core · The Class Atlas

A single participatory mechanic with five panels, one per chapter.

The reader sees a stylized diagram of a household — a small icon of a family — at the center. Five orbits surround them, one per chapter (housing, employment, healthcare, credit, civic). Each orbit contains the relevant Season II mechanism (composite, score, feed, etc.) and the relevant Season I family.

The reader clicks a Season II mechanism on any orbit; the orbit lights up and a small arrow traces from the mechanism through the household icon to the Season I family. The reader sees: *this score → this household → this outcome*.

The reader clicks any Season I family on any orbit; the arrow reverses — the family lights up, traces back through the household, to the mechanism that produced the outcome. The reader sees: *this outcome ← this household ← this score*.

The reader clicks all five Season II mechanisms in sequence; the diagram pulses; the family icon at the center darkens; the line emerges: *"Five mechanisms. One household. One class."* The reveal lasts three seconds. Then the diagram resets and the reader can replay with a different chapter combination.

The mechanic is the visual proof of the compounding effect that the prose chapter describes. The reader's own clicks demonstrate multiplicative integration.
