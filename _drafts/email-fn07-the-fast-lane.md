## Body

A new field note is live, and this one is about something that almost no one will report, which is exactly why we did.

**The headline.** On June 9 the State Department published a forgettable fee rule. Inside it: at busy embassies and consulates, you can now pay **750 dollars** to move to the front of the visa appointment line. It is framed around the crowds coming for the 2026 World Cup and the 2028 LA Olympics. It starts July 1 and is labeled a pilot.

**The mechanism.** Burden Shift. A fee does not add interviewers or hours. The same number of people get seen. So when a paid lane opens in front of a fixed window, everyone who does not pay moves back, not because anything was taken, but because something was added ahead of them. The cost is real and invisible at once: it lands as a slightly longer wait, spread across the people with the least leverage to refuse it.

**Where.** Every high-demand consular post, worldwide. Which is to say: a quiet change to how a public line works, made one temporary final rule at a time.

**What we found.** The tell is not the start date everyone will quote. It is the end date almost no one will. The rule expires December 31, which is what lets everyone call it a pilot. But pilots that produce revenue have a way of getting renewed. The moment to watch is not the launch. It is the quiet extension after the World Cup justification expires but the income does not.

The note is a walkthrough, not a lecture. There is an interactive queue you can reorder yourself to watch the burden move, a three-rung ladder from the fee to what it means, and the honest case both for and against, so the view you leave with is yours.

**And there is a lever, open right now.** The Department is taking written comments until **July 9**. The question on the table is plain: should a public appointment system sell front-of-line access? You do not have to be an applicant or an expert. For it or against it, the same channel is open. We link straight to the rule and the comment button.
