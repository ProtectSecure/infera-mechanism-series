### OUTCOME · *Implication*

v1's three reader populations survive (jurisdictions with levers, without levers, without local access). New in v2, a fourth: **readers who belong to an institution** (a congregation, a union local, a school board, a family). For them the nearest lever is a written policy, and the Faith Layer has already drafted four.

Named directly: **the patchwork is not coherent, but the patches are dated, and some of them are yours.**

### COMPETING · *Systems Realization (one half)*

v1's four defenses, hosted unchanged in substance (innovation, federalism, sectoral law, market correction), plus one earned by the Afterlife levers:

5. **Families decide, not statutes.** Mourning is private; a griefbot made by a consenting family is nobody's business. A data directive should be optional, never default. (The Shotline families and many griefbot users would say this.)

### DISCIPLINE · *Systems Realization (other half)*

Refusals: no utopianism, no nihilism, **no partisanship** (the four hero cases come from a Democratic-led FCC, a Republican-led FTC's letter, a private estate and a state appellate court; the instrument table pairs red-state and blue-state laws wherever both exist).

What we did NOT prove: that any lever would have prevented any specific harm; that the table is complete.
What we DID prove: that each of the four hero levers moved on a dated day; that every ◌ in EP 10 has at least one proposed or existing instrument somewhere.

**The scorecard.** The publication pre-registered outcomes in Briefs 006 and 007 and in Trickle Part Two. v2 prints our hit rate at the top of this episode, including misses. A governance episode that grades others should show its own grade first.
