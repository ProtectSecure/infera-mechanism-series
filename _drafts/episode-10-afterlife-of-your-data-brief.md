## What changed in v2, and why

v1 was a map of what survives a death. Since June the publication has watched the dead's data **change hands in real time**, in three different kinds of room:

- **A courtroom.** Brief № 006 followed Spirit's workplace archive to a $10 million winning bid from Google (REPORTED). Brief № 007 is following Character.AI's estate, where chats and user-built characters, including personas of people who have died, sit in a bankruptcy with a November 24 sale hearing and no consumer privacy ombudsman appointed as of October 23 (WATCH).
- **A store.** Grandma, Updated documented the commercial afterlife: genealogy animation, obituary scrapers, a broker listing that gave a dead man a "reputation score" of 3.6 out of 5 (REPORTED).
- **A shadow.** The Shadow Market dispatch covered the criminal after-market for breach data. Folded in here, because the obituary scraper is the hinge between the gray market and the black one.

So v2 is built on one organizing image: **three buyers for the dead.** The court, the store, and the shadow. Each one wants something different; none of them asked.

The Second Death already carried the grief and the theology. This episode carries the *infrastructure*, and points mourners back to the exhibit for the rest.

## Six-Act Chassis

### HERO · *Familiarity* · The notification

Someone you knew has died. Their account still sends you a birthday reminder. In a 2024 survey of 2,112 French adults, 29% had received automatic notifications from a dead person's account and 31% had seen content from one (FACT, CNIL). Open on a phone lock screen with one such notification. Tap it, and the page opens the three doors.

*Callback:* the lock screen is the same device frame used in The Companion Confessor's 2 a.m. scene. Different hour, same phone.

### PRESSURE · *Unease* · What survives, with persistence marks

v1's survival map, kept and re-graded with the persistence marks (◉ continues · ◯ memorialized · ◌ no protocol):

- Bank and credit ◉ until the estate closes them; the labor is the family's.
- Medical records ◉ under HIPAA retention, *unless the custodian dies first* (Crozer: destruction scheduled after March 2, 2027, per Brief № 002). → Special Report.
- Civic records ◉ until notification cascades clear them; asymmetric by level.
- Platform accounts ◯ or ◌ at platform discretion. A legacy contact on a memorialized Facebook account cannot read messages (REPORTED, as published in The Second Death).
- Broker profiles ◌. No statute requires retirement.
- The replica ◌. No retirement protocol anywhere in US law.
- Message archives used to train a bot ◌. RUFADAA (adopted in about 46 states, REPORTED, verify) governs *access* to an account, not *training* a model on it (INFERENCE).

### PIPELINE · *Recognition* · Three buyers

#### Buyer 1 · The Court (the estate sale)

When a company holding people's data fails, the data becomes estate property. The federal brake, 11 U.S.C. §363(b)(1), protects personally identifiable information a *customer* gave *about themselves*, defined by §101(41A) around "personal, family, or household purposes" (FACT, statute). Three consequences the reader can feel:

- **The worker's door has no handle.** Spirit's 17,000 employees were not consumers (Brief 006).
- **The dead are not customers.** A grieving user who typed a mother's memories into a chatbot was the customer; the mother was not (Brief 007, INFERENCE).
- **Users may not even be creditors.** Character.AI told the court its roughly 163 million current and past accounts are not creditors entitled to mailed notice (REPORTED).

The single precedent where the dead were named: the 23andMe ombudsman recommended "posthumous use safeguards, since people who have died cannot delete their data" (REPORTED, WashU). *Easter egg:* this sentence is the hinge quote of three pages (Brief 007, this episode, EP 11). Readers who notice it are watching a lever travel.

#### Buyer 2 · The Store (the commercial afterlife)

Lawful, polite, and everywhere. Photo animation of ancestors at the scale of nearly 100 million animations by 2022 (FACT, company release). Talking-ancestor scripts written from census and marriage records in synthetic voices (REPORTED). Broker listings of the dead with addresses, relatives and a score (REPORTED). AI obituaries appearing on six sites within 48 hours of one death, padded and sometimes false, monetized through ad redirects (REPORTED, Secureworks via Sophos). And the griefbot itself, priced in tiers (EP 09's rent ledger, line four).

*Callback to Grandma, Updated:* the "as she was / as we wish" slider is how this buyer edits the product. Sanctification is a known grief pattern (FACT, Lopata 1981); the store sells it as a feature.

#### Buyer 3 · The Shadow (the criminal after-market) · formerly *The Shadow Market*

The dispatch's core frame survives intact: *the dark web is infrastructure, and what flows through it is the question.* Tor began with US Naval Research Laboratory funding and serves journalists and survivors as well as markets (REPORTED). What is new in v2 is the focus on the dead:

- **Deceased identities are attack surface.** Nobody monitors the credit of a person who has died, and families learn of fraud late. (WATCH: locate a current primary estimate of deceased-identity fraud; do not reuse older vendor figures without a check.)
- **Obituaries are the targeting list.** The same obituary that a scraper pads for ad revenue gives a fraud crew the name, dates, town and relatives. The gray market feeds the black one (INFERENCE from the Secureworks finding).
- **Breach data outlives the breach.** Every resolved breach keeps circulating; the dead in those dumps cannot reset a password.
- **AI on both sides.** Voice clones for "grandchild in trouble" calls on the attack side; anomaly detection on the defense side. The asymmetry currently favors attackers (INFERENCE, hosted against vendor counter-claims).

The dispatch's governance line becomes this act's closing line: *the shadow market is governed in pieces, by many actors, with no integrator.* That phrase is planted here on purpose. It is the title-question of Season III's *The Custodian Gap*.

### OUTCOME · *Implication*

1. **Families with time and literacy** close the visible accounts. Buyer 2 and Buyer 3 still have the rest.
2. **Families without them** inherit friction: benefit overpayment recovery, voter-roll lag, fraud discovered at probate. *Callback to S1·02 The Eviction Funnel:* a chain of administrative steps converting one hardship into another, now running after death.
3. **Communities** absorb the aggregate: rolls carrying the dead (S1·05), patient records of the dead dispersed or destroyed when hospitals close (S1·03 → Special Report).

Named directly: **death is a data event the infrastructure was not designed to recognize. The market recognized it first.**

### COMPETING · *Systems Realization (one half)*

1. **Memorialization serves grief.** In one lab study all 16 participants said they would use a "generative ghost" again (REPORTED, Science News). Many families want the account kept.
2. **Retention has lawful purposes.** Fraud detection, research, creditor and tax claims.
3. **Some law exists.** France lets a person leave directives for their data after death and voids terms that override them (REPORTED, art. 85). RUFADAA gives fiduciaries a path to accounts.
4. **Planning works.** Legacy contacts, deletion settings, a written card. The Second Death's *Before I Go* card exists because this is true.
5. **The aftermarket is downstream of breaches.** Security at the source shrinks Buyer 3 more than policing the market (carried over from The Shadow Market counter-read).

### DISCIPLINE · *Systems Realization (other half)*

Refusals: no morbid framing; no single story (jurisdiction and family resources change everything); no conspiracy (most retention is inertia, not intent).

What we did NOT prove:
- Harm in any specific deceased person's case beyond reported examples.
- The scale of deceased-identity fraud today (WATCH).
- What Character.AI's estate will do with chats. Brief 007's pre-registered branches are scored after November 24; v2 cites the branches, not an outcome.

What we did prove:
- That §363(b)(1) and §101(41A) are written around the living consumer.
- That at least one estate sale (Spirit) priced workplace data as AI training material, and one ombudsman (23andMe) explicitly raised the dead.
- That the commercial afterlife (animation, scrapers, broker listings) operates without the subject's consent by design.

## Interactive Core · Three Doors

v1's Afterlife Audit checklist, rebuilt as three doors the reader opens in any order.

- **The Court door.** The reader picks a company type (airline, hospital, chatbot, DNA kit) and watches which statute door has a handle. It reuses Brief 006's *Door With No Handle* mechanic, so readers of the brief feel recognition, not repetition.
- **The Store door.** A shelf of real products (animation, talking ancestor, griefbot tier). Each item shows who consented: the subject, the family, or nobody.
- **The Shadow door.** A buyer-profile selector carried over from the Shadow Market's *What's For Sale* idea, narrowed to the dead: what a fraud crew can assemble from one obituary plus one old breach.

After all three doors, the page renders the reader's own audit: twelve rows with ◉ ◯ ◌ marks. Rows still marked ◌ carry a forward arrow labeled **EP 11**. The closing reveal: *"Every ◌ is a governance problem. The next episode is the list."*
