## Four-act internal structure

### Act I · THE ANATOMY (what's inside the building)

What is actually inside a modern hyperscale data center. This is the building the reader has never been allowed inside.

- **Inside the building:** server rows in industrial-density racks, storage arrays, network switches, fiber distribution, UPS battery banks, diesel backup generators sized to run the facility for hours to days, switchgear yards, chillers and CRAH units, increasingly liquid cooling for AI workloads that exceed air-cooling capacity. Fire suppression. Physical security perimeters. Almost no people.
- **Around the building:** substations sized to a small city, cooling towers, water intake, fiber rights-of-way, buffer land, often more buildings on the same campus.
- **What it doesn't have:** anything that produces visible value for the host community in proportion to the externalities. The 40-permanent-jobs reality S1·07 documented is the labor footprint. The 8,200 MW load is the energy footprint. The ratio is the story.

The Anatomy act is genuinely new editorial work — S1·07 didn't take the reader inside the building. EP 07 does.

### Act II · THE OWNERSHIP (who actually owns this)

The concentration of ownership is the political fact most readers haven't internalized.

- **The four hyperscalers** — Amazon Web Services, Microsoft Azure, Google Cloud, Meta — build and operate their own facilities for their own workloads and for cloud customers. The reader has heard the company names but rarely sees them mapped onto specific buildings and specific states.
- **The colocation REIT layer** — Equinix, Digital Realty, CyrusOne, Iron Mountain, and a longer tail — owns colocation facilities leased to enterprises and to hyperscalers needing geographic distribution faster than they can build.
- **The government-contractor layer** — AWS GovCloud, Microsoft Azure Government, Google Public Sector — operates federally-accredited environments inside the same architectural pattern. The customer mix at any given facility frequently includes both commercial and government workloads.
- **The concentration math:** four companies hold the dominant share of US hyperscale capacity. The REIT layer is itself increasingly concentrated as smaller operators get acquired. The political-economic implications scale with the concentration.

S1·07 named the hyperscalers as customers driving Georgia's load. EP 07 maps them as **owners** of the buildings — different editorial work, sharper political-economy claim.

### Act III · THE PATTERN (the Georgia mechanism is the national mechanism)

This act takes the Georgia case the reader trusts and shows it is the structural template.

For each major host region, name:

- The state's version of the **tax-holiday architecture** (HB 1192 has analogues in VA, AZ, OH, OR, TX). Same legal mechanism, slightly different statutory wording. The pattern is reproducible because state legislatures pass similar economic-development incentive packages with similar disclosure protections.
- The state's **load forecast** — Virginia's Dominion IRP, Arizona's APS IRP, Ohio's AEP IRP, Oregon's PGE IRP. The shape of the Georgia 5,400→8,200 MW year-over-year jump appears in each.
- The state's **rate-allocation rules** — and whether they socialize the buildout the way Georgia does.
- The state's **reform agenda** — every major host state has a small bipartisan caucus introducing hyperscale tariff bills, HB-1192-analog sunset bills, per-facility disclosure bills. They die in committee in every state.
- The **shared blocker pattern** — PSC commissioner composition (often elected statewide in low-turnout cycles), donor architecture (hyperscaler-adjacent contributions reach commissioners through trade associations and individual giving), and constituent visibility (the residential customer doesn't see the line item).

**The editorial line:** *the opposition isn't local. The reform isn't local. The blocker isn't local. The architecture is.*

This is the strongest possible setup for EP 12 The Governance Layer — by the time the reader arrives at EP 12, they already know the reform agenda and why it stalls. EP 12's Lever Map hands them the instruments to push it forward.

### Act IV · THE CONVERGENCE (commercial + civic composites share the same buildings)

The act that lands the load-bearing thesis.

The data-layer fragmentation EP 06 The Composite State named as a structural defense — federal records held separately from state records held separately from local records held separately from commercial brokers — was a real protection in the legal frame. **It is not a protection at the physical layer.** AWS GovCloud and AWS commercial frequently share campuses. Azure Government and Azure commercial share campuses. Google Public Sector and Google Cloud share campuses. The physical convergence is documented in federal procurement records and hyperscaler co-location disclosures.

This means the buildings hosting your commercial composite (EP 02 Personhood Inc., EP 03 Trust Market) are often the same buildings hosting your civic composite (EP 06 Composite State). The legal fragmentation says "no single authority can integrate your file." The physical reality says "the integration is one rack away from being possible."

The act closes with the bridge to S1·06 Vogtle Bill's reactor-cycle preview:

> *The AI compute load is the demand that justifies the next round of Vogtle-pattern reactor construction. The financial loop closes: data centers triggered the load forecast; the load forecast justifies new generation; new generation will become the next $35B kitchen-table-to-dividend story; and the data centers will be the largest single customer of that generation while paying the smallest share of its cost.*

The reader leaves Act IV with the conviction that the cost compounding is structural, not episodic, and that EP 08 The Class Engine is where the compounding becomes a sortable population.

## Six-Act Chassis · pinned to the realization arc

### HERO · *Familiarity*
> *You have seen the bill. Now see the building. The building you have never been allowed inside, owned by four companies, in your state too.*

### PRESSURE · *Unease*
"The cloud" as one of the most successfully deceptive metaphors in modern infrastructure language. The cloud is **land** (Loudoun VA, Maricopa AZ, Umatilla OR, Grant WA, Douglas/Coweta/Newton GA). The cloud is **electricity** (~4.4% US consumption in 2023; DOE projects 6.7–12% by 2028). The cloud is **water**. The cloud is **fiber**. The cloud is **generators**. The cloud is **concrete**. The cloud is **heat**. The cloud is **owned**. The cloud is **billed to the household next door**.

The Pressure act explicitly inherits the Georgia framing from S1·07 — *the bill arrives in a different envelope every time* — and escalates it: *the bill arrives in the same envelope in every host state, and your envelope is one of them.*

### PIPELINE · *Recognition* (Acts I + II + III + IV above)

### OUTCOME · *Implication*
Disparate impact across three populations (carries forward unchanged):

- *Host-county residents* bear local externalities in exchange for the local benefits (tax revenue, construction jobs, the 40-permanent-job operational footprint).
- *Broader utility ratepayers* in every host state bear grid-upgrade costs through socialized investments approved by public utility commissions, often without explicit attribution.
- *Communities denied or opposed to data centers* bear the opportunity cost while another community absorbs the load.

Plus the wider implication, named directly: **the same buildings host both composites; the legal fragmentation that protects you collapses physically; the rate-base recovery mechanism is structural across host states; and the next reactor cycle will compound the bill.**

### COMPETING · *Systems Realization (one half)*
Four defenses hosted fairly (carries forward unchanged):

1. **Economic development.** Data centers generate property-tax revenue, construction jobs, technically skilled long-term employment in small but real numbers. For small counties, hyperscaler campuses transform the local fiscal picture.
2. **Infrastructure modernization.** Transmission line upgrades, fiber buildouts, and water-system improvements often subsidized by data-center development benefit other commercial and residential customers downstream.
3. **AI competitiveness.** The US ability to host AI compute is, in the official framing, a national-economic and national-security priority. Restricting data-center growth cedes that compute to other jurisdictions.
4. **Renewables build-out.** Several hyperscalers have catalyzed renewable generation through power-purchase agreements, accelerating wind and solar deployment.

Each defense holds in part. The honest tension remains: the same buildings driving renewable build-out are also driving fossil-plant retention in regions where renewable capacity cannot yet absorb the load.

### DISCIPLINE · *Systems Realization (other half)*
Three refusals (carries forward unchanged):

1. **No conspiracy.** The hyperscaler-government relationship is the product of decades of procurement and tax-incentive policy. No coordination claim.
2. **No dystopia.** Data centers do useful work. The internet most readers use depends on these buildings.
3. **No mind-control framing.** This is an infrastructure episode, not a persuasion episode.

What we did NOT prove (updated to reflect the new four-act structure):
- We did not prove any specific hyperscaler exceeded authority in any specific siting decision.
- We did not prove the political-economy relationships are intentionally opaque rather than accidentally complex.
- We did not prove the 2028 energy share projection is the most likely outcome.
- We did not prove the GovCloud / commercial co-location is being used to integrate fragmented files. We showed the physical capability exists.

What we *did* prove:
- The buildings exist, the ownership concentration is real and quantifiable.
- The Georgia rate-payer subsidy mechanism (S1·07) reproduces in every other major host state with documented variants of the same tax-holiday + load-forecast + rate-allocation pattern.
- The same three-piece reform agenda is being introduced and dying in committee in every host state, for structurally similar reasons.
- Physical convergence of commercial and civic composites at the data-center level is verifiable through federal procurement records and hyperscaler co-location disclosures.
- The next reactor cycle is initiating, with AI compute as the demand anchor, and the same rate-base recovery mechanism documented in S1·06 will likely apply.

## Interactive Core · The National Lever Map

**Replaces the original four-stage "WHAT IT COSTS YOU" calculator** — that work is now S1·07's job for Georgia, and EP 07's interactive can do something stronger.

A single substantial interactive: a map of the cost-and-reform terrain by US state.

The reader selects their state. The map surfaces:

| Slot | What it shows |
|---|---|
| **Data-center buildout** | MW installed + announced + in pipeline by 2030 |
| **Estimated residential contribution** | Annual $/household to grid upgrades supporting data-center load (modeled, labeled) |
| **Hyperscale tariff?** | yes / no / proposed — with bill number if any |
| **Tax-exemption sunset?** | yes / no / proposed — with bill number if any |
| **Per-facility disclosure?** | yes / no / proposed — with bill number if any |
| **PSC composition** | commissioner names, how they're elected, next election date |
| **Donor architecture** | top three contributors to PSC races in the last cycle |
| **Closest peer state** | for each "no" the reader sees, the closest peer state that has it (so reform feels reachable) |

This is the publication's first **comparative-jurisdiction tool**. It converts the Georgia reporting into something every reader in every host state can use. It also reads in conversation with the EP 12 Governance Layer Lever Map — two interactives, two scales, same logic. The pair becomes a recognizable retention device.

Closing reveal: *"You now have a list of levers and the names of the commissioners who control them in your state. The publication does not vote in your election. You do."*
