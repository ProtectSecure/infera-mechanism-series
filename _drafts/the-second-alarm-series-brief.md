**Status:** approved as a mini-series 9 November 2026. Exhibit 1 live; Exhibit 2 briefed; 3 to 5 proposed.

## Thesis

Every company that handles your money or your attention runs an alarm that protects itself. Fraud, cheating, chargebacks, license risk: those alarms are required, funded and fast. The second alarm, the one that would protect *you* from the product, is usually optional. **Where the law makes the company pay for the harm, the second alarm gets built. Where it does not, it gets shelved.** INFERENCE, argued from sourced cases in each exhibit.

Required mirror on every exhibit: a protective alarm is also surveillance; false alarms cost adults their freedom to choose; some companies build second alarms without being forced.

## Exhibit 2 brief · Who Pays for the Scam

**Compression point.** When Britain made banks pay back scam victims, the banks got better at stopping scams. In the US, the customer usually pays.

**The question a reader leaves with.** If I'm tricked into sending money, who pays: me or my bank? And why does the answer change at the border?

### Rooms and mechanics (no sliders; at least one new to the site)

1. **Cold open · The phone call.** A scripted scam call plays as a text conversation the reader answers in real time (Branching case file, new to site). Every branch ends in the same place: the reader "authorizes" the payment. Source the script's beats to regulator descriptions of common scam types, not to invented detail.
2. **The two-alarm panel, as a bank vault.** Two dials on a vault door: unauthorized fraud (spins and locks) and authorized scam (keeps spinning). Reader presses each.
3. **Cross the border.** The same scam, same amount, played in the UK after October 2024 and in the US. A map toggle (Map pin drop, new to site) shows who absorbs the loss in each. Sources: PSR reimbursement rule and results; US Regulation E boundary (WATCH, verify with CFPB primary text).
4. **The receipts.** UK: reimbursement 54% to 65%, losses down about 21%, about £73 million a year (REPORTED, PSR July 2026). US: CFPB's Zelle suit alleging more than $870 million over seven years, filed Dec 2024, dropped 4 Mar 2025 (REPORTED, NPR). Format: Spot the difference between two bank statements.
5. **Build your own lock.** Monzo's gambling block as the customer-controlled second alarm: 700,000 users, £9 million stopped in 2024, 12-month cooling-off chosen by 41% (REPORTED, Finextra). Reader assembles a lock from options (block, cooling-off, note to future self) and sees what each one did in the data.
6. **History room · Who paid before.** Card fraud liability caps and the rise of card fraud detection (verify statute and dates). The analogue is offered as a comparison.
7. **Coach's challenge.** Bank side: moral hazard (reimbursement may invite carelessness or fake claims); the cap on UK payouts; the cost passed to all customers. Consumer side: Consumer Reports on recovery rates.
8. **Recall card at exit** (new to site): three questions, each linking back to its room.
