## Why combine them

Read apart, the two exhibits are a platform story (who ranks the sermon) and a language story (who translates the faith). Read together, they are one question that Christianity's own founding instruction makes urgent: the Great Commission, "Go ye therefore, and teach all nations" (Matthew 28:19) and "Go ye into all the world, and preach the gospel to every creature" (Mark 16:15). Other traditions carry their own versions of the duty to carry a message outward; the piece must attribute each framing to its tradition and not universalize the Christian one.

The sharper frame: **the commission was given to people; the reach is now rented from feeds and models.** Who carries the word to "all nations" when the carrier is a ranking system and a translation model, neither of which answers to any congregation?

**Compression point (draft):** *"The command was to go to every nation. The feed decides which nations hear, and the model decides what they hear."*

## Ethics question to hold at its center

The same tension the new Exhibit 06 raises about attention tactics applies here at scale: if the commission is to reach everyone, may a church use the feed's own levers (engagement bait, micro-targeting, machine translation at volume) to do it?
