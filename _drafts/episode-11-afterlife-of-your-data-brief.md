## Compression Point

> ***"The composite outlives you. The institutions holding it sometimes don't. The legal frame for either case barely exists."***

**Reader-arrival framing:** *Everything the publication has documented assumes you. EP 11 is what happens when you stop. Your composite from EP 02 doesn't die. Your trust score from EP 03 doesn't die. Your civic file from EP 06 doesn't die. The synthetic version of you from EP 09 can still answer the phone. The subscription stack from EP 10 lapses, and the orphan-account version of you persists, queryable. There is no central registry of you anywhere — and there is no central authority that can retire you.*

## Why this episode is the Mortality movement

The publication has spent ten episodes describing how the machine works on living, participating adults. EP 11 is the episode where the machine encounters the limit case: the person stops. Most of the system does not.

This is also the season's most ethically loaded episode. EP 02–10 could be read as systems journalism. EP 11 is the one that becomes a question every reader has to answer for themselves: *what do you want done with you after you stop being able to manage it?*

The episode is structurally a single movement — Mortality — because the next movement (Governance, EP 12) takes the unresolved questions of EP 11 as its starting material. EP 11 names the legal void. EP 12 names the political instruments that could fill it.

## Six-Act Chassis

### HERO · *Familiarity*

> *Someone you knew has died. They are still receiving email. Their photos are still being recommended. Their voice memo to you, on a defunct service, is still in someone's training set.*

Open with a small piece of reported testimony. A named contributor (real, with consent) describes one specific data-afterlife experience involving a deceased family member. The testimony is short. The episode opens on the reader's own recognition.

### PRESSURE · *Unease*

The mechanism: the data lifecycle is decoupled from the personhood lifecycle. Personhood is finite. Data is not. The legal frameworks for governing data after death are, in most jurisdictions, near-zero. The framework for governing identity persistence (synthetic, EP 09), orphan-account exploitation (EP 10), civic-record continuity (EP 06), and the commercial composite (EP 02) after death are even thinner.

A partial map of what survives a death:
- Bank, credit, and lending records survive on the credit-bureau side and on the institution side; closing them is a manual process the estate executes inconsistently.
- Medical records survive in HIPAA-protected systems, but the protections relax in specific ways postmortem and vary by state.
- Civic records (voter registration, DMV, federal-records) survive until a notification cascade clears them, with documented and substantial delays.
- Commercial accounts (email, cloud, social, streaming) survive at the platform's discretion; explicit memorialization policies exist for some, not others.
- Composite scoring (commercial side) survives until the data brokers retire your profile, which is not statutorily required.
- The synthetic of you (EP 09) survives indefinitely if the training data was acquired and the operator chooses to continue.
- The civic composite (EP 06) survives within whatever fragmentation it was held in. No single authority retires it.

### PIPELINE · *Recognition*

Three loops of mortality, in widening scope.

#### Loop 1 · The administrative death

The estate executor or surviving family handles the visible accounts: bank, credit card, mortgage, insurance, utilities. The labor is substantial. The infrastructure to make the labor easier (digital estate services from EP 10) is itself subscription. Households without that infrastructure produce more orphan accounts.

#### Loop 2 · The composite death (mostly does not occur)

The commercial composite does not retire on a death certificate. Data brokers do not, by default, remove deceased profiles. The composite continues to be queried, traded, and incorporated into modeling. In some markets the deceased profile is recategorized but not removed — used for "look-alike" modeling against living comparable households.

#### Loop 3 · The civic composite + the synthetic + the orphan account

The civic record (EP 06) decays asymmetrically across fragmentation — some federal records retire promptly, some state records take years, some county records never do. The synthetic of you (EP 09), if it was built, has no retirement protocol. The orphan accounts (EP 10) become attack surface for fraud, exploitation, and impersonation.

Together: there is no central registry of you, no central authority to retire you, and no statutorily required protocol for any of the layers. The framework for what survives is uneven, ad hoc, and partially commercial.

### OUTCOME · *Implication*

Disparate impact across populations:

1. **Surviving families with means and digital literacy** can manage the closure of the visible accounts. Their loved one's commercial composite, civic composite, and synthetic potential still persist beyond their control.
2. **Surviving families without means or digital literacy** lose continuity earlier. The deceased's orphan accounts become higher-risk. The civic-record decay produces benefit-system friction (Social Security overpayment recovery, Medicaid estate recovery, voter-roll lag).
3. **Communities** absorb the aggregate effect. Voter rolls bloated with deceased registrations are an active political flashpoint (echo to S1·05 The Provisional Ballot). Medical records dispersed from closed institutions (echo to S1·02 The Closed Hospital + Special Report) include records of people no longer alive to consent to the dispersion.

And the wider implication, named directly: **death is a data event the data infrastructure was not designed to recognize.** The architecture assumes continuous participation. The architecture does not have a clean discontinuity for non-participation.

### COMPETING · *Systems Realization (one half)*

Four defenses hosted fairly:

1. **Memorialization serves grief.** Persistent social profiles, photo archives, and even synthetic-companion services serve real human needs for many surviving families.
2. **Commercial composite persistence has audit utility.** Historical records support fraud detection, research, and the lawful interests of creditors, insurers, and tax authorities.
3. **Legal frameworks exist (in places).** Some jurisdictions (notably the EU under GDPR, certain US states under digital-asset acts) have postmortem data frameworks. The legal infrastructure is partial, not absent.
4. **Technical retirement is possible.** Many platforms offer memorialization or deletion. The reader who plans ahead can substantially constrain their data afterlife.

### DISCIPLINE · *Systems Realization (other half)*

Three refusals:

1. **No morbid framing.** Death is the case the episode examines; the episode is not about death. It is about infrastructure that is not designed for the case of non-participation.
2. **No universalization.** The afterlife experience varies enormously by jurisdiction, by family resources, by the deceased's prior planning. The episode resists rendering it as one story.
3. **No conspiracy.** The data brokers, platforms, and agencies that retain deceased records mostly do so because deletion is operationally hard, not because they intend exploitation.

What we did NOT prove:
- We did not prove the absence of deletion mechanisms causes specific harms in any specific case beyond illustrative ones.
- We did not prove the commercial composite persistence is materially driving look-alike modeling at scale (we documented one industry source claiming the practice; we treated it as modeled).
- We did not prove the postmortem-synthetic risk is widespread; it is documented in a small number of cases.

What we *did* prove:
- That data does not retire on death.
- That the legal framework for postmortem data governance is fragmentary and uneven.
- That the orphan-account problem (EP 10) intensifies postmortem.
- That the civic-record decay is asymmetric and produces documented friction for surviving families and for downstream civic processes.
- That a small but documented postmortem-synthetic category exists and is unregulated.

## Interactive Core · The Afterlife Audit

A reader-anchored mechanic.

The reader is invited to perform a thought-experiment audit on their own data afterlife. The interactive walks through a checklist:

- Where would your bank, credit, and mortgage records go?
- Who has access to your password manager?
- Have you named a digital executor?
- What happens to your medical records?
- What happens to your voter registration?
- What happens to your social and email accounts?
- What happens to your cloud storage?
- What happens to your voice samples?
- What happens to your composite (commercial)?
- What happens to your civic file (EP 06)?
- What happens to your synthetic potential (EP 09)?

For each checklist item, the reader sees a one-line summary of the current default behavior in the US (with state-specific variation where relevant), plus the action they would need to take to change it.

Closing reveal: *the categories with "no clear default" — most of them — are the ones the next episode (EP 12) takes up as governance problems.*
