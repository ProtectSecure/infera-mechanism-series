Pressure Series update. Published Nov. 1, 2026; standards retrofit Nov. 11, 2026 (batch 1, pilot form: follow-up). Priority score 17 (risk 3, evidence gap 3 as scored, reach 2).

## The eight fields

1. **Reader question.** Did the government's steps in the 2020 Fulton matter meet the Justice Department's own written rule against timing actions to affect an election?
2. **Contribution.** Existing coverage (AJC, AP, Democracy Docket, ABC) establishes the events. Infera puts them on one timeline against Justice Manual 9-85.500 and the 2024 election-year memo, and separates the written rule from the unwritten "60-day" custom.
3. **Human situation.** Fulton's 2020 election workers, whose identities the government sought. Reporting basis: records at a distance, with no interviews. Reader entry point: a county's ballots taken by federal agents, nine months before a midterm.
4. **Form.** Follow-up (dominant), with a supporting comparison (two accounts of one chain of custody).
5. **Evidence record (consequential claims).**

6. **Competing explanation.** Ordinary law enforcement acting on real 2020 process failures, versus a politically originated probe. Kept as two columns ("What each side can point to") plus a Brady box. What would discriminate: the redacted parts of the affidavit, any charging decision, and the 11th Circuit's ruling on the statute of limitations.
7. **Agency.**
   - People in the story: election workers can contest subpoenas, and did.
   - Institutions: the 11th Circuit, the Public Integrity Section, and Fulton's board.
   - Reader: can track the docket, and can judge future steps against the written rule.
   - Lever Map tile: none added in this retrofit. *Status since publication* is now on the page.
8. **Ending and boundary.** The page ends on the argued position and a dated watch list. Out of scope: the merits of 2020 fraud claims beyond what the courts and reviews found. Stop rule: the next court ruling or charging decision.
