# Episode 04 · The Influence War — Visual Style Guide & Structural Blueprint

**For:** RENDER phase of EP 04
**Status:** Style direction locked — awaiting visual-prototype approval
**Date:** 27 May 2026

---

## 1 · The Aesthetic Thesis

Episode 04 looks different from every other piece in the publication. The publication's house style has been *restraint at the chassis, electric at the accent.* EP 04 keeps the chassis (nav, footer, page structure, Case File) disciplined but pushes the accent to its outer edge: **type-as-image, cut-and-paste chaos, fragmented composition, mixed-media collage**.

This is not a stylistic indulgence. It is an editorial argument. The persuasion industry's defining characteristic is *simultaneous overlapping messaging* — a hundred ad variants reaching the same person from different angles in the same hour. The page's typographic chaos *is* the mechanism the episode describes. The reader experiences the persuasion stack before they read about it.

The discipline: chaos serves meaning, never spectacle. Every torn fragment, every mismatched typeface, every redaction bar earns its presence by advancing the argument. A composition that looks bold but says nothing has failed.

> *Language as design material, not just communication.*

---

## 2 · Why Type-Collage IS the Argument

Three structural reasons this aesthetic is the right one for this episode specifically:

**Reason 1 — The mechanism is fragmentation.** The persuasion industry breaks the reader into seven personas and serves seven different messages simultaneously. A page composed of seven typographic voices speaking over each other is the same mechanism rendered visually.

**Reason 2 — The mechanism is appropriation.** The persuasion industry recycles every cultural language — protest posters, news headlines, intimate handwritten notes, government documents, advertising slogans — and re-deploys them at the reader. A page composed of mixed eras and mixed media exposes the recycling.

**Reason 3 — The mechanism is noise.** The persuasion industry's defining experience is overwhelm — too much, too fast, too many directions. A page that risks visual overload (controlled, intentional, in service of meaning) earns the reader's recognition: *yes, this is what it feels like.*

The page argues, in design, what the prose argues in claims. The two reinforce each other. This is the publication's first piece where the visual carries equal editorial weight with the text.

## 3 · Structural Sequence — Section by Section

EP 04 runs ten sections. Each gets a distinct typographic treatment that serves its emotional purpose and advances the realization arc.

### S01 · HERO — *Familiarity*

**Composition:** Type-collage hero. The declarative line *"An ad found you. Then another. Then a hundred. Each one was for a different YOU."* is broken across the page in eight typographic voices simultaneously. The word *YOU* appears seven times, in seven typefaces, in seven sizes — a quiet preview of the Persona Cascade that arrives later. Torn paper edges. Highlighter strike-through on one phrase. A handwritten "again?" annotation in marker. A redaction bar over one fragment. A timestamp ("DECLASSIFIED · S2E04") top-right like a leaked document.

**Reader experience:** Reader lands in noise. The page is loud before it has said anything. Reader feels persuasion overload as the page's first sensation, not its later argument.

**Motion:** Subtle. One fragment continuously breathes (slow opacity pulse). The seven *YOU*s fade in on staggered timing so the persona-multiplicity is felt rather than counted. Hover on any *YOU* lifts it slightly and increases its opacity, hinting that each is a distinct persona.

### S02 · PRESSURE — *Unease*

**Composition:** A "wall of voices." The pressure paragraph (*"For most of its modern life, the persuasion industry asked one question: who are you..."*) is laid out in fragmented columns interleaved with vintage advertising fragments — torn from real historical ads (1920s newsprint, 1960s political broadsides, 1990s magazine spreads, 2010s banner ads, 2020s mobile creatives). Each era has its own typographic treatment. The fragments overlap, creating density. The reader's eye has to *work* to follow the argument — which is the point.

**Reader experience:** Reader feels the historical depth of the persuasion industry and the contemporary acceleration simultaneously.

**Motion:** Scroll-tied parallax. Background fragments scroll slightly slower than foreground text. The "wall" appears to have layers.

### S03 · PIPELINE — *Recognition*

**Composition:** Six steps, six distinct typographic experiments. Each step gets its own visual language so the cohesion is intentional inconsistency. Step 01 (Audience Inference): coded grid of broker logos rendered as monogram type. Step 02 (Persona × Mood Mapping): seven persona "ID cards" in mixed era treatments. Step 03 (Creative Variant Spawn): twenty-four ad variants tile in a chaotic grid, each one styled differently. Step 04 (Channel Placement): a "media plan" rendered as a redacted memo. Step 05 (Affective Telemetry): waveforms and emotion words in JetBrains Mono with biometric readout aesthetic. Step 06 (Re-Segmentation): a torn-paper flowchart returning to Step 01 as a loop.

**Reader experience:** Reader sees each step as a distinct *instrument* in the persuasion stack. The visual inconsistency reinforces that the mechanism is itself an assemblage of inconsistent parts that work together because the integration happens computationally, not aesthetically.

**Motion:** Each step has its own internal motion language. Step 03 (variants) shuffles on scroll — variants re-arrange themselves as the reader moves down. Step 05 (telemetry) shows live-looking waveforms tied to scroll velocity. Step 06 (loop) shows the loop visually completing as the reader leaves the section.

### S04 · BUILD-YOUR-VOTER — *Interactive Core · Recognition deepens*

**Composition:** A clearer, more disciplined layout because this is where the reader does work. The chassis here pulls *back* — calmer type, more breathing room — so the reader can focus on the sliders and the cascade. The Persona Cascade visual occupies the right half; the slider panel occupies the left. As the reader moves sliders, the persona halos in the cascade re-color in real time, and the winning ad variant shifts.

**Reader experience:** Reader makes the system do its work in front of them. The kinetic chaos of S01-S03 was the prelude. This is the operating panel.

**Motion:** Direct cause-and-effect. Slider movement triggers immediate visual response in the cascade. No animation lag; the chassis feels live.

**Critical constraint:** the sliders never carry political labels. No "liberal" / "conservative" anchors. Every axis is a human condition. The episode's whole argument depends on this discipline being absolute.

### S05 · OUTCOME — *Implication*

**Composition:** Three-population disparate-impact display, plus the cross-domain reveal. Sports broadcast meets political ad meets rap battle — montage of attention markets in shared typographic chaos. A wall of "this also runs on the same engine" — Sunday-NFL fragments next to Drake-vs-Kendrick fragments next to Senate-debate fragments, all rendered in the same compositional language to make the structural identity unmissable.

**Reader experience:** Reader recognizes their own attention life inside the mechanism. *I felt this watching the game. I felt this scrolling the beef. I felt this reading the news.* All the same engine.

**Motion:** Slow horizontal drift across the wall of cross-domain fragments. The reader can pause-hover to read any fragment.

### S06 · COMPETING — *Systems Realization · one half*

**Composition:** Hard pivot to restraint. After 5 sections of chaos, the counter-read sections breathe. Single-column setting. Serif body type. Generous margins. The hosted counter-reads (Hersh, Barrett, industry defenders) get the visual *seriousness* their arguments deserve. Each defender gets a quiet portrait fragment and a properly typeset quotation.

**Reader experience:** Reader feels the shift from chaos to discipline as part of the argument. The publication is showing that counter-reads deserve cool quietness, not the same noise as the mechanism being critiqued.

**Motion:** Almost none. Subtle scroll-tied dimming of margins so the reader's focus stays centered. The page literally calms down.

### S07 · DISCIPLINE — *Systems Realization · other half*

**Composition:** Even more restrained. The "what we did NOT prove" section uses only two typefaces (Instrument Serif and JetBrains Mono). No color accents except a single quiet lime checkmark per claim. The fourth founding refusal lands here visually: a clean, calm declaration that the system is *not* mind-control, that the reader's agency remains the load-bearing variable. The restraint of this section is its own kind of authority.

**Reader experience:** Reader leaves the discipline section trusting the prior sections more, not less, because the publication demonstrated willingness to be quiet when the argument required it.

**Motion:** None. The section is the page's stillness.

### S08 · CLOSER — *Singular monumentality*

**Composition:** Return to type-as-image, but monumental now, not chaotic. The closer line — *"The most effective persuasion systems do not change your opinion. They change what feels true."* — fills the full viewport. The phrase "what feels true" italicized in Instrument Serif. The rest in heavy display (Archivo Black or new editorial face). Single color accent — a slow-pulse highlight under "feels true" in highlighter yellow that arrives only after the reader has read the line.

**Reader experience:** Reader leaves the body of the episode with a single sentence as their parting souvenir.

**Motion:** Single-frame moment. The highlighter under "feels true" draws itself across the line over 1.5 seconds when the section enters the viewport. Then stillness.

### S09 · CASE FILE INTEGRATION — *Receipt + Trigger Mirror*

**Composition:** The Session Receipt and Trigger Mirror render as "leaked document" aesthetics — typewriter face, redacted bars, official-document header treatment, hand-stamped "EYES ONLY" graphic. The reader's own data appears as if pulled from a manila folder.

**Reader experience:** Reader's accumulated session data is presented as evidence found in an archive. The aesthetic primes them to take seriously what they're about to see about themselves.

**Motion:** Document "assembles" — pages slide in, stamps land, redactions appear, the reader's personalized data populates the fields. Then settles.

### S10 · BRIDGE — *To the next piece*

**Composition:** Restrained again. Three echo dots forward (to Replayable City field report, to Composite State EP 05, to the Convergence essay). Each rendered as a small annotated fragment, like marginalia in a notebook. The reader can hover for a one-sentence preview.

**Reader experience:** Reader leaves with vocabulary and a map, knowing what comes next.

## 4 · Engagement Experience Vehicle

How the reader physically moves through the piece.

**Scroll posture.** Vertical scroll, single-page architecture. Total scroll length: approximately 14-18 viewport heights. The reader can scroll continuously or stop and explore.

**Section anchoring.** Each major section has a small anchor link in the chapter rail (right edge). The reader can jump non-linearly. The rail itself uses the cut-paste aesthetic — section labels in mixed type voices.

**Interactive surfaces (inventory).**

1. **Hero · YOU multiplicity** — hover on any *YOU* fragment lifts it; reveals a one-word persona tag ("the anxious one," "the patriot," etc.) on hover.
2. **Pressure · era fragments** — clickable to expand into full reproduction of the historical ad. Educational easter-egg layer.
3. **Pipeline Step 03 · variant grid** — variants reshuffle on scroll. Clicking any variant pins it and reveals which persona-mood pairing it was generated for.
4. **Pipeline Step 05 · telemetry** — waveforms respond to scroll velocity. Reader can move slowly to see calm waveform or scroll fast to see agitated waveform.
5. **Build-Your-Voter · slider panel** — ten sliders driving live persona-cascade morph. Most important interactive in the episode.
6. **Outcome · cross-domain wall** — pause-hover on any fragment expands it to readable size with a brief annotation of which attention market it's from.
7. **Affective probes** — three to five embedded prompts at section breaks. Two-tap (icon choice). Logged to localStorage.
8. **Case File · Trigger Mirror opt-in** — single button reveals personalized output; single button burns it.
9. **Session Receipt** — auto-populated at episode end with the reader's actual signals collected during reading.
10. **Easter egg — torn paper fragments throughout** can be peeled back with click to reveal hidden text. Some are meta-commentary ("you read this section in 47 seconds — the median is 2m 14s"), some are extra evidence, some are quotes from designers/theorists whose work informs the aesthetic.

**Inactivity behavior.** If the reader stops engaging for 20 seconds, the page "settles" — animations slow to stillness. When the reader resumes scroll, motion resumes. This is the publication doing in design what the editorial argument names: *the system reads your engagement, then adjusts*. Except the publication does it transparently and gently.

**Reduced-motion mode.** All cause-and-effect motion stops. Layouts hold. Sliders still work. Interactivity still works. The chaos becomes legible static composition rather than kinetic display.

## 5 · Typography Stack — The Nine Voices

Each typeface serves a distinct narrative function. The reader doesn't see "nine typefaces" — they hear nine voices speaking from nine positions in the persuasion conversation.

| # | Face | Role | Tone |
|---|------|------|------|
| 1 | **Archivo Black** | Monumental display, hero declarations | Authority, weight, public address |
| 2 | **Instrument Serif** (italic) | Quiet emphasis, intellectual interjections | Reflection, doubt, complication |
| 3 | **JetBrains Mono** | Data, telemetry, technical voice, timestamps | Machine-readable, observational, log-like |
| 4 | **PP Editorial New** (or Reckless / Migra) | Heavy literary serif for hero declarations | Literary gravitas, editorial weight |
| 5 | **Inter** (or GT America) | Clean grotesque for body alternatives | Contemporary neutrality, calm authority |
| 6 | **Special Elite** (or Courier Prime distressed) | Typewriter for "first-person leaked" voice | Confessional, archived, declassified |
| 7 | **Permanent Marker** (or hand-letter face) | Scrawled annotations, marginalia | Human intervention, dissent, urgency |
| 8 | **Old Standard TT** (newspaper face) | Historical fragments, archival quotations | Periodicity, depth, history-as-evidence |
| 9 | **Glitched/distorted treatment** (CSS-applied) | AI-generated content callouts, synthetic-content warnings | Synthetic, machine-output, uncanny |

All faces loaded via Google Fonts where possible. Performance budget: ≤350KB total font weight across the page, with display-only weights wherever possible (no full family loads). Subsetting where Google Fonts permits.

**Compositional rules.**
- No section uses fewer than two typefaces.
- No section uses more than five typefaces in a single composition.
- Typeface choice is *functional* — it signals voice, era, or position. Never decorative.
- COMPETING (S06) and DISCIPLINE (S07) sections use only a restricted subset (Instrument Serif + JetBrains Mono + one body face). Their restraint is part of the argument.

## 7 · Mixed-Media Illustration Language

Beyond typography, the page incorporates five illustration registers, each used purposefully.

**Halftone screen prints.** For portraits of named counter-read defenders (Hersh, Barrett, industry voices). Halftone dot pattern in single ink color. Evokes editorial respect — the seriousness of mid-century print journalism portraiture.

**Risograph aesthetic.** For mid-section graphic accents. Limited-color, slight mis-registration, paper-grain texture. Used sparingly, primarily in S03 (Pipeline) for the step illustrations.

**Newspaper print decay.** For historical advertising fragments in S02. Distressed, slightly yellow, smudge texture. Real era reproduction (not pastiche).

**Glitch / synthetic aesthetic.** For AI-generated content callouts and deepfake references. Pixel-sort artifacts, RGB-split, scanline distortion. Used to mark *synthetic* content distinctly from *organic* fragments.

**Manual collage marks.** Tape, staples, pen circles, marker underlines, paperclips. Used to signal *editorial assembly* — the reader sees the publication's hand in the composition. These marks are aesthetic *and* honest: the page was assembled with intention; the marks make the intention visible.

**Photography.** Sparingly. When used, treated like found documents — corner-stamped, edge-torn, slightly off-axis. Never illustrative-stock. Always archival-feel.

## 8 · Motion & Interaction Language — The Cause-and-Effect Rules

The publication's motion rule is *static frame, kinetic interior*. EP 04 extends this with explicit cause-and-effect — every motion has a *cause* the reader can identify.

**Eight cause-and-effect rules.**

1. **Scroll causes drift.** Background fragments scroll slightly slower than foreground (parallax). Effect: the reader feels depth.
2. **Hover causes lift.** Hoverable elements rise slightly and gain opacity. Effect: reader knows what they can engage.
3. **Click causes reveal.** Torn-paper elements unfold to show hidden content. Effect: reader feels they're discovering, not being shown.
4. **Slider causes morph.** Sliders in S04 drive immediate visual response in the cascade. Effect: reader operates the system.
5. **Scroll velocity causes agitation.** Telemetry waveforms in S05 visibly react to scroll speed. Effect: the reader can *see* the system reading them.
6. **Section entry causes settle.** Each section's elements arrive in staggered sequence — typography, then accents, then motion. Effect: the section composes itself for the reader.
7. **Inactivity causes calm.** 20+ seconds of no interaction → motion slows to stillness. Effect: the page mirrors the reader's state.
8. **Reduced-motion preference causes layout.** When the system reduced-motion flag is set, all motion ceases; compositions hold statically. Effect: the chaos becomes legible composition rather than display.

**Forbidden motion patterns.**
- No autoplay video.
- No carousel auto-advance (carousels must be reader-driven).
- No parallax that breaks legibility.
- No pan/zoom on hero compositions (the publication's standing rule).
- No animation that competes with reading speed.

## 9 · Vibe Register — Hipster · Gen Z Pro · Elder Millennial

The aesthetic intentionally spans three reader cohorts who experience the persuasion mechanism differently and bring different cultural references to it.

**Hipster.** Recognizes the design lineage — Carson, Brody, Sagmeister, zine culture, Riot Grrrl, Constructivist political posters, early Dazed & Confused. Wants the references to be earned, not surfaced. Appreciates internal jokes (the manual collage marks, the easter-egg torn fragments).

**Gen Z professional.** Reads the page on phone first, has tolerance for visual complexity that older readers find overwhelming, recognizes meme-language fragments and platform-aesthetic references (Instagram-grid moments, TikTok-caption typography, redaction-as-content). Expects high information density.

**Elder millennial.** Recognizes the historical advertising fragments (the 90s magazine ads, the 2000s banner aesthetic, the early-social-media UI fragments). Has lived through several persuasion-mechanism generations and feels the cumulative weight of the argument personally. Appreciates the discipline of the COMPETING/DISCIPLINE sections.

The publication does not have to choose. The aesthetic is *deep enough* that each cohort finds their own entry. The hipster reads it as design heritage. The Gen Z reader reads it as platform-native chaos. The elder millennial reads it as autobiography.

**What the aesthetic refuses:**
- No nostalgia for nostalgia's sake. Every era reference advances the argument.
- No ironic distance from the editorial claims. The chaos is sincere.
- No "this is for the algorithm" composition. The page is for the reader, not for screenshots.

## 10 · Performance & Responsive

Aggressive performance budget despite the visual ambition.

**Page weight target:** ≤450KB above-the-fold (fonts + critical CSS + hero SVGs). Total page weight target: ≤1.8MB.

**Strategy:**
- **SVG-first.** All type compositions render as SVG, not images. Sharp at any zoom, small file size, accessible.
- **System font fallbacks.** Each loaded face has a system fallback specified. Page renders before fonts arrive.
- **Critical CSS inlined.** First viewport CSS in `<head>`; rest deferred.
- **Lazy-load below-fold.** Pipeline (S03) onwards loads as reader scrolls toward it.
- **No images for type.** Every typographic composition is web type, not raster.
- **Subset fonts where possible.** If a face is used for only 12 characters, subset to those 12.

**Responsive breakpoints.**
- **Mobile (≤640px):** Compositions simplify — fewer overlapping fragments, single-column flow, larger touch targets, slider panel becomes vertical-stack.
- **Tablet (641-1024px):** Two-column compositions; cascade remains horizontal.
- **Desktop (≥1025px):** Full multi-column type-collage; persona cascade renders at intended scale.

**Reduced-motion:** All motion ceases. Compositions hold statically. Sliders still work; hover still works (just without lift).

**Zoom and pan tolerance:** Up to 200% browser zoom; SVG compositions remain sharp. Pinch-zoom on touch devices works as expected for accessibility.

**Accessibility:**
- Every SVG composition has an `<aria-label>` describing its content.
- Color is never the only carrier of meaning (provenance still uses lime/amber/pink, but also iconographic markers).
- All interactive elements keyboard-accessible (tab order, focus states, enter-to-activate).
- Affective probes have keyboard alternates.
- Trigger Mirror opt-in is opt-in, never auto-revealed.

## 12 · Build Approach

Technical stack and production approach for RENDER phase.

**Stack.**
- HTML5 + CSS3 + vanilla JS (no framework dependencies).
- SVG for all type compositions, interactive cascade, telemetry waveforms.
- Google Fonts (subset, async-loaded) for the type stack.
- LocalStorage for affective probes, Session Receipt, Case File integration.
- IntersectionObserver for section-entry animations.
- ResizeObserver for responsive composition reflow.

**Component architecture.**
- Each section is a discrete `<section>` with its own CSS scope (using CSS custom properties for theming, no global namespace pollution).
- Reusable components: torn-paper fragment, highlighter strike, redaction bar, era marker, affective probe, persona pill, slider, waveform.
- Components are loaded once and composed per-section — no per-section duplication.

**Production sequence.**
1. **Phase 1 — Hero prototype.** Build S01 (HERO) standalone. Validate aesthetic, performance, motion language. Human review.
2. **Phase 2 — Composition library.** Build the reusable components (torn paper, highlighter, redaction, etc.) as a library file. Validate cross-section consistency.
3. **Phase 3 — Linear build.** Build S02 through S10 in order, validating performance and accessibility at each section.
4. **Phase 4 — Interactivity wiring.** Wire Build-Your-Voter, Trigger Mirror, affective probes, Session Receipt to localStorage. Validate full reader-journey.
5. **Phase 5 — Audit pass.** Structural Integrity Auditor (skill 01), Idempotency Sentinel (skill 02), Episode Production Linter (skill 05), Mobile/A11y Auditor (skill 07).
6. **Phase 6 — Founding 500 preview.** Soft-launch to founding cohort for feedback before public release.
7. **Phase 7 — Public ship.**

**Performance gates.**
- Lighthouse Performance ≥ 90 on mobile
- Lighthouse Accessibility ≥ 95
- First Contentful Paint ≤ 1.5s
- Total Blocking Time ≤ 200ms
- Cumulative Layout Shift ≤ 0.05

These are aggressive targets given the visual ambition. They are achievable because the visual work is SVG and web-type rather than image-heavy.

## 13 · The Discipline Underneath the Chaos

The piece will look chaotic. Its execution must be the opposite of chaotic. Every composition is deliberate. Every overlap has a meaning. Every typeface choice is a voice. Every color accent is a marker. Every motion has a cause.

The publication's editorial voice survives the aesthetic shift because the discipline is preserved: the four founding refusals hold (no partisanship, no conspiracy, no dystopia, no mind-control framing), the five-stage realization arc still walks, the validation discipline still binds every claim, the law of exposition still requires three of six vectors satisfied.

The chaos is *content*, not posture. That distinction is what keeps EP 04 honest.
