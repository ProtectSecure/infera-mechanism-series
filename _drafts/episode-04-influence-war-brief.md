## Six-Act Chassis — pinned to the realization arc

### HERO · *Familiarity*

> *An ad found you.* Then another. Then a hundred. Each one was for a different *you*.

### PRESSURE · *Unease*

For most of the modern persuasion industry's life, the operative question was *who you are*. Voter file. Donation history. District. Demographics. Targeting was a sorting problem.

What changed between 2016 and 2024 wasn't the targeting. It was a second question the system learned to ask: *how do you feel right now*. Sentiment models reading every text you type. Language-model affective classifiers tagging your replies. Dwell-time as an arousal proxy. Scroll velocity as agitation. Voice prosody as openness. The wearables turned the inference from a guess into a measurement — the mood the system used to read from your typing it can now read from your pulse.

Until the system could see your mood, persuasion was a guess. Once it could see your mood, it became arithmetic. And arithmetic compounds.

The persuasion industry is no longer an event. It is the weather.

### PIPELINE · *Recognition*

Six lawful steps, each gets an interactive surface whose motion carries the argument.

```
01  AUDIENCE INFERENCE          who you are (broker composite)
02  PERSONA × MOOD MAPPING      which you, in what mood, right now
03  CREATIVE VARIANT SPAWN      24 variants per pairing per week
04  CHANNEL PLACEMENT           right surface, right second
05  AFFECTIVE TELEMETRY         emotional fingerprint, not clicks
06  RE-SEGMENTATION             mood update → tomorrow's targeting
```

Each step is a feedback loop, not a funnel. The output of Step 06 becomes the input of Step 01 the next day.

### OUTCOME · *Implication*

The disparate impact is real and quantified where possible.

- *Politically uncommitted voters* receive 3-5× the variant exposure of committed voters. The system spends its budget where it can move the most weight.
- *High-emotion-response demographics* (identifiable by prior engagement patterns) receive disproportionately outrage-tuned content. Algorithmic vulnerability bias.
- *Older voters* receive fewer variants and slower A/B cycles. They are persuaded by an older, blunter version of the same machine. A different distortion, not its absence.

And the wider frame, which the episode names directly: this engine isn't a political artifact. It runs sports broadcasting, music feuds, celebrity content, and partisan politics on the same loop. The reason political content feels different now isn't because politics has changed. It's because the optimization function found politics. Politics used to be mostly low-arousal — committee hearings, policy papers — and nobody shared it. The system learned which 1% of political content was rage-producing and built distribution machines that ensured you only ever saw that 1%. The other 99% became invisible.

If you've felt yourself unable to stop scrolling a sports rivalry, a rap beef, a celebrity feud, a controversy that contributes nothing to your life — you've felt this mechanism. The campaign is using the same engine.

### COMPETING · *Systems Realization · one half*

The persuasion industry's strongest defenders argue three things.

First, Eitan Hersh's research at Tufts shows that the measured effect size of microtargeting on actual vote choice is small — often smaller than vendors claim. Second, ad-tech vendors argue personalization is more efficient (less wasted spend) and more democratizing (small campaigns reach niche voters). Third, both major U.S. parties now run nearly identical infrastructures, which they frame as competitive necessity rather than escalation.

The counter-read deserves real airtime in the episode. The mechanism is real and documentable; the question of *how much it changes any single election* is contested and should remain so.

### DISCIPLINE · *Systems Realization · other half*

The episode must refuse three convenient simplifications.

First, the persuasion industry is not *trying* to polarize the country. It is trying to maximize engagement, and engagement happens to be maximized by rage. The polarization is a structural side-effect. The optimization function would happily optimize for joy or curiosity if those drove the same metrics. They don't.

Second, this means there is no villain to remove. The villain is the equilibrium. That is more disturbing than the alternative, because mechanism is harder to legislate than malice.

Third — and this is where the **fourth founding refusal** binds — the system does *not* mind-control anyone. It optimizes the emotional conditions under which certain beliefs, reactions, and identities are more likely to emerge. The reader's agency remains the load-bearing variable. The episode must never imply otherwise.

What we did NOT prove:
- We did not prove any specific election was decided by microtargeting.
- We did not prove any individual voter changed their mind because of a single ad.
- We did not prove personalized persuasion is more powerful than mass persuasion.
- We did not prove the biometric affective inference vendors sell is as accurate as they claim. Lisa Feldman Barrett's body of work shows facial-expression emotion inference is much weaker than commercial systems claim — and the system *acts* on the inference whether it is accurate or not, which is its own harm.

What we *did* prove: the infrastructure for industrial-scale identity targeting exists, is in continuous active use by both U.S. parties and most major issue campaigns, and its inputs are the broker composites and trust scores documented in Episodes 02 and 03.

### CLOSER

> *"The most effective persuasion systems do not change your opinion. They change what feels true."*

## Visual Chassis — Persona Cascade + Build-Your-Voter

**Opening visual (atmosphere):** Persona Cascade chassis as previously mocked. Single voter at center. Seven personas in a ring. Seven ad variants in outer ring. Each persona has an **affective halo** pulsing with inferred mood color (calm-blue, agitation-amber, anxiety-pink, fatigue-grey). One variant (the angry one) glows because the engagement-telemetry loop has flagged it as winning. Re-targeting loop pulses around the perimeter — bright pulse traveling from winning persona back through center.

**New: Wearable sensor at top of diagram.** Small ring icon sends a continuous HRV waveform down into the Persona × Mood Mapping ring. **Clean-room glyph** appears between broker layer and persona layer, indicating that identifiers are matched without raw data exchange. Both elements pulse in time with the existing loop.

**Interactive core (participation):** Build-Your-Voter slider panel sits below the chassis. Ten sliders:

```
fear            loneliness        education pressure
income stress   media trust       religiosity
resentment      civic fatigue     isolation
identity intensity
```

As the reader moves sliders, the chassis above morphs in real time:
- Persona halos change color
- Different variants light up as winning
- The re-targeting loop accelerates or slows
- The dominant emotional architecture shifts

The reader sees, with their own hands, that the system is not choosing between left and right framings. It is choosing between emotional architectures. That distinction lands harder when the reader makes the system do it themselves.

**Critical editorial rule for the interactive:** the sliders never carry political labels. No "liberal" or "conservative" anchor at any end. Every axis is a *human condition*, not a partisan stance. The episode's whole argument depends on this discipline being absolute.

## Case File Integration — what EP 04 contributes to the reader's accumulating persona

EP 04 is the episode where the **affective layer** of the reader's Case File begins populating. New components shipped with this episode:

1. **Affective probes** — 3-5 micro-interactions inside the episode (two-tap "how does this make you feel" prompts, A/B headline picks, "did you expect this" yes/no).
2. **Session Receipt** — visible log at episode end showing every signal collected during the session.
3. **Temperament Meter v1** — radial signature in Case File, 3 axes initially (trigger latency, outrage susceptibility, curiosity weight), sharpens to 6 over later episodes.
4. **Valuation Ticker** — four-tier CPM ladder showing the reader's persona value as data layers compound.
5. **Trigger Mirror (opt-in)** — end-of-episode optional reveal: the reader sees the three ad variants an advertiser would test on them first, based on their actual reading session.

All client-side. All in localStorage. Burn-the-file button visible from page one. Nothing leaves the device. The publication never has the data.
