# Remix exhibits · proposals, ranked · 10 November 2026

**Purpose.** New exhibits made by putting existing ones together, so a reader sees a connection that neither piece could show alone, and can experiment until they find one we missed. Each proposal names what it reuses (data and mechanics already on the site), what is new, what the reader leaves with, and what it does for the publication. Ranked by benefit against cost. Nothing here is built; pick and it goes through the usual brief, preview and sign-off.

All claims in a remix inherit the tag they carry in their home exhibit. A remix may add INFERENCE; it may not upgrade a tag.

## 1 · The Loom (start here)

**What it is.** One page where the reader picks any two things on the site, two exhibits, two threads, or an exhibit and a state, and the page weaves them: the mechanism they share, the place they diverge, the law that touches both, and one open question. It ends with "You found a thread. Keep it?" which saves the pairing on their device and offers to send it to us.

**Built from.** The four threads (Money, Records, Clocks, Voices) and their item lists, which today are copied by hand into each page; the 20-state map from The Permanent Record; the ledger and through lines in the curiosity engine.

**What is new.** A single `connections.json` (every piece, its threads, states, laws, entities, mechanisms, and its best three tagged facts). Every thread bar, end card and state link on the site then reads from that one file, which also ends the hand-copying that let thread lists drift out of date this week.

**Mechanics.** Pick two cards and drop them on the loom (no sliders); a "surprise me" shuffle; a state lens ("Georgia: nine stories touched your state"); a visible count of pairings no reader has tried yet.

**Reader leaves with.** A connection they made themselves, which is the kind people remember and share.

**For Melophon.** Retention (a reason to come back to old pieces), a natural sign-up moment (tagged by state and thread, so emails can be relevant), and reader-found convergences that feed the curiosity engine as new cues.

**Honesty rule.** The Loom only shows a link where both pieces carry a sourced fact for it. Where they do not, it says "no documented connection yet" and offers the pairing as a question.

## 2 · One Life, Every Ledger

**What it is.** The Permanent Record follows one person through a church. This follows one person through everything: the parish, the school, the bank, the betting app, the chatbot, the hospital, the utility. At each age the reader sees who opened a file, which alarm protects the institution, whether a second alarm protects the person, and which law applies. At the end, the life's data goes to the estate sale.

**Built from.** The grow-up stepper and register printer; the two-alarm panel; EP 09's two ledgers; Who Pays's authorized/unauthorized fork; FN 20's bankruptcy route; Check-In.

**What is new.** A single "coverage strip" across the whole life showing the covered years and the gaps, and a reader choice at each stage (take the app, decline the app) that changes the file.

**Why second.** It is the natural bridge into EP 10, The Afterlife of Your Data, and the strongest single statement of what the site is about. It is also the most work.

## 3 · The Liability Switchboard

**What it is.** A wall of switches, one per domain: scam losses, problem gambling, a data breach, a data center's power bill, a nuclear plant's overrun. Each switch has two positions: the company pays, or the customer pays. Flip one and the board shows what the record says happened where that rule was tried.

**Built from.** Second Alarm 01 and 02; FN 19's capital stack (who loses first); Same Building, Two Bills (who pays for the grid); The Vogtle Bill (who paid for the overrun).

**Why it matters.** The series argues that the alarm follows the bill. This lets a reader test that across five domains and find where it fails. Each failure is a curiosity cue, and we should invite it on the page.

## 4 · Clocks Collide

**What it is.** One wall, every clock the site has documented, on a single scale: two business days to report a stolen card, 13 months to claim a UK refund, about 15 years for a company in the S&P 500, 50 years of health privacy after death, 463 years of parish registers, plus the regulatory clocks from the Briefs and the three clocks on a data center. The reader picks two and races them.

**Why fourth.** Lowest cost on the list (every number is already sourced on the site), highly shareable as single images for the social desk, and it gives the Clocks thread a home page.

## 5 · Same Model, Different Customer

**What it is.** The reader sorts the same eight facts about a person (income, a missed payment, late-night use, giving history) into five machines: a sportsbook's value model, a church giving predictor, a lender's score, a feed's ranking, a landlord's screen. Same inputs, five prices.

**Built from.** House Already Knew (sort the data), Class Engine, Trust Market, Permanent Record Room 2, The Feed. Through line 2 in the curiosity engine.

## 6 · The Promise Audit

**What it is.** Pick the apps you use; see what each one's privacy policy says happens to your data if the company is sold or goes bankrupt. Extends FN 20's finding that the promise is the trigger.

**Cost.** Needs fresh primary sourcing (each policy read and dated) and a plan to keep it current. High practical value and the best sign-up driver here ("tell me when one of my apps changes its promise"), so worth doing after the list is working.

## 7 · One Message, Upstream

**What it is.** The Chain of Title's living circuit, pointed at AI: follow one chatbot message upstream through the data center, the power contract, the capital stack and the ratepayer's bill.

**Built from.** The engine device; FN 19; The Data Center Load; Same Building, Two Bills. Carries the Anthropic conflict disclosure.

## Suggested order

Build **The Loom** first, because its data file is the foundation the others plug into and it fixes a real maintenance problem. Build **Clocks Collide** alongside it as the quick win. Then **One Life, Every Ledger** as the lead-in to EP 10. The rest follow as updates to their home stories trigger the standing review.
