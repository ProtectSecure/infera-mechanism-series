# Editorial Standard v2 · Claims, Labels, Models and Routes

**Status:** Adopted 23 October 2026 after an outside critique of the Faith Layer and its companions. Applies to every exhibit, episode, brief and field note from now on, and to the drafts listed in [season-ii-weave-map.md](./season-ii-weave-map.md).

---

## Why this exists

The critique's central finding was correct: **a qualification at the bottom cannot undo an overbroad impression at the top.** Readers remember the hero line and the first number. Our disclosures were honest but often far from the claims they qualify. This standard closes that distance, and makes five related fixes.

## 1. The adjacency rule

A claim's critical limit sits **in the same sentence, caption or card** as the claim. The "What this exhibit did not prove" block remains, as a summary, never as the only place a limit appears.

Tests before publishing:
- Read only the hero, the stat cards and the room headings. Would a reader leave believing something the page does not support? Fix it there.
- Every stat card names its population and scope in the card ("general companion apps, not griefbots"; "two vendors' help pages").

## 2. Labels: what FACT does and does not mean

The four labels stay (FACT, REPORTED, INFERENCE, WATCH). FACT certifies **provenance, not truth in the world**: we read it at the primary source. Where that difference matters, add a sublabel:

| Sublabel | Use for |
|---|---|
| company documentation | a company's own help page, FAQ or policy (it proves the promise, not the practice) |
| study finding | a result within one study's sample |
| rule text / statute | the words of a law or regulation |
| official analysis | an agency's or market monitor's analysis |
| search result | an absence we observed ("we found no trial"); this is INFERENCE, never FACT |
| editorial model | weights, routes or scores Infera built |
| industry-funded | research produced with a party that sells into the market |

Banned: "FACT (opinion)". A scholar's argument is REPORTED · scholars' argument.

## 3. Models show bands, not precise scores

Any score built from Infera's weights displays a **band** (Low, Moderate, High, Very high), keeps a visible rubric, and says "editorial model." A number out of 100 implies measurement we have not done. Measured quantities keep their numbers.

## 4. Fairness is not symmetry

When two sides are shown together, name what they share and **state how they differ** in authority, scale, duration and remedy. The Same Building, Two Bills method is the template: say what each argument measures and when it holds. The Quiet Guest's Exhibit D now carries this comparison.

## 5. Attribute every theological claim

Each claim about God, mourning, idolatry, the body or silence is one of three, and the page says which:
1. **A tradition's teaching**, cited to that tradition's own source.
2. **A writer's interpretation**, named.
3. **Infera's argument**, introduced as "In Infera's reading."

Our contribution is framing questions communities deliberate over. We do not settle them. Scripture used in pastoral rooms is "the frame these sources use, not Infera's theology."

## 6. Moral language may not outrun the evidence

Where outcome evidence is thin, argue from what is established (consent, representation, commercial dependence, control) and present harm claims as warnings by named sources, beside the evidence that cuts the other way.

## 7. Two routes into every page

Every exhibit opens with the shared **At a glance** guide (`exhibit-guide.css` and `exhibit-guide.js`):
- The question, the strongest evidence, the main uncertainty, and **where Infera stands**.
- **Read the argument**: one plain-text conclusion per room, each labeled, so every interactive result also exists as a sentence. Works without JavaScript.
- **Explore the exhibit**: straight to the first room.

## 8. Say where we stand

Infera makes an argued case with an effort at fairness, not a neutral one. Faith Layer pages state the commitments: consent, truthful representation, limited collection, meaningful endings. Other pages state their own (Same Building: "the answer is conditional; we take no side on building data centers").

## 9. Housekeeping that carries credibility

- Exhibit numbering matches the homepage everywhere (Check-In is the third Faith Layer exhibit, Confessor the fourth, Inferred Faith the fifth).
- "Evidence through" is the date of the newest source cited; if older data underlies the main figures, say so: "Evidence through 2 October 2026 · market data through July 2026."
- Grief-counselor material in The Second Death stays exactly as published.
