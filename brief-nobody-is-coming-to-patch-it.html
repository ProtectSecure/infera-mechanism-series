Nobody Is Coming to Patch It

Overnight, someone reached into supermarket freezers across Europe and turned them into ovens. The machines were old, online and out of support. Their makers had stopped fixing them, and no law said they had to keep going. The same is true of the router in your hallway and the camera on your door.

Update, Nov. 10. France has now named a culprit. On Nov. 9 its cyber agency and foreign minister said a unit of Iran's Revolutionary Guards carried out the October attack on about 700 French stores. ALLEGED Iran's foreign ministry called the accusation baseless. REPORTED No government has attributed the November wave, the blood service attack or the U.S. commissary failures. Pro-Russian groups NoName057(16) and Z-Pentest have claimed the November supermarket attacks; investigators have not confirmed those claims. WATCH On Nov. 5, U.S. agencies issued a joint advisory on attacks against refrigeration controllers. REPORTED Sections 01, 02, 04, 05, 08 and 10 are revised; nothing about why the machines were exposed has changed.

01The nights the freezers ran hot

Two waves, one accusation

The attackers never needed to enter a store. They reached refrigeration controllers, the small computers that hold a case at its set point, through interfaces left reachable on the internet. Press play to step through what is on the record.

Late Aug. Event 1 of 13

    Built only from published reporting and official statements. Store counts are the agencies' and companies' own. France has attributed the October attack to Iran, and Iran denies it. France's report publishes no indicators of compromise, and we found no public independent confirmation of its evidence. Two pro-Russian groups have separately claimed the November wave; investigators have not confirmed that.

    The blood service attack changes the stakes more than the scale. A supermarket loses stock; a blood bank holds plasma for transfusion and biobank samples that cannot be replaced. What saved it was not a patch but a person: monitoring alarms and a technician on call. INFERENCE The 110°C figure is the set point the attackers programmed, not a temperature the products reached. REPORTED FrenchBreaches, citing Le Canard enchaîné; The U.S. Sun, citing BFMTV.

    02Why the old ones

    End of life is not end of use

    Danfoss says most of the hit units were older products it no longer updates, including the AK-SM 800 (end of life May 2021) and the AK-SC 355 (phased out in 2016). Some current AK-SM 800A units were also hit because they ran outdated firmware. REPORTED Danfoss announced a "hard End of Service" policy for legacy products in October 2025. REPORTED After the French attack, Danfoss and Copeland both said the affected devices were mainly discontinued products that no longer received security updates. REPORTED

    A freezer controller can run for many years after its maker stops patching it. The store still owns a working machine; nobody owns its security. In Belgium, the installer Sabcobel said about 20 affected units it manages need physical modification, not just a software update. REPORTED

    05Counterexamples

    Where control came back

    The lag is real, but not total. These are the cases where a rule, a court or an agency moved control back toward owners or closed a hole fast.

    Farm equipment · July 2026

    Deere opens its tools

    The FTC, with Illinois, Arizona, Minnesota, Michigan and Wisconsin, settled its antitrust suit. For ten years, owners and independent shops get dealer-level tools on "fair and reasonable terms," including resetting electronic locks and immobilizers, reprogramming engine computers and an offline mode. Deere files compliance reports every 60 days, verified by its CEO. FACT

    Limit: access to engine software depends on approval from a third-party vendor, and Deere can seek "good cause" extensions. FACT

    European Union · Sept. 2026

    A 24-hour clock on exploited flaws

    Since Sept. 11, makers selling into the EU must report actively exploited vulnerabilities to ENISA's platform within 24 hours. FACT The rule puts a duty on the maker up front, not through lawsuits after harm.

    Limit: the reporting duty covers products already in the field, but it makes the maker report, not patch. The design and support duties do not reach controllers already on store walls, and enforcement is untested. WATCH

    Phones · June 2025

    Five years after the shelf

    The EU requires OS updates for at least five years after a phone model leaves the market. FACT Google, Samsung and Honor now promise six to seven years. REPORTED It proves a minimum support period can be required.

    Limit: phones only, and one maker argues the wording makes updates optional. REPORTED

    Home routers · April 2026

    The FBI patches for you

    Under a court order, the FBI sent commands to Russian-hijacked TP-Link routers in more than 23 states, removing the attackers' settings and blocking their way back in. FACT It fixed devices whose owners would never have patched them.

    Limit: a factory reset undoes the fix, and the Justice Department gave no router count. FACT

    The response in France was fast: CERT-FR issued containment guidance within a day and the prime minister ordered ANSSI to investigate. That is a strong response, not prevention. INFERENCE Four weeks later France named a state, summoned its ambassador and asked for arrest warrants and sanctions. That is accountability aimed at the attacker. It does not patch a single controller, and the makers' duty to the machines already installed is the same as it was on Oct. 10. INFERENCE

    08Both sides

    Should makers keep old machines safe?

    Make makers carry it longer

    • The harm lands on people who never chose the device: shoppers, workers and the food supply. INFERENCE
    • The EU already requires minimum support periods for phones and, from 2027, for most connected products; a rule can be written. FACT
    • Makers know how long their machines stay in service; ending patches while units run keeps the risk in the field. INFERENCE
    • Some units need physical work to fix, which owners cannot do alone. REPORTED

    Owners and operators must replace

    • Danfoss announced the end of service for legacy products a year before the attacks and urged updates. REPORTED
    • CERT-FR's first fix was to take the controllers off the open internet, a choice the operator controls. REPORTED
    • Indefinite support raises costs for every buyer, including those who replace on schedule. INFERENCE
    • The cost of new controllers would fall hardest on small grocers either way. INFERENCE

    And who did it?

    France's case

    • France's report, issued by its interministerial cyber crisis centre (ANSSI, cyber command, the defence procurement agency and the intelligence services), attributes the October attack to Shahid Kaveh, a unit of the Revolutionary Guards' cyber centre, and names two of its commanders. FACT That the unit did it remains an accusation. ALLEGED
    • The evidence it describes: the attack infrastructure was also used, since at least April 2026, to scan for a type of Schneider Electric equipment used in small energy and water plants; the attackers used a scanning tool the report says the attack group habitually uses; a propaganda outlet the report links to the unit relayed the attack from Oct. 13; and "traces of Farsi" were found in the attack infrastructure. It calls this "a body of indicators." FACT
    • Paris prosecutors have opened an investigation into the unit and asked for international arrest warrants for its leaders; reports differ on whether two or three people are named. REPORTED
    • Outside analysts quoted by NPR called the attribution plausible. REPORTED

    The other side: what is not established

    • Iran's foreign ministry rejects the accusation as "baseless and malicious." REPORTED
    • We read France's report (CERTFR-2026-CTI-009). It publishes no indicators of compromise that outsiders could test, and it does not mention Danfoss, the November wave or the blood service. FACT We found no public independent confirmation and no other government joining the attribution. WATCH
    • Language traces can be planted, so on their own they are weak proof. INFERENCE
    • The accusation covers France in October. No government has attributed the November wave, the blood service attack or the U.S. commissary failures. WATCH
    • Separately, pro-Russian groups NoName057(16) and Z-Pentest have claimed the November wave of supermarket attacks. Investigators have not confirmed those claims, and one threat researcher doubts the groups did more than claim credit. We found no report that they claimed the October attack. REPORTED
