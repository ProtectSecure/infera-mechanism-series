i.
2009 · the filing · ACT I

Fourteen billion was the promise.

In 2009, Southern Company filed its certificate application for Vogtle Units 3 and 4 with the Georgia Public Service Commission. The budget: $14 billion. The promise: the first new commercial nuclear reactors built in the United States in three decades, ready by 2016 and 2017.

The structure of the deal mattered. Under the original Georgia statute that enabled Vogtle, construction risk sat with the utility's parent company. Ratepayers would begin paying only after the units were producing power.

GA PSC · Docket 27800
ii.
2013-2017 · the slip · ACT II

The schedule slipped. The statute changed.

By 2013, the first revision: +$1 billion, +2 years. By 2017, Westinghouse, the lead contractor, declared bankruptcy mid-construction. Southern Company assumed direct project management. The legislature passed an amendment to the original Vogtle statute allowing Construction Work In Progress — ratepayer payments to begin before commercial operation.

Each rate case after 2017 layered an increment onto the residential bill, while the cumulative project cost crept upward in publicly filed updates.

AJC · PSC raises rates for Vogtle
iii.
Dec 2023 · the transfer · ACT III

$7.6 billion moved
to ratepayer recovery.

December 2023. The PSC voted 4-1 to move $7.6 billion of remaining Vogtle costs directly into the rate base. The one dissenting vote was Commissioner Tim Echols. The mechanism was procedural; the effect was a permanent line item on every Georgia Power residential bill.

Cumulative final construction cost across both new units: $36.8 billion. The original budget, $14 billion, had been exceeded by 163%. The schedule slip was approximately 7 years.

PSC Order · Dec 2023
iv.
Today · the flow · ACT IV

From a kitchen table
to a dividend.

An average Georgia household now pays approximately $420 per year in Vogtle-attributable charges, embedded in the monthly bill. Multiply that across roughly 2.7 million residential accounts.

The dollars flow as designed: Georgia Power recovers ~$2.4B annually in Vogtle rate base. Southern Company, the parent corporation, reports consolidated net income near $3.9B and declares ~$2.8B in dividends. Vanguard, BlackRock, and State Street, the three largest institutional shareholders, together hold approximately 46% of the stock.

The asset is real. The distribution of who paid for it is the political question.

Southern Co · 10-K · 2024

Two stories live inside Vogtle. The first is a major piece of long-duration carbon-free infrastructure finished in a country that has not finished one in thirty years. The second is a fifteen-year case study in how regulatory structures quietly transfer risk from equity holders to ratepayers, one rate case at a time. Both are true. The political question is which one gets told.
