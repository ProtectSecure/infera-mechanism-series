The promise audit.

When a company dies, the law protects mostly what it promised. So what did your apps promise?

Room 1 · Your apps

Tap the ones you use. See what each promised.

The Estate Sale found that when a company goes bankrupt, the law slows a sale of your data mainly if the company had promised not to transfer it. So the question for any app is simple: what does its privacy policy say happens to your data if the company is sold or fails? Here are five, with the words as captured by policy trackers.

Room 2 · Do it yourself

Audit any app in one minute.

  1. Open the app's privacy policy and search the page for merger, bankruptcy, sale and assets.
  2. Find the sentence. Does it say your data may be transferred? Then the company kept the right to sell it with the business.
  3. Does it say the buyer must keep the same promises, or that you will be told and can delete first? Those are the lines that protect you.
  4. If you would not want a stranger to own it, delete what you can now. After 23andMe filed for bankruptcy, about 1.9 million customers asked for deletion before the sale. REPORTEDfrom The Estate Sale

The other side

  • A transfer clause is normal and often good for customers: it lets a buyer keep a service running instead of shutting it down.
  • Amazon's notice says transferred data stays subject to the promises in the earlier notice unless the customer agrees otherwise. REPORTED

What this remix cannot show

  • What any company would actually do. A policy says what is allowed, not what is planned.
  • State laws that may add rights. California, New York and Texas raised theirs in the 23andMe case, and lost. REPORTED
