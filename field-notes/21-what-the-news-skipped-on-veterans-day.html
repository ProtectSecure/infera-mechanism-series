Field Note № 21 · Evening Close · Wednesday, 11 November 2026

What the news skipped on Veterans Day.

The bond market was closed and Washington was quiet, so the cameras pointed at the ceremony at Arlington, a proposed arch and a tech rally. Behind the spotlight, three stories about the same war kept moving: who got their ballot counted, who gets to write the rules for the world's most important oil lane, and how close the war's threats now come to the people running it.

EXHIBIT I · THE QUIET THREADA quarter of Mississippi's fallback ballots were thrown out

ExhibitM02a
CatalogElection count
MediumAffidavit ballots
ProvenanceWLBT, CNN, AP figures

An affidavit ballot is the fallback: when a poll worker cannot confirm your registration, you vote on paper in a sealed envelope and the county decides later whether it counts. On Wednesday CNN projected that Democrat Scott Colom had beaten Senator Cindy Hyde-Smith, with Colom ahead by 4,585 votes at 99 percent counted. REPORTED The Associated Press had not called the race as of 4:19 p.m. Central. REPORTED

What the call did not explain: Jackson's WLBT reported that 24 percent of affidavit ballots were rejected statewide, and that nearly half were thrown out in Harrison County, on the Gulf Coast. REPORTED · headline only Colom's campaign says about 6,400 affidavit ballots were still pending and cites historical rejection rates of 55 to 65 percent. REPORTED · campaign figures Hyde-Smith has not conceded. Counties certify Friday.

Why this reaches you

If you move, change your name, or show up at a precinct whose list has an error, the affidavit ballot is what you get. Whether it counts depends on paperwork you may never hear about, and the rate varies by county. In a race this close, the rejection rate is not a footnote. It is the margin. If you vote in Mississippi, your county election office can tell you whether your affidavit ballot was accepted.

EXHIBIT II · THE QUIET THREADIran says the Strait of Hormuz now runs on its own law

ExhibitM01a
CatalogWar · energy
MediumInterview · shipping report
ProvenanceAl Jazeera, Khaleej Times, AFP

The Strait of Hormuz is the narrow channel between Iran and Oman that oil tankers must pass to leave the Persian Gulf. In an exclusive interview with Al Jazeera, Iran's parliament speaker and lead negotiator, Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf, said the strait will "never return" to its pre-war status, that it is governed by the laws of the two countries on its shores, and that the UN treaty on the law of the sea does not apply there. Iran and Oman were reported to have reached an understanding to oversee the waterway. REPORTED

The same day Iran's army said a tanker struck a mine in the strait and caught fire, and India's government said a tanker was hit in a "possible attack," with three Indian sailors safe. REPORTED The US military said it is keeping the strait open and enforcing its blockade of Iran. Iran said it has sent its answer through a Qatari mediator and the next move belongs to Washington. Brent crude rose between about 1 and 2 percent during the day. REPORTED · no settlement retrieved

Why this reaches you

The price of the gasoline in your tank and the diesel that trucks your groceries is set partly by whether ships can cross this water, and on whose terms. If one country's law replaces the shared international rules, every crossing carries a new risk premium, and you pay it at the pump before anyone signs anything. Tuesday's inflation report already showed energy up 0.7 percent in a month.

EXHIBIT III · THE QUIET THREADThe war's threats moved closer to the people running it

ExhibitU03
CatalogSecurity
MediumWire and live-blog items
ProvenanceCNBC, Khaleej Times, WSJ via Times of Israel

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent was reported to have moved to a military base after Iranian threats. REPORTED An Iranian security chief threatened to strike US bases in Europe if they were used for attacks, and US refuelling aircraft were pulled from RAF Mildenhall in England, which the Wall Street Journal tied to an Iranian threat, likely on Tuesday. REPORTED The Justice Department announced indictments of three Iran-backed operatives. REPORTED Each item appeared in a market blog or a regional live feed. None led a US evening newscast that we could find.

Why this reaches you

When the official who runs the country's borrowing has to work from a military base, the war is no longer only something happening far away. Threats to bases in Europe pull allies closer to the fight, and that changes what Congress will be asked to pay for and approve. You are the one who pays for it and votes on the people who approve it.

EXHIBIT IV · THE LOUD THREADWhat the cameras watched instead

ExhibitM05 · U01
CatalogCeremony · markets
MediumSpeech · price tape
ProvenanceAP, Spectrum, PBS, TheStreet

At Arlington National Cemetery the President spoke for about 25 minutes, defended the Iran war and said he wants "total victory, not a 93% victory." REPORTED He said a "magnificent triumphal arc" would soon be dedicated to veterans. The roughly 250 foot arch he wants near Arlington and the Lincoln Memorial is under a 14 day court order barring work, in a suit that includes three veterans. REPORTED The arch line became the clip.

On Wall Street, CoreWeave, a company that rents out computing power for artificial intelligence, jumped 24.2 percent after its earnings, and the Nasdaq closed up 1.60 percent, the S&P 500 up 1.31 percent at 7,619.38 and the Dow up 0.49 percent. REPORTED The bond market was closed for the holiday.

Why this reaches you

The ceremony deserved coverage. The number inside it got less: Pentagon figures cited by Spectrum News count 23 US service members killed and at least 865 wounded in this war. A rally in AI stocks moves retirement accounts for a day. The war those service members fought is moving the price of fuel for months.

THE CHAIN · HOW THESE ARE ONE STORYThe same war ran through the wreath, the water and the vote count

The news presented Wednesday as separate items: a holiday ceremony, a Mideast interview, a Senate projection, a stock rally. Follow the links instead.

The war honored at Arlington is the war whose strait Iran now claims to govern. That claim, and a tanker burning in the channel, pushed oil up again on a day when Tuesday's report had already shown energy prices rising. The fuel bill is the war arriving at your kitchen. Meanwhile, the Mississippi count, decided partly by which fallback ballots survived, helps set who controls the Senate that will vote on the war's money and terms, and on the arch the President dedicated to the people who fought it.

The same war ran through the wreath, the water and the vote count. The cameras showed the wreath.

  • Coverage is estimated. We did not query AP, Reuters, Bloomberg, the New York Times, the Washington Post or the Atlanta Journal-Constitution directly. Counts out of six are inferred from search results. NBC, CNN, the Anchorage Daily News and several other pages were blocked to our tools.
  • Headline-only figures. The 24 percent statewide and nearly half in Harrison County rejection figures come from WLBT headlines; we could not open the articles. The 6,400 pending affidavit ballots figure is the Colom campaign's. The Mississippi call is a CNN projection; the AP had not called it when our sources closed, and the 55 seat figure is from an NBC headline we could not open.
  • Single-chain reports. The Bessent relocation, the Mildenhall withdrawal and the mine strike each rest on one reporting chain. Iran's mine account and India's "possible attack" account differ.
  • No oil settlement. We report the intraday rise in Brent (about 1 to 2 percent), not the closing price, which we could not retrieve.

Method. Evening Close compares the events Infera scored before the day (board rows M01 to M10, plus delta rows) against what happened, how many of six major outlets covered each one, and how markets moved. Rows where importance far exceeds coverage are "what the cameras missed"; rows where coverage far exceeds importance are "the story that took the oxygen." This run's gap score is importance minus coverage share, rounded to 5. Full ledger: outcome-ledger-2026-11-11.csv.
