Field Note № 12 · The Ventriloquist 23 Oct 2026 8 min · companion to The Second Death

The dead don't stop talking. Someone else writes their lines.

Eleven days before the midterms, a ledger of every documented AI voice of a dead person used to persuade: Karunanidhi and Jayalalithaa campaigning again in Tamil Nadu, Suharto urging votes for Golkar sixteen years after his death, an AI Martin Luther King Jr. endorsing a Michigan House candidate, six gun-violence victims calling Congress with their families' consent, a Parkland student interviewed on what would have been his 25th birthday, and an AI Charlie Kirk, author unknown, speaking about his own killing from three megachurch pulpits. The dead are not frozen; they are updated by whoever holds the strings. The rules mostly look the other way: state deepfake laws are written around candidates, platforms only ask for a label, and the only laws requiring an estate's consent cover commercial uses. No study has yet tested whether a dead loved one's voice persuades more than a living one.

Field Note № 12 · Evening Close 12 Jun 2026 9 min · the cliffs that held

Two cliffs were set to fall on Friday, and neither did.

Section 702 lapsed at midnight, the first time in the program's history, then the Senate re-passed it 60-34 about forty minutes later, though the House had left town so the fix is not yet law (T01, gravity 92). The threatened strike on Kharg Island, the terminal that ships ninety percent of Iran's oil, was called off toward a deal (T02, 90). Underneath the relief, the quiet numbers: consumer sentiment bounced to 48.9 and, more importantly, inflation expectations eased (4.8 to 4.6 percent year-ahead, 3.9 to 3.4 long-run) on the one day the cameras were at a record SpaceX IPO (T03, 62). And the market rose for a third straight session over Thursday's record producer-price print (T04, 55). One pivot from escalation to deal-signaling relaxed the whole board, but two of the steps back are unsigned: the Iran deal is not finalized, the 702 fix is not law. Scaffolds fired 3 for 3, no misses, a second clean calibration day. A next-morning backfill of the missed June 12 close.

Field Note № 11 · Public Inspection 10 Jun 2026 9 min · the reading room · updated 06.12

Tomorrow's law sat on a public table all day Wednesday. Some of it was effective on arrival.

Every weekday at 8:45 AM, the documents that publish the next morning go on the Federal Register's Public Inspection desk, free and readable by anyone. Wednesday's stack held 144 documents: a final rule removing the disparate-impact standard from transportation civil rights (effective on publication, no advance comment), an interim final rule putting a new fee schedule on immigration court (live before its comment window opens), and an SBA proposal to make individually owned 8(a) firms prove social disadvantage case by case, the one item with a real lever attached, docket SBA-2026-0133, comments due July 13. All of it printed Thursday morning, the same hour the Supreme Court handed down opinions. Publication-day postscript included: 144 of 144 published, zero withdrawals, the two sealed rules effective the day they arrived. Updated June 12 with the open-windows ledger: five dockets, five deadlines, a CFTC move on prediction markets, and a lapse day on which nothing printed.

At the open the market bought the ceasefire. By the close it took the relief back.
