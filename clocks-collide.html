Clocks collide.

You get two days. A parish register gets 463 years. Ten clocks that decide who is protected, and for how long, on one wall.

A remix puts sourced facts from other exhibits side by side. Each fact keeps the tag it carries at home and links back there. What the remix adds is marked INFERENCE.

Room 1 · The wall

Ten clocks from across the site. Pick two.

Each bar is a length of time that decides something: how long you have to report, how long a record is kept, how long a company lasts. The bars use a compressed scale so that two days and 463 years fit on one wall; the number on the right is the real length. Tap any two clocks to race them.

Bar length uses a logarithmic scale: each step to the right is a multiple, not an addition. Years are counted as 365 days and months as 30.4.

Room 2 · What the wall shows

The clocks you must act within are the short ones.

The patternOn this wall, the windows a person must act within are counted in days and months. The spans that institutions and records get are counted in years and centuries. INFERENCEfrom the ten tagged clocks

The other side

  • Short reporting windows exist partly to stop fraud claims made long after the fact.
  • Long retention protects people too: a hospital record or a parish register may be the only proof of a birth, a treatment or a marriage.

What this remix cannot show

  • These ten are the clocks this site has documented. They are not a sample of all law. WATCH
