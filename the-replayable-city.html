Map key

Surveillance here is not one system. It is overlapping jurisdictions watching the same person for different reasons.

A normal commute in the DC metro passes through data environments owned or operated by separate agencies, vendors, and private participants. Each collects for its own purpose, under its own rules, on its own retention clock.

  • Metropolitan Police Department CCTV
  • District Department of Transportation traffic and speed cameras
  • WMATA transit cameras and body-worn cameras
  • U.S. Park Police monument-zone systems and aviation unit
  • U.S. Secret Service security perimeters
  • Federal building security (DHS / FPS / GSA)
  • Private property cameras (CameraConnect / Capital Shield)
  • Residential doorbell networks (Ring, Nest, others)
  • Automated license-plate readers (vendor networks)
  • School and transit fleet camera systems
  • Toll and traffic-enforcement systems
  • Federal and military installations (presence documented; capabilities not)
None of this becomes one clean composite file every morning. It becomes reconstructable. If something happens, pieces can be pulled from different systems and stitched into a timeline.

Marginalia · what the cartograph refuses

Two failure modes, marked on the margin.

refused · 01

Not paranoid surveillance panic.

"Big Brother is watching you live" is wrong, and it weakens the piece. The accurate frame is recoverability, not live tracking. Most footage is reviewed by no one unless an event triggers retrieval.

refused · 02

Not government conspiracy.

No agency coordinated this. The architecture is the product of decades of independent procurement, vendor consolidation, federal grant programs, and ad-hoc public-private partnerships. The accidental nature of the assemblage is part of the story.

honored

The watched keep their agency.

The commuter chose the mode. The commuter chose the route. The surveillance is conditional on lawful activity in public space. That conditionality is what makes the reconstructability question civically serious rather than merely lurid.

The other side

The strongest defense of the architecture, hosted fairly.

Defenders argue four things, each true in part. The point is not to refute them. It is to show that a defense calibrated to one system at a time misses the architecture of a system of systems.

01

Public safety

Cameras enable faster emergency response, suspect identification, crash reconstruction, missing-person searches, counterterrorism, transit safety, and event protection.

True. The response and identification value is real, and transit-security reporting documents it.
02

Public-space expectation

Courts have largely held that observation in public space is not a Fourth Amendment violation. The architecture operates within existing doctrine.

Public-space doctrine assumes the watched can identify the watcher and adjust behavior. An unmarked LPR network defeats that assumption.
03

Retention limits

Most systems retain footage for finite windows: MPD 90 days, Flock 30 days by default, private rebate minimum 48 hours.

Limits are circumventable when footage is preserved for litigation, investigation, or "training" purposes.
04

Oversight exists

Body-camera programs, internal-affairs reviews, audit logs, and in some cases public-records requests provide accountability.

Oversight operates per system. The integration across systems has no equivalent integrated oversight.
