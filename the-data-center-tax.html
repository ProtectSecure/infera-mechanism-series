Part of your power bill may be financing somebody's AI grid.

i.
The footprintAct 01

Georgia is now the second-largest data-center market in the country.

Hyperscale data centers — the warehouse-scale facilities that run AI training, cloud services, and content delivery — have clustered in Georgia at a pace that briefly made the state #1 nationally and currently puts it second to Northern Virginia. Atlanta metro, Douglasville, Newnan, the I-20 corridor.

The footprint is real, the demand is real, and the demand is growing.

SourceCBREJLL2024–25
ii.
The forecastAct 02

Georgia Power projects a 47% load increase by 2031.

The utility's most recent Integrated Resource Plan, filed with the PSC, forecasts an unprecedented load growth — driven primarily by large commercial customers, which is regulator-speak for hyperscale data centers. To meet it, the utility plans new natural-gas generation, extended coal-plant operation, and accelerated build of solar and battery storage.

Building generation costs money. That money becomes ratebase. Ratebase determines rates.

SourceGA Power IRP 2025PSC Docket 56002
iii.
The abatementAct 03

Most facilities arrived with a tax holiday.

Georgia exempts qualifying high-technology data center equipment from state and local sales tax. Counties layer property-tax abatements on top — sometimes for 10 or 20 years. The local case for these incentives is jobs and economic activity. The counter-case is the foregone revenue and the public-cost externality.

The hyperscaler arrives. The grid expands to serve it. The ratepayer pays for the expansion. The hyperscaler pays the abated rate. The math runs through.

SourceGA HB 696County tax abatements
iv.
The billAct 04

The cost lands on the residential rate. Plausible

Georgia Power's rate cases over the next three years will determine how the data-center-driven capital expansion is allocated. Existing rate structure socializes much of the new capital across all customer classes — residential, commercial, industrial. Residential customers without a data center pay a meaningful share of the new generation built to serve customers who do.

Other states are running the same experiment. Some are exploring "tariff isolation" — making hyperscalers pay the full marginal cost of the capacity they require. Georgia, so far, has not.

SourceGA PSC docketsIEEFA
v.
The horizonAct 05

The political ask is simple. The political will is not. Competing

Tariff isolation for hyperscale load is technically achievable in PSC procedure. Several utility commissions in other states are actively considering it. The question is whether Georgia's elected PSC, whose campaigns depend in part on utility-industry support, will adopt it before the residential rate impact compounds.

By the next gubernatorial term, the rate trajectory becomes a political variable. How it is presented to voters — as "AI bringing jobs" or "AI raising your bill" — depends on who tells the story first.

SourceNARUCPSC commissioners

What else could explain the GA grid build-out?

Hyperscale load is being socialized into the rate base — that is the framing this piece traces. There are several counter-framings that an industry economist or a county commissioner would press in response.

A · this piece

Grid investment to serve data-center demand is shared across all rate classes including residential.

B · pro-development

Jobs + tax base offset

Data centers generate construction jobs, ongoing operations jobs, and property/sales tax revenue that partially offset the rate impact at the county and state level.

DISTINGUISHING TESTDo per-county property tax and direct employment gains exceed the per-county residential rate burden attributable to hyperscale load?
C · structural

Demand-following grid policy

Utilities are legally required to serve load. Once a hyperscaler interconnects, the cost of generation to serve it has to land somewhere. The question is allocation, not whether the cost exists.

DISTINGUISHING TESTCould a marginal-cost pricing scheme isolate hyperscale cost without violating utility-regulation principles in Georgia?
D · dynamic

Grid modernization spillover

Capital that arrives to serve data centers also funds modernization (transmission, storage, renewables) that residential customers benefit from over time.

DISTINGUISHING TESTOver a 10-year window, do residential reliability and decarbonization metrics improve faster in hyperscale-heavy regions than in matched non-hyperscale regions?
