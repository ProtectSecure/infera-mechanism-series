# Discovery Questions — Ask Before Designing

Never start a site without answering these. The skill requires this step.

Use the `AskUserQuestion` tool (in Cowork mode) or ask in natural conversation. Group into the four rounds below. Do not skip a round to save time — every round protects against rebuilding later.

---

## Round 1 — The hero (who is on the page?)

1. **Who specifically arrives here?** A title, a role, a moment in their week. Not "decision-makers." Try: "the chief of staff at a Fortune 500 family office, on a Thursday afternoon, reviewing options for next quarter."
2. **What did they Google to land here?** This is the language they will recognize as their own. Use it in Act 2.
3. **What other tabs are open?** What alternatives are they evaluating? You will be implicitly compared.
4. **What time pressure are they under?** Day, week, month, year? This calibrates urgency in the setup.

## Round 2 — The conflict (what is the stake?)

5. **What is the quiet thing they're worried about?** Not the loud thing they say in meetings — the thing they think about at 11pm.
6. **What have they tried that didn't work?** This is what you implicitly differentiate from.
7. **What is the cost of doing nothing?** Quantify if possible. This becomes the setup's tension.
8. **What is the cost of getting this wrong?** Different from #7 — this is the worst case, not the status quo.

## Round 3 — The guide (what does the brand know?)

9. **What is the one thing the brand knows that almost nobody else does?** This is the heart of Act 3, chapter 1.
10. **What is the proof?** Specific outcomes, specific clients (or anonymized versions), specific numbers with sources. This is the Tufte data through the page.
11. **What is the strongest case study the brand has ever delivered?** This becomes Act 4 — the breaker.
12. **Who at the brand will the user actually meet if they convert?** A name, a role, a calendar. This becomes the reassurance line.

## Round 4 — The resolution (what is the next step?)

13. **What is the one action the user should take?** One. Not three.
14. **What happens immediately after they take it?** This is the reassurance copy.
15. **What is the longest-term outcome the brand wants the user to remember six months later?** This is the memory anchor — plan recurring visual motifs around it.
16. **What single phrase do you want them to quote to a colleague?** Write it. It will appear as the pull-quote or the closing line.

---

## Compress into a single paragraph

After all four rounds, compose this paragraph aloud or in writing. If you can say it without hesitation, you can build the site.

> The user arriving at this site is **[who]**. They are quietly worried about **[conflict]**. They are looking for **[outcome]** but have been let down by **[common alternative]**. By the end of this scroll, they will understand **[single insight]** and will feel that the natural next step is **[action]**. The single thing they will remember six months later is **[memory anchor]**.

If any blank cannot be filled, return to that round's questions.

---

## Tone calibration check

Also confirm:

- **Industry vertical** (finance / government / luxury / tech / healthcare / consulting / etc.) — determines palette and type system
- **Audience seniority** (board chair, CFO, ops director, technical lead) — determines copy density and proof type
- **Geography** (US, EMEA, APAC, global) — determines spelling, currency, regulatory framing
- **Sales motion** (high-touch consultative, product-led, RFP-driven, founder-direct) — determines CTA architecture
- **Existing brand assets** (typography, colors, voice) — apply or override?

If the brand is new (no existing assets), choose the palette and type system from `references/04-typography-color.md` deliberately based on industry.
