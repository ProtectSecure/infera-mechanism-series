Act II · The Weld

Do not cover them apart

The day's two heaviest threads share a single heat source. The CPI energy component is literally priced by the Strait of Hormuz. Reporting the print and the crisis as separate items misses the only story that matters tomorrow, the seam between them.

T01 · Gravity 82 · FACT / REPORTED

CPI (May) · 8:30 AM

Consensus reads plus 0.5 percent month over month and 4.2 percent year over year on the headline, which would be the largest annual gain since April 2023, with core at plus 0.3 percent. Two drivers are stacked, the Iran energy spike feeding gasoline and a second wave of tariff pass-through. Ship the energy branch if gasoline carries the headline; the tariff branch is the quieter burden-shift read.

T02 · Gravity 80 · REPORTED / FACT

Iran-US ceasefire · Hormuz · intraday

Trump is floating a signed deal in two or three days. The Israel-Iran mutual halt holds, but Iran conditions it on Israeli strikes in Lebanon stopping, and the dual naval blockade keeps the Strait effectively closed. The 24 billion dollar frozen-asset release is the named talks price. A fragile halt riding on a promise, not a signed text, with Lebanon as the trip wire.

The under-covered risk is the supply-side mirror. The EIA petroleum report at 10:30 AM quantifies the same energy move and gets buried between the two giants. Three reads of one shock: the CPI energy line, the EIA inventories, and the Hormuz blockade.

Act III · The Gauge

CPI · coverage gravity

Why now. The April 2026 print ran at the fastest annual pace in roughly two years on energy. The recurring shape is eve-prep, then an energy-led headline. The new variable tomorrow is the simultaneous tariff pass-through wave landing on top of the Gulf spike. Two checkpoints follow the print: the EIA inventories at 10:30 AM, which mirror the energy line from the supply side, and the Treasury 10-year auction at 1 PM, a clean referendum on whether buyers demand more yield after the number.

Next checkpoint: the 8:30 AM release, then PPI on Thursday June 11 tells you who absorbs the cost.
