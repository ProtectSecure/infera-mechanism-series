The Seized Record

Nine months ago the FBI took Fulton County's 2020 ballots. Here is what the record shows, tested against the Justice Department's own published rules. We do not predict how it ends.

Illustration from Midjourney Explore, made by another Midjourney user. It is not the Fulton County facility and depicts no real place.

AS OF Nov 1, 2026 READ ~15 min LAYER Facts on the record METHOD SVNIF

On Jan. 28, 2026, FBI agents executed a court-ordered search at Fulton County's election hub in Union City and drove away with the county's 2020 ballots. Nine months later, no one has been charged, the ballots have not come back, and Election Day is two days away.

This update does one narrow thing. It lines up what the search warrant claimed against what earlier reviews found, then holds the government's steps up to a rule the Justice Department wrote for itself, in Justice Manual section 9-85.500 and its election-year memos: no action timed to affect an election.

TIMING MEMO · We publish this before Nov. 3 because every fact in it is already public, and it names no candidate. It reports on events, not on how anyone should vote. A watch layer follows after the election.
What happened

The record, in order

  1. DEC 11, 2025FactThe Justice Department sues Fulton's Clerk of Courts for the 2020 election records. In February a court pauses that civil case pending potential criminal proceedings.
  2. JAN 28, 2026FactThe FBI executes a search warrant at the election hub. It authorized seizure of every physical 2020 ballot, plus machine tapes and voter rolls. Reports put the haul at more than 600 to about 700 boxes; the counts are not reconciled.
  3. FEB 10, 2026FactA federal court unseals the warrant and affidavit. The probe began in part with a referral from Kurt Olsen, the White House's director of election security and integrity.
  4. APR 17, 2026FactA federal grand jury subpoena seeks names and contact details of thousands of 2020 election workers and volunteers.
  5. MAY 6, 2026FactU.S. District Judge J.P. Boulee rules the government need not return the ballots. Fulton received digital copies.
  6. JUL 3, 2026ReportedAn FBI memo surges about 260 analysts into a "priority investigation." AP's sources say it supports the Georgia 2020 inquiry.
  7. JUL 7, 2026FactU.S. District Judge William Ray quashes the worker subpoena: it "is unreasonable and must be quashed." He notes the statute of limitations has run.
  8. AUG 6, 2026FactThe Justice Department appeals Judge Ray's ruling to the U.S. Court of Appeals for the 11th Circuit.
  9. SEP 28, 2026ReportedOlsen leaves the Justice Department, where he had been moved in June. FBI Co-Deputy Director Andrew Bailey, who worked on the Georgia probe, resigns the same day, citing personal reasons.
  10. OCT 5, 2026FactThe Justice Department files its opening brief in the 11th Circuit appeal.
  11. BY OCT 24FactThe Justice Department notifies six Georgia counties it will send election monitors for Nov. 3: Bibb, Chatham, Cobb, DeKalb, Fulton and Gwinnett. Five of them also had monitors in 2024.
  12. OCT 29, 2026ReportedActing Deputy Attorney General Trent McCotter writes election officials in several states that it is "a crime to willfully fail to comply" with federal record-retention rules, "punishable by up to a year in prison." The letter does not name Fulton County, ABC News reports.

Exhibit B

The norm ruler

"Law enforcement officers and prosecutors may never select the timing of public statements (attributed or not), investigative steps, criminal charges, or any other action in any matter or case for the purpose of affecting any election, or for the purpose of giving an advantage or disadvantage to any candidate or political party."

Attorney General Merrick Garland, Election Year Sensitivities memo, June 24, 2024 (now in the department's archive; we found no 2026 reissue). The standing written rule is Justice Manual section 9-85.500.

There is no written 60-day rule. Former officials describe a longstanding practice of holding overt steps that could affect an election, and the memos tell staff to consult the Public Integrity Section about timing, a section that has itself been sharply cut (Lawfare). So the ruler below marks distance from Election Day, not a deadline. Tap an event for how the rule applies.

Both sides, in their own terms

What each side can point to

The case that this is ordinary law enforcement

  • FactA federal magistrate judge signed the warrant, and a district judge declined to order the ballots returned.
  • FactA January 2021 report by Seven Hills Strategies, the State Election Board's monitor, called Fulton's absentee processes "extremely sloppy and replete with chain of custody issues."
  • FactState investigators confirmed Fulton double-scanned more than 3,000 ballots during the recount.
  • ReportedThe Justice Department called its subpoena the "next step in the normal investigative process," and argued the statute of limitations was premature: "we are not sure what charges can be brought. That's the whole point of the investigation."

The case that this is political pressure

  • ReportedThe referral came from an official who, the AJC reports, "has long pushed debunked claims of election fraud."
  • ReportedThe AJC reports each allegation was investigated earlier and no intentional misconduct was found.
  • FactJudge Ray noted the statute of limitations has run, so the records could not support charges for 2020 conduct. The Justice Department disputes this on appeal; the 11th Circuit has not ruled.
  • ReportedGeorgia's Republican secretary of state has called similar claims "baseless and repackaged."
  • ReportedOlsen was moved off the matter in June and left the Justice Department in September.
ON THE MONITORS · Election monitoring is a long-running Justice Department program; five of the six counties had monitors in 2024, and Fulton's election board chair called this year's deployment "not unusual." InferenceIt is not simply separate from the 2020 matter: the same Civil Rights Division, led by Harmeet Dhillon, runs the monitors and filed the December suit for the 2020 records. All six counties voted Democratic in each of the last three presidential elections.
Brady box · the strongest evidence against our reading

Our reading is that the record shows real 2020 process failures and no public evidence of intentional fraud. Against it: parts of the supporting documents were kept redacted at the government's request, so evidence we cannot see may exist. Judge Boulee found the affidavit flawed but said it did not show an officer lied or hid facts that would defeat probable cause. And an investigation can be legitimate even when its subject has been examined before.

Where Melophon stands

Our argued position

InferenceThe January search does not break the Justice Department's timing rule on its face; it came more than nine months before the vote. The rule is tested by what happens now. One step has already landed inside the window: the Justice Department's Oct. 5 appeal brief, whose date was most likely set by the appeals court's schedule. Charges, a new subpoena, or a public statement about the 2020 matter before Nov. 3 would deserve the scrutiny the Department's own memos call for. Election monitors should be judged by how they behave at the polls.
