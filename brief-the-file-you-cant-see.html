The File You Can't See

A merit board picks an officer for promotion. Then, by two newsrooms' accounts, someone searches the internet for what that officer once said and did under orders, prints it, and puts it in a paper file the officer never sees. The promotion stops. The law says the officer should be told why, in writing, and get to answer. This brief is about that gap: what happens when lawful acts become a record, the rules change after the fact, and the file is kept where its subject cannot reach it.

      03The clock

      Delay is the decision

      An officer on a promotion list does not need to be removed to lose. The law sets limits on delay, but it also takes a name off the list automatically if the officer is not appointed within the eligibility period, unless the Senate has already consented. Move the clock.

      05Where a check exists

      One court, one bill, one open letter

      Federal court · Feb. 2026

      Kelly v. Hegseth

      Judge Richard Leon blocked Hegseth's censure of Sen. Mark Kelly, a retired Navy captain, and the reopening of his retirement grade over a video urging troops to refuse illegal orders. He ruled it violated Kelly's First Amendment rights and wrote that "our retired veterans deserve more respect from their Government." FACT

      Limit: a retired officer and a speech claim. The Pentagon appealed to the D.C. Circuit on Feb. 24. No court has ruled on a withheld promotion. REPORTED

      Congress · FY2027 defense bill

      Write it down, tell us

      The Senate committee's version would cap withholding before disposition at 30 days without a detailed explanation to Congress, require notice to Congress within 30 days of a delay, and remove the "impracticable" exception to written notice for the officer. FACT

      Limit: committee text, not law. We found no final enacted version as of Nov. 10. WATCH

      Inspector General · July 2026

      Eleven questions

      Sens. Brian Schatz and Cory Booker asked the Pentagon's inspector general to review the removals: what authority was used, the documented reason for each officer, whether race, gender or public statements played a role, and whether officers were told. FACT

      Limit: a request. No findings have been published. WATCH

      Senate · Sept. 2026

      Seven days, in writing

      Sen. Tammy Duckworth asked the bill's negotiators to require written notice within seven days, with a clear justification, for any officer whose promotion is withheld, delayed or removed, plus a records-correction process, retroactive to Jan. 20, 2025. REPORTED

      Limit: a request to committee leaders. WATCH

      06From printouts to pipelines

      This file was made by hand. The next one may not be.

      Nothing reported shows that software chose these officers. MS NOW's source describes internet searches, printouts and paper files, deliberately off the department's systems. ALLEGED We found no report that AI was used. WATCH

      But the machinery to read a service member's public life at scale is already being built for other purposes. Since October 2021 the Pentagon has run continuous vetting of all its personnel for insider threats, flagging new records as they arrive, and officials said social media screening would likely be added. REPORTED In June the Defense Counterintelligence and Security Agency said it wants AI to cut parts of background checks "from months to hours," handling "little tiny decisions" and passing an evidence package to a human analyst who decides. REPORTED

      Those systems exist to catch security risks, not to grade loyalty, and they come with rules. The point is narrower: a search done by hand and a search done by machine differ in speed and scale, not in kind. The safeguard against both is the one the promotion statute already names: tell the person, in writing, what is in the file, and let them answer. INFERENCE

      07Both sides

      Civilian control, or a hidden test?

      The Pentagon's case

      • "Military officers serve at the pleasure of the President and the Secretary," spokesman Sean Parnell told MS NOW; the Secretary "has acted and will continue to act within his authority." REPORTED
      • A board's selection is not a promotion: the Secretary, and for senior grades the President, can delay or remove names, and the Senate must still confirm. FACT
      • Hegseth says he wants a promotion system that is "ruthlessly meritocratic" and focused on warfighting, and that earlier policies put identity ahead of merit. Parnell: the department "will not entertain demands for demographic scorecards." REPORTED
      • An anonymous senior official told RedState that several white male officers were also removed. REPORTED
      • Civilian leaders choosing senior officers who share their priorities is how civilian control is meant to work. INFERENCE

      The critics' case

      • The new test came after a merit board had already selected the officers, and was applied to acts that were lawful, and often required, at the time. ALLEGED
      • The statute ties delay to listed grounds and requires written notice and a chance to answer; two officers say they were told by phone and given no chance to respond. ALLEGED
      • About half of the 40-plus officers blocked were women or members of minority groups, per The Times. REPORTED
      • Sen. Jack Reed, the Armed Services Committee's top Democrat, said removing the four Army officers, if accurately reported, "would be illegal." REPORTED
      • Keeping the file on paper and the instructions by phone, as described, is a choice that leaves no record for an inspector general or a court to review. INFERENCE

      What nobody outside can see yet: the files themselves; whether every removal from a one-star list was formally signed by the President, who holds that power; and whether any officer received written notice. WATCH
