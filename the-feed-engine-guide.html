SECTION 01 · DIAGNOSISPAGE 02
01 · the diagnosis

What just happened to you
on The Feed Engine.

You didn't pick the world you were shown. You also weren't manipulated. You were served convenience, and convenience compounded into a private reality.

The Feed Engine showed you two phones in the same city on the same day. The headlines were different. The ads were different. The prices were different. The framings of the same events were different. No memo was written that said show this person worse choices. Nothing about the result required malice.

What it required was scale, optimization, and your consent, given thousands of times, in millisecond increments, every time you tapped on something and every time you didn't. Your information environment is now the artifact of those consents.

The trade-off was convenience. The cost was shared reality.

This manual is not about deleting apps. Most of the apps will stay. This is about rebuilding the diet that runs through them, and rebuilding it in a way that is small enough to actually maintain.

Six sections. Fourteen pages. One printable card. By the end, you'll have a single weekly discipline that can survive the moment a week from now when you've forgotten you read this.

SECTION 02 · THE MAPPAGE 03
02 · the map

Five layers of curation
in a normal day.

Most readers think their information diet has two ingredients: the news they read and the social posts they scroll. The honest count is closer to five.

Each of these five layers is making editorial decisions about what reaches you, and each is optimized for a different outcome. Some serve the platform, some serve the advertiser, some serve themselves at your expense. Knowing which is which is half the battle.

01
PLATFORM RECOMMENDATIONS"FOR YOU"

What TikTok, YouTube, Instagram, Netflix, and Spotify decide you'll watch / hear next. Optimized for retention, not relevance.

02
SOCIAL FEEDS"FRIENDS"

What of your network's content the algorithm surfaces and what it suppresses. Most posts you've never seen.

03
SEARCH RESULTS"ANSWERS"

What Google, ChatGPT, Perplexity, and TikTok-search return to your questions, and which sources they trust. Personalized more than you think.

04
AD INVENTORY"OFFERS"

What products you're shown, at what prices, under what framings. The most directly economic of the five layers.

05
PRICE DISCRIMINATION"DEALS"

Same product, different price, different person. Airline fares, ride-share, streaming subscriptions, even some retail. Often undetectable without comparison.

Your first surprise on reading this list: there is less unmediated input in a normal day than you imagined. That's the honest baseline. Now we work from it.

SECTION 03 · PRACTICE 01PAGE 04
03 · the five practices · one of five
01
ROTATE · one alternative source per topic per week.

If you read about politics from one source, read about politics from a different source on Friday. If you get tech news from one publication, get it from a second one this week. Not "instead of," but "in addition to."

The reason rotation matters is that tone, framing, and selection diverge far more than facts do. Same election. Same earnings report. Same supreme court ruling. Read about it from three places and the differences in which facts get emphasized tell you something the facts themselves never would.

You are not trying to read every perspective. You are trying to disrupt the one your algorithm has narrowed you to. One additional source per topic per week is enough to bend the curve. Less than that and the algorithm wins; more than that and you'll quit.

DO THIS WEEK
  • Pick one topic you already follow (e.g. AI, your city's politics, climate, a sport).
  • Pick one publication you've never read that covers it (the tools page has suggestions).
  • Read one of their long pieces on the topic. Don't skim. Read.
  • Notice what they emphasized that your usual source didn't.
SECTION 03 · PRACTICE 02PAGE 05
03 · the five practices · two of five
02
TRIANGULATE · read the same story across three outlets.

When a story actually matters to you (before you form an opinion, before you share it, before you argue about it) read it from three different outlets, ideally with three different editorial leans.

Most arguments online are between people who read different summaries of the same source material. The argument feels substantive but it isn't, because the underlying disagreement is about which facts surfaced first.

Triangulation breaks that pattern. You don't have to read three full articles. Often three headlines and three lede paragraphs are enough to show you the shape of the disagreement, and once you see the shape, you stop being inside it.

DO THIS WEEK
  • Find a story you might share or argue about today.
  • Open it in Ground News or AllSides (page 09): both show coverage spread.
  • Read the headline and first paragraph from one left-leaning, one centrist, and one right-leaning source.
  • Decide what you think after the triangulation, not before.
SECTION 03 · PRACTICE 03PAGE 06
03 · the five practices · three of five
03
AUDIT · monthly reset of your feed inputs.

Once a month, deliberately review what's feeding your feeds. Unfollow what doesn't compound. Unsubscribe from what arrives but never gets read. Revoke app permissions you forgot you granted.

Your information diet has accumulated by default. Most of the accounts you follow, sites you subscribe to, and apps that have access to your data, you didn't deliberately choose. You consented once, in a moment, and they've been with you since.

A monthly audit re-asserts authorship. The point is not to minimize: it's to re-decide. The accounts you actively pick after a year of drift are very different from the ones you picked in a hurry the first time.

MONTHLY · 30 MINUTES
  • Social: unfollow 5 accounts that no longer compound. Add 1 that does.
  • Email: open the last 20 newsletters in your inbox. Unsubscribe from any you didn't open this month.
  • Permissions: review which apps have location / contacts / camera / mic access. Revoke any you don't actively need.
  • Subscriptions: open your phone Settings → Subscriptions. Cancel one. (There's always one.)
SECTION 03 · PRACTICE 04PAGE 07
03 · the five practices · four of five
04
SLOW · chronological feeds, scheduled checking, inserted friction.

The algorithm needs your speed. Slow it down and you starve it. Switch your feeds to chronological order where you can. Batch your checking. Add small barriers to the apps that make you scroll without choosing to.

The algorithm's edge is the gap between your finger landing on the screen and your brain catching up. Every fraction of a second of inserted friction (a 5-second app delay, a hidden app icon, a closed tab, a chronological feed instead of a "for you" feed) moves a decision from autopilot into awareness.

Awareness is the one variable the system cannot optimize around.

DO THIS WEEK
  • Switch Instagram and X to "Following" instead of "For You" at least until next week.
  • Move social apps off your phone home screen. Put one boring screen between you and them.
  • Use a screen-time limit on the apps you scroll most. (iOS Screen Time · Android Digital Wellbeing.)
  • Pick one window per day for email and news. Outside that window, don't open them.
SECTION 03 · PRACTICE 05PAGE 08
03 · the five practices · five of five
05
CROSS · deliberately consume what your cluster doesn't.

Once a week, deliberately read or watch something that the algorithm would never serve you. Different politics. Different class. Different region. Different generation.

This is the practice the system cannot help you with, because it has no way to model the value of breadth. Its incentive is to make you predictable. Yours, here, is the opposite.

Crossing doesn't mean accepting. It means encountering. Read what people unlike you are reading, watch what they're watching, before you decide what you think of them. Most of what looks like ideological disagreement online is actually asymmetric exposure to different information environments. Cross the gap regularly and the disagreement looks smaller.

DO THIS WEEK
  • Read one substantive piece from a publication associated with a politics you disagree with.
  • Watch one long-form video by a creator outside your demographic.
  • Have one conversation with someone whose feed you'd guess looks nothing like yours. Ask what's in it.
  • Notice what you assumed about that world that turned out to be wrong.

SECTION 06 · CIVIC FRAMEPAGE 12
06 · the civic frame

Why this matters past
individual hygiene.

A shared reality is the civic precondition for democracy. The information you consume becomes the votes you cast, the policies you defend, the neighbors you trust.

Democracy is not the act of voting. Voting is the visible part. Democracy is the much longer act of forming a view about your community, your country, and your shared situation that is compatible enough with everyone else's view to negotiate decisions together.

That negotiation depends on a baseline of shared information. When that baseline diverges to the degree The Feed Engine demonstrated, when two neighbors are quite literally inhabiting different epistemic worlds, the negotiation breaks. Not because either neighbor is wrong. Because they don't have the materials to recognize what the disagreement is actually about.

You are not just protecting yourself.
You are protecting the possibility of shared facts.

SECTION 06 · CIVIC FRAMEPAGE 13
06 · the civic frame · continued

The practices in this manual are individually small. Their power is that they are compatible with a full life: they don't require leaving social media, deleting smartphones, moving off-grid, or pretending you don't enjoy the convenience. They require, instead, that you act as the editor of your own information diet at least once a week, with a printed card on your fridge.

This is the soft version of cognitive sovereignty. The hard version costs years of practice and a different relationship with your phone. The soft version costs roughly ninety minutes a week and gives you, in exchange, the recoverable thing: the ability to know what you've been shown, and to choose, occasionally, to be shown something else.

That's the leverage. That's what the system cannot auto-correct around. Awareness, exercised in small ritual, scaled to the day.
