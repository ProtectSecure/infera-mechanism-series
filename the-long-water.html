Election week · Exhibit

The Long Water

Two years after Hurricane Helene, by the state's own figures, most of the damage in western North Carolina is still unpaid. The storm is on the ballot as a fight over who failed. This exhibit measures the gap and sets up the test we will run when the votes are counted.

Illustration from Jared Anderson's Midjourney library. Not a real town; no damaged homes or people are shown, by design.

AS OF Oct 29, 2026 READ ~18 min LAYER Pre-election facts METHOD SVNIF

North Carolina puts Helene's damage at about $59.6 billion statewide, most of it in the western counties. As of June 30, the federal government had committed about $9.7 billion of it. The state's own August report puts that at roughly 16%.

The rest of the gap does not vanish. It sits in houses still not rebuilt and in towns that lost residents, in a region that counts about 2,500 fewer tourism-supported jobs than in 2023. This exhibit shows the gap at the scale of the state, then sets out a test for the question everyone is asking: does a gap like this move votes?

On the ballot

A blame fight in one state, a credit fight in the next

ReportedIn North Carolina's Senate race, Democrat Roy Cooper's campaign says Republican Michael Whatley, the president's Helene recovery point person, "has completely failed in his role as federal recovery czar." Whatley's campaign says he helped "deliver more aid for Hurricane Helene than any other natural disaster" in state history.

ReportedBoth campaigns blame the other for slow aid, but their recent ads lean on the economy and crime. A Western Carolina University political scientist told Grist that Helene has figured in ads less than he expected.

ReportedAt a Sept. 16 rally, President Trump joked that more FEMA money for western North Carolina depended on a Whatley win, then said he was kidding, WUNC reported. The White House denies that disaster decisions are political.

ReportedIn Georgia, a $531 million federal block grant for farmers opened for applications in March 2026. Sen. Jon Ossoff said the money came "over a year late." When the deal was first announced in September 2025, Agriculture Secretary Brooke Rollins said it would give producers relief "in record time."

InferenceSame storm, opposite grammar. In North Carolina the money is a question of who failed. In Georgia it is a question of who delivered.

Exhibit B · built to run on Nov. 4

The test, stated before the results

We write the test down now so the answer cannot shape the question.

Hypothesis

Counties with a larger unpaid share of Helene damage will show a larger change in turnout or margin from 2022 than less-damaged counties with similar politics.

What would support it

Hard-hit counties move together, and in the same direction, beyond what statewide trends explain.

What would refute it

Hard-hit counties track their neighbors and the state. A statewide surge alone proves nothing about Helene.

FactStatewide, North Carolina early ballots were about 36 to 37% above the same point in 2022 through Oct. 27, per state election board data reported by WLOS and Call the Map.

ReportedBy Oct. 27, Buncombe County had already cast 72,585 early ballots, 93% of its entire 2022 early vote, by Call the Map's count of state files.

InferenceHigh early turnout in a storm-hit county is not evidence that the storm moved votes. The surge is statewide. The test compares counties against each other, and county shifts never describe individual voters.

The part no gauge reads

The street reopened. The season did not all come back.

ReportedThe region has lost about 2,500 tourism-supported jobs since 2023, most in Buncombe County, according to an August 2026 North Carolina Rural Center report cited by WLOS.

ReportedRegional unemployment is now about 3.3%, below state and national rates, while wages remain below both averages, WLOS reports from regional estimates.

ReportedThe state's Renew NC home-rebuilding program is about $500 million short of what eligible repairs need, according to the Land of Sky Regional Council's director.

InferenceLost wages and slow rebuilding are part of the gap that damage estimates count only once, when the storm hits.

Both sides, in their own terms

How the aid is argued

The aid is flowing

  • FactThe state's August report shows federal commitments up to about $9.7 billion, and about $14.4 billion with state money, as of June 30.
  • ReportedGrist reports about $10 billion in federal assistance has been provided so far, and that Vice President Vance promised $858 million more.
  • ReportedSmoky Mountain News reported in April that disbursements had sped up under Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin.
  • ReportedWhatley's campaign credits him with more Helene aid than for any other natural disaster in state history.

The aid has failed

  • FactThe state's report puts federal money at about 16% of damage as of June 30, 2026.
  • ReportedGrist reports the state puts unmet need at about $41 billion after state funding and insurance.
  • ReportedA WRAL/WSOC poll found half of North Carolinians dissatisfied with the government's response; in the west, 5% were "very satisfied."
  • ReportedAn Asheville Citizen-Times investigation found no documented communication between Whatley and FEMA in the two years after the storm, Grist reports.
Brady box · the strongest evidence against our reading

Our reading is that most of Helene's damage is still unpaid, and that the gap is a real force in western North Carolina. Against it: the $59.6 billion includes $5.8 billion for strengthening and mitigation, so it is more than repair alone, and federal aid rose by about $1.9 billion between the state's March and June reports. Disaster aid never covers all damage; insurance and private money fill part of it. And the region's unemployment is now below the state's.

Where Melophon stands

Our argued position

InferenceBy any measure the state or the press has published, most of Helene's cost is still being carried by the people and towns it hit. Whether that changes how they vote is a separate question, and we will not answer it with early-vote counts. We will answer it, or fail to, with county results and the test above.
