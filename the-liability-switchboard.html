Room 1 · The board

Who pays? Flip it and watch the alarm.

The Second Alarm argues that a company builds protection where the law makes it pay for the harm. This board lets you test that across five kinds of loss. Set each switch to who pays. The lamp shows whether the record on this site says a protective alarm got built under that rule: green for yes, red for no, striped where we have no record.

If a switch seems to break the thesis, flag it. Those flags are the most useful thing a reader can send us.

Room 2 · Read against it

Where the thesis is weakest.

Against the thesis

  • Two of five domains have no "company pays" case on this site. An untested switch proves nothing. WATCH
  • Britain's scam losses might have fallen for other reasons; the review credits the rule using its own method. WATCH
  • Some alarms are built with no liability at all, such as a bank's customer-set gambling block. REPORTED
  • Making a company pay also makes it watch its customers more closely. Protection and surveillance arrive together. INFERENCE

For it

  • Where the loss was moved by law (card fraud, British scams), detection followed in the record. INFERENCE
  • Where the customer carries the loss, the documented alarms protect the company first: integrity monitoring, construction-cost recovery. INFERENCE
