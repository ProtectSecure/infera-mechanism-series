What else could explain the registration gap?

Multiple interpretations of the eviction-to-non-vote pipeline drift below. They're indistinct by default — like all real explanations in a real news environment. Move your cursor through them. Click one to bring it forward.

A · PRIMARY · THIS PIECE
Eviction-driven attrition

Housing instability triggers address change, mailer failure, inactive status, and ultimately purge — a structural pathway that removes voters.

— Test: Do magistrate eviction filings correlate with inactivation rates at the precinct level above the rate predicted by overall mobility?
B · ALTERNATE · NULL
Non-voters either way

Voters affected by eviction would not have voted with high probability regardless. The registration loss may not change electoral outcomes meaningfully.

— Test: Among voters who lost registration via this pathway, what was their pre-eviction voting frequency in the prior two cycles?
C · ALTERNATE · PROCEDURAL
Race-neutral by design

List maintenance is statutory, applied uniformly, and not designed to track any demographic variable. Any disparate impact is incidental.

— Test: Do counties with substantially different demographic compositions produce identical inactivation rates after controlling for mobility?
D · ALTERNATE · REFORM-TRACTABLE
Same-day registration would fix it

The pipeline exists because Georgia requires advance registration. States with same-day registration produce different and smaller attrition signatures.

— Test: Have states with same-day registration shown measurable reduction in post-eviction non-voting?

Why this direction

The Fog of Interpretation puts the reader in the same epistemic position as a working investigator: nothing is automatically clear, every reading has to be earned by attention. The cursor-as-lens metaphor makes interpretation an active labor rather than a passive consumption.

Strengths: emotionally honest about uncertainty, novel interaction language, rewards careful readers, signals editorial humility.
Weaknesses: requires explicit instruction, slow on mobile, can feel obscurantist if overdone.
