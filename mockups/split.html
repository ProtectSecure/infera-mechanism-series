A constellation of competing reads · split-reality mode

One fact. Four different worlds.

The same number — the registration gap — read through four interpretive frameworks. Each pane uses different vocabulary, different causal chains, different emotional tone. Hover any pane to expand it.

The shared fact
"In high-eviction Fulton precincts, voter registrations dropped at roughly 1.4× the rate of low-eviction precincts in the same county over 2022–2026."
SOURCE · GA SoS list maintenance · Fulton magistrate filings
A · ECONOMIC LENS
Material instability
"The system removes voters when their economic ground gives way."

The registration loss is downstream of the housing market. Rising rents, stagnant wages, and a tight inventory produce eviction filings, which displace households, which break the address-on-file pipeline that registration depends on. The civic harm is a knock-on effect of a market failure.

eviction filing→displacement→address-stale→purge
What this lens emphasizes: rent levels, wage trends, builder supply, county economic conditions. What it underplays: the specific statutory rules of the registration system.

B · POLITICAL LENS
Structural disenfranchisement
"The system, by design, removes the politically marginal."

The same data read as a question of access to ballot. List-maintenance procedures are state-legislated; the disparate impact on lower-income and renter populations is a politically tractable choice. Reform options exist (same-day registration, automatic re-enrollment); the absence of reform is itself political.

statutory rule→disparate burden→non-vote→election outcome
What this lens emphasizes: who benefits from the procedure as-is, what reform looks like, who blocks it. What it underplays: the upstream economic causes.

C · MEDIA LENS
Invisible attrition
"The story doesn't break because nothing dramatic happens at any single moment."

The registration loss is structurally untellable by event-driven media. There's no rally, no scandal, no soundbite — just six administrative steps each lawful and undramatic. Coverage privileges flashpoints; this story has none. The invisibility is itself a media-system property.

no flashpoint→no coverage→no salience→no reform pressure
What this lens emphasizes: news production constraints, attention economy, why slow stories die. What it underplays: the actual mechanism causing the loss.

D · CULTURAL LENS
A vanishing civic ritual
"To be a voter is increasingly to do paperwork most people don't know exists."

The same loss read as a question about civic culture. Voting once required physical presence; now it requires sustained administrative literacy. Households experiencing instability have less of that literacy. The civic identity 'voter' is becoming a class-coded competence.

civic literacy→administrative survival→turnout→representation
What this lens emphasizes: civic identity, education, household norms, generational shifts in voting habit. What it underplays: the structural and political fixes.

Why this direction

Split-Reality makes the literacy point at a glance: frameworks shape conclusions. Watching four panes treat the same number with four vocabularies, four causal chains, and four moods produces an experience most people have never had — and one that is the missing core of media literacy.

Strengths: the comparison is the argument; new and pedagogically powerful; mobile-friendly stacks well; great for sharing screenshots.
Weaknesses: requires careful editorial writing to make the lenses genuinely distinct without parody; loses some emotional intensity per-pane.
