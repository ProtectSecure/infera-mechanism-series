Stream 01 · Vogtle · utility cost recovery

A nuclear plant gets built once. Its balance sheet gets paid every month, forever.

The cost overran by a factor of two and a half. Original budget fourteen billion; final cost thirty-six point eight billion. The state's Public Service Commission moved the largest tranche — $7.6 billion in December 2023 — from shareholder books onto ratepayer bills. A residential rate sheet that read one way before the units came online reads twenty-three percent heavier after.

The cost is now distributed in arithmetic. Roughly four hundred and twenty dollars a year, per residential ratepayer, on average, for the life of the recovery period. The arithmetic does not move when the headline does. It does not care which party holds the runoff. It is in the kitchen drawer, in the envelope, in the late notice. The bill is the politics.

Burke County hosts the reactors. Burke does not pay more for hosting them. Burke counts as one of one hundred and fifty-nine in the rate base. The geometry of who built it, who lives next to it, and who pays for it does not align.

Stream 02 · Data centers · load forecast

A handful of buildings redraw the demand curve for an entire state.

Georgia Power's 2024 Integrated Resource Plan projected eight thousand two hundred megawatts of new load by winter 2030/31, an increase of more than two thousand two hundred megawatts over what the same plan had forecast just a year earlier. The single largest driver is hyperscale data centers, concentrated in a narrow ring of metro and exurban counties.

The IRP filing kept coal-fired units online beyond their planned retirement to serve the new load. Multiple intervenor experts at PSC hearings testified that the magnitude is uncertain at the level claimed. The regulatory commitments built on the forecast are not uncertain. They have been approved.

Who pays for the new gigawatts is being decided in rate-design proceedings now. The current default is socialization across the residential class. Coweta hosts. Fulton pays. Burke continues to host the reactor that already gets paid for. The accounting is statewide; the development is intensely local.

Stream 03 · Opioid settlement · abatement inflow

Six hundred and thirty-eight million dollars, paid in installments, for harm already done.

Georgia and its local governments stand to receive roughly $638 million under the national distributors and Janssen settlements, with the National Distributor share disbursed across eighteen years and the Janssen share across nine. The Kroger settlement adds another $55 million. In November 2024 the Department of Behavioral Health and Developmental Disabilities awarded $70.3 million to 128 organizations, averaging roughly two hundred thousand dollars each, against more than $250 million in requested funding.

The split is seventy-five state, twenty-five local. The local share is concentrated — Atlanta and the largest counties absorb the bulk — while a long tail of small counties receives modest direct allocations. The use is restricted: funds must be spent on opioid abatement, not transferred to general operating budgets. The restriction is the entire moral architecture of the settlement.

The geography of receipt is correlated, but not identical, to the geography of harm. The check arrives where the case was filed; the harm is where the prescription was written. The atlas is built to show both.

Hidden systems · cross-stream

What only becomes visible when you read all three together.

Each stream alone is reported. The geometry of overlap is not. Five short vignettes — each a small system that exists between counties, not within them.

Vignette 01 · the triple-hit county
A county is hosting, paying, and receiving simultaneously.
A handful of counties — Fulton above all — pay the largest absolute share of Vogtle recovery, host a meaningful slice of the data-center buildout, and absorb the largest direct opioid abatement allocation. The political experience inside that county is three contradictory financial signals at once, each with its own constituency, each with its own evening news segment, each requiring its own theory of grievance.
Triple-hit · n=11TIER · STRONG
Vignette 02 · the host that doesn't get paid
Burke hosts the reactor. The rest of the state pays for it.
Burke County lives next to Plant Vogtle. Burke's share of the recovery is indistinguishable from any other small ratepayer county. The geography of construction, the geography of risk, and the geography of revenue are three different maps. Tax-base hosting agreements have value; they do not net out the rate impact across one hundred and fifty-nine counties. Hosting a reactor is not, financially, the same as benefiting from one.
Single county · BurkeTIER · PROVEN
Vignette 03 · socialized infrastructure
The data center is in Coweta. The transmission rebuild is in everyone's bill.
Hyperscale data-center development concentrates in roughly six counties. The transmission and generation buildout required to serve them is being socialized across the residential rate class. The PSC's rate-design proceedings are arguing now over whether tariff structures will keep doing that. The default has been yes. The dataset to test the alternative does not yet exist publicly at the county level.
Concentrated host counties · n≈6TIER · STRONG
Vignette 04 · the harm-receipt offset
Opioid receipt is weakly correlated with opioid harm.
The settlement distribution is largely scaled to population and to litigation participation, not to opioid-death rate or first-responder workload. Appalachian counties with the highest per-capita opioid mortality receive a long tail of small awards; metro counties with the largest absolute receipts have lower per-capita harm. The architecture is legal, not epidemiological. Use restrictions matter precisely because the math does not.

Vignette 05 · the runoff inside the stack
The runoff cohort is a subset of the stack.
The June 16 runoff cohort — the voters who actually return for the second ballot — is whiter, older, wealthier, and more reliably partisan than the primary cohort that produced the runoff. That same cohort is, on average, less exposed to opioid-mortality strain, more exposed to direct billing from the utility layer, and located disproportionately in counties that benefit from data-center commercial activity. The runoff is not a separate event from the stack. It is the stack, sampled.
Cross-cycle, post-certTIER · PLAUSIBLE
Vignette 06 · the silence
No statewide candidate is running on any of these three streams.
Five statewide and federal runoff campaigns are active as of this writing. None of them have made Vogtle, the IRP load forecast, or the opioid settlement architecture a top-three message of the runoff cycle. The streams are documented, the dollars are large, and the agenda is empty. The agenda's silence is itself a finding; it is the variable the Common-Ground Detector and Discourse Cartographer were built to surface.

Methodology · how this atlas was constructed

The page tells you what kind of claim each number is.

Atlas-level claims are tier-tagged. Per-county figures shown in the Ledger and the Top-5 strips are illustrative-of-pattern unless the corresponding county-level dataset is fully sourced. Marker dots and triple-hit flags are deterministic from the underlying inputs. Where county-level granular numbers are not yet derivable from public filings, we mark the panel and we do not invent the decimal.

Stream 01 · Vogtle

Aggregate Proven · per-county Illustrative

$36.8B build, $11B rate-base addition, $7.6B Dec 2023 recovery order — multi-source verified. County-level annual ratepayer share is approximated by household count × statewide average; precise per-county tariff incidence is not published as such.

Stream 02 · Data centers

Aggregate Strong · contested · per-county Illustrative

8,200 MW IRP forecast is filed and approved; expert intervenor testimony actively contests the magnitude. County-by-county MW assignments shown here are based on publicly identified projects and analyst-reported clusters, not a tariff-class breakdown.

Stream 03 · Opioid settlement

Aggregate Proven · per-county Approximate

$638M aggregate, 75/25 split, $70.3M DBHDD November 2024 awards — verified. Per-county cumulative receipt approximated from the local-share formula reported by gaopioidtrust.org and DBHDD allocations; exact county totals will update as quarterly disbursements close.

Triple-hit identification

Deterministic from the three flags

"Triple-hit" = county above the 50th percentile in Stream 01 AND above the 50th percentile in Stream 02 AND above the 50th percentile in Stream 03 (or above the per-stream cluster threshold for stream 2). Eleven counties currently meet all three.

Correlation matrix

Pearson · illustrative magnitudes

r values shown are pedagogical approximations of the relationships present in the underlying county dataset; before publishing as a single statistical claim we run population-adjustment and report bootstrap intervals.

Flow map

Schematic geography · not topographic

The Georgia outline is simplified for readability. County positions are roughly geographic but not GIS-accurate. Flow magnitudes are scaled by stream total; flow directions are deterministic from each stream's documented direction (utility outflow, datacenter capex into host, settlement inflow).
