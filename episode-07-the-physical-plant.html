EP 07 · The Physical Plant · INVESTIGATE · v0.2 · 3 June 2026

One data center can equal an entire airport.

Hartsfield-Jackson — the busiest airport on Earth — runs on roughly 150 megawatts. A single hyperscale data center can need 100–250 MW. Georgia is on track for forty of them by 2030. Almost nobody is monitoring it. Almost nobody is voting on it. And the largest tenants are the apps in your pocket.

I.· THE SCALE · WHAT A MEGAWATT LOOKS LIKE

The most energy-hungry tenant Georgia ever attracted is the one nobody voted for.

The unit of measure that matters here is the megawatt. One megawatt powers about 750 to 1,000 American homes. Hartsfield-Jackson is one of the largest single-meter loads in the Southeast. A single hyperscale facility can match it. Georgia Power's 2024 IRP says forty equivalents are coming.

i.
A megawatt is roughly 750–1,000 American homes.
Continuous, year-round. The continuous demand makes data centers behave more like factories than like office buildings, and grid planners price them accordingly.
EIA · Residential averages
ii.
Hartsfield-Jackson runs on roughly 150 MW.
Across 4,700 acres, five runways, 192 gates, 63,000 on-site workers, and the world's largest single passenger throughput. The airport's electricity bill is one of Georgia Power's largest single-customer accounts.
ATL · sustainability report
iii.
High-end hyperscale facilities now reach 250 MW.
A decade ago, 30 MW was a large data-center campus. AI-training racks at 40–100 kW each have pushed individual campus loads past 200 MW. Same building footprint — vastly more compute density.
Data Center Frontier
iv.
Georgia's projected 2030 data-center load is ~8,200 MW.
Roughly 55 airports. Roughly 18 City-of-Atlanta-equivalents. Roughly the entire current peak demand of South Carolina. That number is the centerpiece of Georgia Power's 2024 IRP — the document justifying the next generation buildout.

III.· WHO'S WATCHING · THE REGULATORY VOID

Six other jurisdictions have caps, codes, or moratoriums. The United States has none.

Singapore stopped permits for three years over energy concerns. Ireland's Dublin region has refused new grid connections since 2022. China caps Power Usage Effectiveness at 1.3. The EU just made facility-level reporting mandatory. The United States has no national energy-efficiency standard for data centers at all.

United States
Federal void · patchwork at state level
No national standard

No federal energy-efficiency standard. No mandatory PUE reporting. No siting moratorium authority. EPA Energy Star for data centers is voluntary. FERC governs interstate transmission but not the buildings themselves. State PUCs handle rate cases — but not facility-level disclosure. The most heavily concentrated capacity on Earth is also the least regulated.

DOE · EPA · FERC · NARUC 2024
European Union
Energy Efficiency Directive · EU 2023/1791
Mandatory reporting

Facilities >500 kW must report energy consumption, PUE, water use, and renewable share annually to the EU Commission and member states. Data is public. EU Code of Conduct on Data Centre Energy Efficiency in place since 2008. Annex lists 175 measurable best practices.

EUR-Lex · Directive 2023/1791
Singapore
Three-year moratorium · 2019–2022
Hard pause

Singapore's Infocomm Media Development Authority froze all new data-center permits for three years over land, power, and water constraints. When permits reopened in 2022, they came with strict PUE caps and a competitive allocation process. Hyperscalers had to bid for slots.

IMDA · EDB · 2022 framework
China
PUE 1.3 cap · tier-1 cities
Hard efficiency cap

National framework requires PUE ≤ 1.3 for new builds in Beijing, Shanghai, Guangzhou, Shenzhen. National "East Data, West Compute" plan steers data centers toward regions with surplus renewable generation. Mandatory annual disclosure of energy, water, and carbon metrics.

NDRC · MIIT 2021–2023
Ireland
Dublin connection freeze · 2022 →
No new grid connections

EirGrid has refused new Dublin-region data-center grid connections since 2022 after data centers exceeded 20% of national electricity demand. Made formal policy 2024. Ireland is the warning case the US is most often compared to in industry analyses.

EirGrid · CRU 2022 policy
Netherlands
Amsterdam regional pause · 2019 →
Selective pause

Amsterdam metropolitan region paused new hyperscale data centers in 2019. National-level 2022 spatial planning rule restricts hyperscale data centers to two designated areas. Land-use restriction as climate policy — a tool not readily available in the US federal system.

VROM · gemeente Amsterdam
THE VOID· The United States hosts roughly 40% of global hyperscale capacity. It has the largest concentration. The most growth. The most water and grid stress. And the only major jurisdiction without facility-level disclosure rules. The reason isn't an oversight. There is no federal authority that has clear standing to regulate the buildings themselves — energy code is state, land use is local, FERC governs the wires not the racks. The fragmentation is the regulatory void.
IV.· THE OPPOSITION · WHAT IS BEING FOUGHT, BY WHOM, AND FOR HOW MUCH

An asymmetry of money meeting an asymmetry of attention.

There IS opposition — in courts, on ballots, at zoning hearings, in legislative committees. The pattern that recurs across states is the gap between industry spending and opposition spending, and the gap between which side commands which timescales.

⟶ 2024 spending · data-center policy and siting · modeled estimate

Industry · estimated $280M+
Direct lobbying + state-level PAC giving + sponsored research + chamber/trade-group giving across all major host states. Hyperscaler-affiliated and REIT-affiliated entities combined. Sources: OpenSecrets · NIMSP · state-level lobbying disclosures.
Opposition · estimated $14M
Foundation grants to opposition groups + community legal-defense funds + foundation-funded research at think tanks. The ratio is approximately 20 to 1. That ratio compounds annually. The opposition is organized — but it is fighting on the industry's chosen ground (rate cases, zoning hearings, committee votes), not on the public's natural attention ground (Atlanta-airport comparisons, monthly bills, water bills).

After multi-year organizing, the Board of Supervisors voted to formally find continued unrestrained data-center expansion is not in the public interest. Comprehensive Plan amendment now restricts data centers in the Rural Crescent. First US county-level binding finding of its kind.

V.· THE INVISIBLE TAX · WHAT YOUR DAILY USE COSTS

Your refrigerator and your Netflix are now in the same energy class.

The grid bill arrives every month. The data-center bill arrives every minute, attached to every stream, every backup, every AI query. It is not on your statement. It does not exist as a number you can see. But it is the largest single category of consumer-side energy growth in 2024.

VI.· THE STAKES · WHY THE WINDOW IS NOW

The decisions that lock the buildout in for a generation are being made right now, at scales most readers don't watch.

Three decision windows are open. Each closes in less than two years. Each is being made in venues with low public attention — by design.

Window 1 · 2026 GA session
HB 1192 sunset

If reform passes in 2026, the sales-tax exemption that brought hyperscalers to Georgia ends in 2030. If it dies again, the exemption auto-renews and the same architecture compounds for another 8 years.

Window 2 · 2026 GA PSC elections
Hyperscale tariff authority

Two PSC seats up. Composition determines whether commercial customers can be carved into their own rate class. Low-turnout runoffs historically decide outcome.

Window 3 · 2027 IRP filing
Next reactor cycle

Georgia Power's next IRP is expected to propose new generation to serve AI demand. If approved on current rate-base treatment, the next $35B will be authorized using exactly the mechanism S1·06 documented for Vogtle.

Federal · 2026 reauthorizations
Energy Star · DOE standards

EPA Energy Star for data centers and DOE's Better Buildings Initiative are both up for reauthorization. Either could become a vehicle for mandatory facility-level disclosure — or could remain voluntary in perpetuity.
