    The Four Locks

    A woman in Maryland recorded the crime against her. The recording could not be used. Two years of bills tried to change that. Here is what each one would actually have done.

    Read time 13 minUpdated 11 Nov 2026Sources: bill texts, amendments, testimony, court opinions

    Content note: the opening describes a sexual assault, briefly and without detail beyond what a prosecutor put in the public record.

    Under Maryland law today, a secret recording usually stops at the first lock.

    Researched and compiled with Claude (Anthropic). Final editorial judgment: Melophon. This exhibit discusses AI-generated audio; Anthropic is not a subject of it. Melophon is based in Maryland, so the law in this story also governs how we report.

    File 01 The phone was already recording

    She pressed record before she walked in

    In February, a senior assistant state's attorney in Calvert County, Rebecca Cordero, sent the House Judiciary Committee a letter about a case from years earlier. FACTwritten testimony on HB 132, 20 Feb 2026

    A woman brought their two-year-old daughter to her estranged husband's home for a visit. She knew he could be violent, so she started her phone's recorder before she went inside. He held a gun to her head and raped her. The phone captured the encounter. She went straight to the police. REPORTEDas described in the letter

    "I could not admit it as evidence in his trial."Rebecca Cordero, Calvert County State's Attorney's Office, to the House Judiciary Committee

    The reason was not doubt about the recording. It was the way it was made. Maryland makes it a crime to record a private conversation unless everyone in it agrees, and it bars courts from hearing audio captured that way. FACTCourts and Judicial Proceedings 10-402 and 10-405

    What the letter does not tell us: the woman's name, the court, the year, the verdict, or whether a judge ruled on the recording. We did not try to identify her. Her account reaches us through a prosecutor who was arguing for a bill; we treat it as one documented case, not as proof of how often this happens.

    File 02 Four questions, not one

    The debate keeps asking one question. There are four.

    Every secret recording that might matter in a courtroom has to clear four separate locks. They protect different things. Opening Lock 1 usually opens Locks 3 and 4 as well, but no key at Locks 1, 3 or 4 answers Lock 2. INFERENCEour framework, built from the statute and the rules of evidence

    Lock 1Lawful?Was it legal to record? In Maryland, not without everyone's consent. A violation is a felony: up to five years, a $10,000 fine, or both. FACT
    Lock 2Real?Is it what it claims to be, unedited and unfabricated? Courts call this authentication. FACT
    Lock 3Admissible?May a court hear it? Maryland excludes unlawfully intercepted audio and anything derived from it. FACT
    Lock 4Shareable?May it be passed on or published? Disclosing an unlawfully intercepted conversation is its own offense. FACT

    Arguments about this law often treat the locks as one. Try sorting the real arguments from the record yourself.

    File 03 What the bills said

    The same bill, two different ideas

    Between 2025 and 2026, Maryland lawmakers filed two kinds of changes, and one bill switched from the first kind to the second in the middle of the session.

    The judge's test. SB 1007 in 2025, then HB 132, HB 802 and SB 661 as first filed in 2026, left recording illegal but let a judge admit a recording after specific findings: that the case involved a crime against a person (SB 661 as filed also allowed hate crimes; HB 802 dropped this condition and reached civil cases too), that the recording went to a material fact, that it was not made for Maryland police, that it was more probative than any other evidence reasonably available, and that admitting it served the interest of justice. The other side got at least 14 days' notice under SB 1007, HB 132 and SB 661, and notice "sufficiently in advance" under HB 802. FACTbill texts and DLS fiscal notes

    On 11 February, the Maryland Judiciary sent the House a neutral note on HB 802. Its point was narrow: the recording would become admissible, but making it would still be a crime. The note called that a "statutory conflict." FACTJudiciary informational comment on HB 802

    The lawful-recording rule. In March, the Senate Judicial Proceedings Committee rewrote SB 661 with one amendment. The new version made it lawful to record the commission or attempted commission of three crimes: murder, rape, or a first-degree assault that is domestically related. It gave anyone charged for recording a defense if they reasonably believed they were in imminent danger of becoming a victim of one of those crimes. It let such recordings be used only as evidence of those crimes, and it left Maryland's rules of evidence untouched. The Senate passed it 36 to 2. FACTamendment 643622/1; Third Reader fiscal note; Senate vote record

    For the three listed crimes, the rewrite closes the gap the Judiciary had flagged. It also covers far fewer cases than the judge's test. The record we read does not say why the committee chose it. INFERENCE The Judiciary's 11 February note addressed HB 802, not the March text of SB 661. FACT We keep the two apart.

    File 04 The fake

    The AI argument is real. It is about Lock 2.

    At a House Judiciary briefing in November 2025, a Baltimore City public defender, Marguerite Lanaux, argued that two-party consent protects people from evidence fabricated with AI and audio manipulation. REPORTEDMaryland Matters and WMAR; we have not reviewed the briefing video The concern is serious. In one study, listeners picked out synthetic speech 73 percent of the time, and training helped only a little. The study used older voice tools and warned listeners that fakes were coming, so real-world detection may be worse. FACTMai et al., PLOS ONE, 2023

    The consent rule does stop a fabricated "secret recording" at the courthouse door. It stops it by category: every secret recording is out, real or fake. It never asks which is which. And a forger who claims everyone agreed to be recorded never meets the consent rule at all. INFERENCE

    The question of whether a file is genuine belongs to authentication. In Maryland, that bar is evidence sufficient for a reasonable juror to find the item is what its proponent claims; the state's highest court applied that test to video in 2024. FACTMaryland Rule 5-901; Mooney v. State, 2024 None of the 2025 or 2026 bills changed it. SB 661 said so in its text. FACT We found no documented Maryland case of fabricated audio being used against a defendant. That is a gap in what we could find, not proof that it has not happened. INFERENCE

    The other side has more than the AI argument. The public defender's written testimony in 2026 did not mention AI at all. It argued that the consent rule protects everyone's privacy, including people accused of serious crimes, and asked lawmakers, if they changed anything, to limit it to particular cases and to bar any police involvement in the recording. FACTOPD testimony on HB 802 and HB 132, 24 Feb 2026 A law professor at the 2025 briefing framed the choice as one between two rights: to consent before being recorded, or to record any conversation you are part of. REPORTEDDavid Gray, as quoted by WYPR and WMAR

    Federal courts are working on the same problem and have not settled it.     File 05 Two chambers, two halves

    Each chamber passed one half

    Lawmakers also tried a second approach: keep recording illegal, but make it a misdemeanor instead of a felony. Supporters said that would spare survivors who record abuse a felony charge. The maximum penalty would stay the same. FACTHB 688 and SB 680 texts and testimony A county prosecutor's office and the public defender both testified for it in 2026. FACTSB 680 witness list

    Why no vote?

    Four explanations. Open each to see what the record supports, what cuts against it, and what is missing. More than one can be true.
    Not enough time Weakened
    For: SB 1007 passed the Senate on 4 April 2025, three days before the session ended. SB 661 got its House hearing on 31 March 2026, 13 days before the end. FACT
    Against: HB 132 and HB 802 were heard on 24 February, seven weeks before the end, and got no vote either. FACT Time alone does not explain them. INFERENCE
    The committee wanted a broader rewrite Open
    For: the committee spent two hours in November 2025 on the whole law, including why doorbell video is allowed and its audio is not. Several competing bills were filed. REPORTED
    Missing: we found no statement linking the lack of a vote to a broader plan.
    Not enough support on the committee Open
    For: the public defender asked for limiting amendments, and privacy concerns were on the record. FACT
    Against: the chair sponsored one of the bills. FACT
    Missing: no committee vote was taken, so there is no tally to read.
    Each chamber waited on the other Open
    For: the mirror pattern across two sessions. An advocacy group urged passing the penalty bill together with an evidence bill. FACTMNADV testimony on SB 680
    Against: a pattern is not evidence of intent. We found no record of any linkage. INFERENCE

    Our status: not established. The record cannot tell these apart. What would: statements from committee members, voting-session agendas, or how the 2027 session handles the same bills.

    File 08 Method

    What we could not establish

    • Why the House committee took no vote, in either year.
    • The outcome of the case in File 01, or whether a judge formally ruled on the recording.
    • How often Marylanders are charged for recording abuse, or how often fabricated audio has been offered in Maryland courts.
    • Whether the public defender's words at the briefing match the press accounts exactly. Two outlets agree; we have not reviewed the video.

    Where sources disagree: Maryland Matters, citing the National Conference of State Legislatures, counts 10 all-party consent states; WYPR says 11. REPORTED Counts can differ by how states with mixed rules are classed. We do not use either number in our argument.

    A record note: the Department of Legislative Services' final note on SB 661 says similar legislation was not introduced in the previous three years, while its notes on HB 132 and HB 802 from the same session cite SB 1007 and HB 314 of 2025.
