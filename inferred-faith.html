FACT Virginia defines sensitive data as personal data "revealing" religious beliefs and requires a consumer's clear, affirmative consent to process it. Colorado's rules expressly require consent for "Sensitive Data Inferences," with a narrow exception for consumers over 13 when the inference is obvious, used only as disclosed, not shared and deleted within 24 hours. REPORTED California gives a right to limit sensitive information used to infer characteristics; Utah and Iowa require notice and a chance to opt out; Maryland allows sensitive data only when strictly necessary for a requested service and bans its sale. 2019 to 2020 · REPORTED

Mass-goers, by phone

CatholicVote said it geofenced churches and found nearly 200,000 Wisconsin Catholics who attended Mass three times in 90 days; about half were not registered to vote. Its president: "We don't need permission to reach millions of voters."

2020 to 2021 · REPORTED

A prayer app's location data

Motherboard found Muslim Pro, with over 96 million downloads, sold location data to the broker X-Mode, which had sold location data to military contractors. Apple and Google later banned X-Mode.

Jan 2022 · REPORTED

A platform pulls the label

Meta removed detailed ad targeting for "religious practices and groups," such as "Catholic Church" and "Jewish holidays," calling them topics people may perceive as sensitive.

Dec 2024 · REPORTED

The FTC steps in

The FTC alleged Gravy Analytics sold lists of people who visited places of worship and linked people to religious viewpoints inferred from location; its order bars selling sensitive location data.

BOTH SIDES, AT THEIR STRONGEST

Should a system be allowed to guess your faith?

Some inference is ordinary, and sometimes useful

  • A grocery app that notices kosher or halal orders and shows more of them is serving a customer, not surveilling a believer.
  • Churches, mosques and synagogues use the same tools to reach people who are searching; outreach is a form of free exercise too.
  • Consent banners for every guess would bury people in prompts; Colorado's 24-hour exception exists because some inferences are obvious and harmless.
  • Platforms have already pulled religious targeting labels voluntarily, and the FTC has acted against the worst brokers without new law.

Faith is the wrong thing to guess without asking

  • Religion is the category history punishes people for. A list of who prays where is dangerous in the wrong hands, and lists change hands.
  • You cannot correct a guess you never see. A wrong inference can follow you into prices, politics and feeds.
  • Thirty states have no comprehensive privacy law at all, so the same guess is regulated in Richmond and unregulated across the river in other places.
  • "Revealing" religious belief was written for what people disclose. Whether it reaches what machines infer is mostly untested.
WHAT THIS EXHIBIT DID NOT PROVE

The inference weights in Room A are our model; we did not test any platform's systems, and major platforms say they do not target ads by religion. Signals are chosen to be ordinary practices, not stereotypes, and any one of them can belong to someone of another faith or none.

We read Virginia's statute and Colorado's rule directly. California, Utah, Iowa, Connecticut and Maryland come from law-firm summaries; the remaining states' consent rules come from one January 2026 comparison chart whose grid we could not fully verify. Florida's law reaches only very large companies; we mark it but did not analyze it.

Whether state laws treat an inference as "revealing" religious belief, outside Colorado's explicit rule, is untested as far as we found. Nothing here is legal advice.

FORM YOUR OWN READ

Five questions to ask of any app on your phone

  1. Does it collect your precise location on weekend mornings, and who does it share that with?
  2. If it guessed your faith, would it have to tell you, and could you see the guess?
  3. Does your state require your consent before it uses that guess, and did you ever click yes?
  4. Does your house of worship's own app or website share data with ad networks?
  5. Would you answer differently if the guess were wrong?
