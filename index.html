How to read these pieces

People experience politics materially before they experience it ideologically.

Electric bills. Commutes. Insurance. Housing. Food prices. These shape emotional receptivity long before voters articulate formal politics. This series traces those structural pressures — not as the single cause of outcomes, but as one set of underweighted inputs into a multi-causal system. Every claim on every page is tagged with a confidence level. Every story names the competing explanations a hostile editor would raise. Every section flags what would falsify it.

ProvenDirectly documented by primary sources — court filings, certified records, SEC reports.
StrongEstablished by peer-reviewed research or aggregated public data with replicable methodology.
CorrelatedTwo variables move together at the population level. Direction of causation not yet established.
PlausibleMechanism is theoretically coherent and partially evidenced. Not yet quantified.
SpeculativePattern-recognition hypothesis. Useful for framing future investigation; not for asserting fact.
CompetingMultiple defensible interpretations exist. We name them rather than choose.

What we are

A navigation system for complex pressure systems. We map structural inputs that traditional political coverage underweights, utility rates, commuter flows, eviction filings, ballot adjudication, donor-committee overlap.

What we are not

Not a prediction engine. Not a verdict. Not a claim that any single mechanism explains an outcome. Voter behavior is multi-causal, identity, media ecosystem, candidate quality, national narratives, and demographics all matter alongside the pressures we trace.

What would falsify it

Each piece names the test that would break its mechanism, what evidence, if found, would change the inference. If we can't name the falsifier, we can't claim the conclusion. That discipline is the spine of the series.
