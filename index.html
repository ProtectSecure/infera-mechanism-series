THE WORK · WHAT THIS IS

One method. Two seasons.

01 · WHAT WE DO

Each episode takes a system most coverage flattens into a headline — a county-level eviction docket, a behavioral-ad auction, a credit-score black box, a Senate runoff — and traces it from publicly sourced records to outcomes you can verify. No anonymous sources. No hostile framing. The pipeline is the article.

02 · HOW WE WORK

Every claim is tagged with one of six confidence levels (Proven · Strong · Correlated · Plausible · Speculative · Competing). Every piece names the falsifier — the evidence that would change the inference. Every section flags its own blind spots. We use the Infera narrative-integrity framework end-to-end and the source-tier classifier on every URL we cite.

03 · WHY IT MATTERS

The premise: shared facts produce shared ground. A reader who disagrees with us politically can still verify the same records and reach a useful conversation. The series is a deliberate counter-frame to coverage that maximizes engagement by maximizing disagreement — built for common ground in America, not against either side of it.

The method · how every piece is built

Every claim, marked. Every blind spot, named. Every conclusion has a falsifier.

The discipline is the spine of the series. Whether a piece is investigating a county magistrate court or a $300B ad-tech market, the same rules apply: every assertion gets a confidence tier, every source gets a tier label, every section flags what would break its own conclusion. This is what lets two readers with very different politics read the same page and disagree about implications without disagreeing about facts.

ProvenDirectly documented by primary sources — court filings, certified records, SEC reports.
StrongEstablished by peer-reviewed research or aggregated public data with replicable methodology.
CorrelatedTwo variables move together at the population level. Direction of causation not yet established.
PlausibleMechanism is theoretically coherent and partially evidenced. Not yet quantified.
SpeculativePattern-recognition hypothesis. Useful for framing future investigation; not for asserting fact.
CompetingMultiple defensible interpretations exist. We name them rather than choose.

What we are

A systems-journalism practice. Each episode picks a system most coverage flattens to a headline and traces it from public records to outcomes you can verify.

What we are not

Not a prediction engine. Not a verdict. Not a claim that any single mechanism explains an outcome. Most things worth covering are multi-causal — we mark our piece alongside the other inputs, not above them.

What would falsify it

Each piece names the test that would break its mechanism — what evidence, if found, would change the inference. If we can't name the falsifier, we can't claim the conclusion.
