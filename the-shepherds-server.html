    The angel's file on his weak spots

    To guard a weakness, it has to write it down. This is what the angel now holds.

    Who decides what counts as a sin?

    • For an angel: the market already keeps this list and sells it; an angel you configure is the only version that works for you. Accountability partners are an old practice; this one is awake at 1 a.m.
    • Against: a list of someone's temptations is the most dangerous file a church could hold. False positives shame people. If a church, not the person, sets the list, guidance becomes surveillance. And an angel from an ad-funded vendor has a conflict of interest.

    REPORTED In 2017, a leaked Facebook document prepared for an advertiser described detecting when young users felt "defeated," "stressed" or "overwhelmed"; Facebook said advertisers cannot target by emotional state and that no campaign resulted. The FTC's 2022 staff report on dark patterns names fake countdown timers and false scarcity among design tricks that bypass normal decisions. In 2022, California's first privacy settlement required a retailer to treat the Global Privacy Control browser signal as a valid opt-out of the sale of data, which is the real tool behind "Starve the profile."     BOTH SIDES, AT THEIR STRONGEST

    Should a church minister through a data center?

    Yes, carefully: the church should be there

    • Churches already hold intimate records: giving, counseling notes, prayer chains, often in spreadsheets and rented apps. A church-governed server could be safer than what exists now.
    • Two in five younger adults already trust AI spiritual advice as much as a pastor's. If churches do not build something accountable, unaccountable apps will fill the gap.
    • People ask machines what they are too embarrassed to ask a person; Vallejo members say exactly that. A system that hears it and hands it to a human can reach someone who would never walk into an office.
    • Data can route real help: a meal train after a job loss, a recovery group, a ride to the hospital.
    • A congregation can write a charter, elect its stewards and say no to vendors in a way an individual user cannot.

    No: some things should not be stored at all

    • Confession heals partly because it is forgotten. A log can be subpoenaed, breached, inherited by the next pastor or sold with the software company.
    • Privilege law was written for spoken words to a minister. Whether it covers a server log is untested, and the chatbot industry's own leader says AI chats carry no privilege today.
    • Inference crosses consent. Being ministered to for a struggle you never named, because a system guessed it, is surveillance even when kindly meant.
    • Small churches run on volunteers, the institutions least able to secure the most sensitive data in a town.
    • 79% of pastors fear AI as a replacement for God; the Vatican calls the substitution idolatry. An always-available guardian may quietly replace the slower work of people showing up.
    WHAT THIS EXHIBIT DID NOT PROVE

    We found no public record of a US congregation operating its own on-site server to host members' personal data or AI. Room F describes designs, not existing churches.

    The Gloo figures come from a 2021 newspaper profile as described by later outlets; we did not read the original. MIT Technology Review withdrew its own 2025 story about the company. Treat the data-driven profile in Room D as a description of reported practice, not a finding about any church today.

    The Barna surveys were produced with Gloo, a company that sells church technology. Ghost's and Oki's privacy promises are the companies' claims; we did not test the devices.

    We found no court ruling on whether clergy-penitent privilege covers a church AI's records. That is our reading of what is missing, not a legal opinion. Magnifica Humanitas is quoted from a published summary, not the full text.

    FORM YOUR OWN READ

    Five questions before a congregation builds a guardian angel

    1. If the server were subpoenaed tomorrow, what would be on it, and would every member who spoke to it be comfortable with that?
    2. Does anything the system knows about a person come from inference rather than from that person telling us?
    3. When the pastor leaves, who inherits the conversations, and can a member take theirs with them or erase it?
    4. Does every serious matter end with a human being, and does the machine know when to stop?
    5. Are we building this so people are known, or so they are measured?
