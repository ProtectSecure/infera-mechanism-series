I.· PRESSURE · WHAT NOBODY NAMED AT THE TIME

Every outcome you watched in Season I was produced by the same composite scoring Season II spent six episodes describing.

No single act of sorting is dramatic. The compounding effect across years and across categories is what produces what social scientists call cumulative disadvantage and what this episode will call, more honestly, engineered class.

The machine does not intend to stratify. It just does. Compounding optimization, run at scale, produces a digital caste the optimizer would never have written down. That is the mechanism of the engine.

Each of the five families above appeared in a different Season I story. They were not in different stories. III.· OUTCOME · NOT ADDITIVE · MULTIPLICATIVE

The compounding effect across the five chapters is not additive. It is multiplicative.

A household sorted disadvantageously in any one chapter is statistically more likely to be sorted disadvantageously in the others — because the same composite feeds all five.

The honest social-science term is cumulative disadvantage. The honest editorial term is engineered class. The episode uses both, in that order, so the reader sees the documented language first and the editorial framing second.

Each of the five anchor families in this episode can be traced through at least one chapter besides their anchor. Maria + Devon's housing outcome runs through healthcare (the medical debt) and credit (the FICO drop). Dorothy's healthcare outcome runs through civic (her polling distance after closure) and credit (the prior-authorization economic impact). LaToya's civic outcome runs through housing (the within-ZIP move that triggered the broker lag) and employment (her social-worker employer participates in algorithmic-hiring infrastructure her own composite feeds).

Each family is at least double-anchored. The compounding integration is the engine.

IV.· COMPETING · THE DEFENSES, HOSTED FAIRLY

Four positions each hold in part. The episode hosts each, then names where each breaks down.

a.

No single actor's intent

No housing platform, no scoring vendor, no insurance underwriter intended to produce a digital caste. The result is emergent from many separate optimization decisions, each rational in isolation.

b.

Net gains for many users

Lower friction, faster decisions, broader access in some categories. Removing the algorithmic layer would not return the system to an equitable baseline; it would return it to slower manual decisions that produced their own well-documented disparities.

c.

Some outcomes pre-existed the mechanism

Inequality preceded composite scoring. The machine accelerates and refines existing patterns more than it invents new ones. The honest empirical question is magnitude attribution, not pattern existence.

d.

Data + optimization are publicly auditable

Regulatory remedies exist in some jurisdictions. The EU AI Act is operational. NYC AEDT is live. The system is not a black box if anyone with standing chooses to look inside.

⟶ Three hosted defender voices · spoken in their own framing

DEFENDER 1
FAccT-community algorithmic-fairness researcher
Outreach in progress · 14-day response window
"What this episode calls an 'engineered class' is a real pattern, and it deserves a serious name. But the FAccT community has been working on this problem for a decade, and the tools are more mature than the piece allows. The mechanisms' opacity is contingent, not necessary. The regulatory horizon is also more developed: the EU AI Act, the Colorado AI Act, the NYC AEDT law, the Illinois AIVIA. The trajectory is toward visibility, not away from it."

Real and worth acknowledging. But cumulative-disadvantage holds even if every individual decision passes a formal-fairness audit, because the same composite feeds all five domains. The mechanism is auditable; the integration across five auditable domains largely is not. No NYC AEDT-equivalent regulates the meta-pattern of an applicant scored for tenancy, then hiring, then insurance, then credit, then voter eligibility — by overlapping composites flowing through overlapping infrastructure to overlapping institutional capital.

DEFENDER 2
Industry / vendor / trade-association voice
Outreach in progress · 14-day response window
"The framing of an 'engineered class' attributes intentionality and coordination to a market that has neither. No tenant-screening vendor, no hiring-platform operator, no insurance underwriter sets out to produce stratification. Each system is built around well-established compliance frameworks — FCRA, FHA, Title VII, HIPAA, ECOA, the Voting Rights Act. The mechanisms have also produced measurable gains. The honest accounting is not algorithm-vs-fair-system; it is algorithm-vs-manual-system, and the manual baseline was not fair either."

True at each point. The episode does not rebut intentionality (no one is claiming intentionality) or compliance-framework existence. But the compliance frameworks the defender names were designed around single-decision discrimination. None of them governs the cross-decision integration that produces cumulative disadvantage. ECOA does not prevent the same composite from feeding both a credit decision and a tenant-screening decision. The compliance frameworks are necessary; they are not sufficient.

DEFENDER 3
Sociological skeptic of cumulative-disadvantage framing
Outreach in progress · 14-day response window
"The cumulative-disadvantage literature is real and worth engaging with, but it has been criticized on technical grounds. The framework can be made to fit almost any pattern of accumulated outcome. When magnitudes are measured carefully, the algorithmic-mechanism contribution to cumulative disadvantage tends to be smaller than the pre-existing-inequality baseline by an order of magnitude or more. The mechanism may be real; the share of the outcome it explains is the empirically open question."

Fair. The episode does not claim the algorithmic layer explains all of any chapter's outcome — only that it provides a sufficient and increasingly dominant channel for the compounding observed. The Class Atlas's confidence-chip feature surfaces the per-chain confidence breakdown. The skeptic's argument that "magnitude is the open question" is not contradicted; it is the question the episode invites the reader to extend.

V.· DISCIPLINE · WHAT WE PROVED · WHAT WE DID NOT

The receipts in this episode are the most defensible the publication has shipped. They are also labeled honestly.

What we DID prove

  • The mechanisms in EP 02-07 produce outcomes of the kind documented in S1·01-S1·10
  • At least one named family per chapter can be traced from documented S1 outcome to specific S2 mechanism via inference chain
  • The compounding effect across categories is multiplicative because the same composite feeds all five
  • The pattern matches the definition of cumulative disadvantage in the published literature

What we did NOT prove

  • That all five Season I families in this episode were sorted by the same scoring vendor
  • That provisional-ballot challenges in S1·05 used the same composite as targeting in EP 04 (we showed architectures are compatible)
  • That Hospital Closes record dispersal goes to the same Composite State infrastructure described in EP 06 (we showed architectures are compatible)
  • That removing the algorithmic layer would eliminate the underlying disparity

⟶ Three founding refusals

i.
No conspiracy.
The machine is not coordinated. The result is not designed. Naming it a class engine is naming a systemic pattern, not a plot.
ii.
No determinism.
Not every reader is sorted the same way. Not every chapter applies equally to every household. Every modeled aggregate is labeled as modeled.
iii.
No despair.
EP 12 The Governance Layer will name the political instruments that could change any of this. EP 08 hands the reader off with the conviction that outcomes are produced, not given.
