BOTH SIDES, AT THEIR STRONGEST

Is a new separation of church and state needed?

No: the law should apply in every building, and cameras keep people safe

  • DHS: "Criminals will no longer be able to hide in America's schools and churches to avoid arrest." ICE says it does not target places of worship. REPORTED
  • Even the injunctions allow arrests with a warrant, in pursuit of a suspect, or in an emergency. REPORTED
  • A federal judge ruled in January that photographing plates on public roads and searching the database is not a Fourth Amendment search. REPORTED
  • Churches face real threats. The Family Research Council counted 415 hostile incidents against U.S. churches in 2024 and 485 in 2023, from open sources. Many congregations installed their own cameras for this reason. REPORTED
  • Streams serve the 8% of adults who worship only online, many of them sick, elderly or far away. FACT

Yes: worship needs a protected space, and the data outlives the service

  • An appeals court found the policy change "already has caused declines in attendance" among plaintiff Baptist and Sikh congregations. REPORTED
  • Another appeals court ruled in October that members of the Episcopal Church and 26 partner groups "likely face imminent harm." REPORTED
  • An ICE agent ran about 983 plate searches in six states through his wife's local police login, outside the safeguards Flock describes. REPORTED
  • ICE's Mobile Fortify app checks faces against more than 270 million biometric records; ICE says photos are kept 15 years, even for citizens. REPORTED
  • The plaintiffs argue under the First Amendment and the Religious Freedom Restoration Act that fear of being seen is itself a burden on worship. REPORTED
WHAT THIS EXHIBIT DID NOT PROVE

We found no public record of ICE using a church livestream to identify or locate anyone. WATCH The stream risk in Exhibit B is a capability, not a documented practice. The Minnesota surveillance targeted protest organizing in church buildings, not worship services. Flock's limits on federal access are the company's own description. The incident counts for churches come from an advocacy group's open-source tally.

What would change this read: a court record showing livestream footage used in an enforcement action; the merits rulings now ordered in the DC case; audits of plate-reader searches near houses of worship.

FORM YOUR OWN READ

Five questions for a congregation deciding whether to stream

  1. Who is in our room who cannot afford to be seen, and who is at home who cannot come in?
  2. Does our camera need faces to carry the service, or would the pulpit and the choir do?
  3. How long do we keep the archive, and who decided that?
  4. Who holds our parking lot footage, and what would we do with a request for it?
  5. Have we asked the people in the pews?
