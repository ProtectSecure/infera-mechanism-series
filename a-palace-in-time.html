A Palace in Time

The feed was built to fill the hours a Sabbath was built to empty.

Abraham Joshua Heschel called the seventh day "a palace in time." Shabbat, the Lord's Day and the Friday prayer each set hours apart. The attention economy treats every hour alike. Four rooms on who owns the hours a tradition set apart, then the charter your choices across this series add up to.

    BOTH SIDES, AT THEIR STRONGEST

    Do screens erode sacred time, or extend it?

    Technology extends practice

    • About a quarter of US adults watch services online or on TV in a month, and most regular viewers are highly satisfied. FACT
    • A Catholic prayer app's downloads jump about 25 times on Ash Wednesday: digital tools can move millions onto a liturgical calendar. FACTdata provider estimate
    • A Bible app reports a billion installs across thousands of languages, reach print never had. REPORTEDcompany figure

    The attention economy erodes it

    • A jury found Meta and YouTube negligent in designing their platforms in March 2026; the plaintiff's lawyers pointed to infinite feeds, autoplay and notifications. REPORTEDappeals pending
    • Internal documents alleged a habit can form in 260 videos, and a screen-time prompt saved about a minute and a half a day. REPORTEDallegation, disputed by the company
    • Religious writers from Heschel to Pope Leo XIV, who asked Catholics in Lent to turn off their phones "for a while," treat silence as a condition of faith. FACT
    • What this exhibit did not prove. We did not show that phones reduce religious practice; no study we found measured it. The teen overnight figure comes from one study of Android users.
    • The lawsuit claims in Room B are allegations, and the jury verdict is under appeal. Faith-app details come from a 2013 analysis, a data provider's estimates and press reports; we did not test the apps.
    • Buddhist, Bahá'í and Hindu rhythms of rest are not covered here because we could not source them properly in time. That is a gap, not a judgment.
    FINALE · THE CHARTER

    Nine exhibits, nine clauses. Your congregation's charter.

    Each exhibit ended with a choice. Where you made one, it is already here; change any clause. This is the working form of a standard we call "HIPAA for the Pews." HIPAA is a federal health-privacy law that does not apply to most congregations; we borrow the name only because people recognize it. Nothing you choose leaves this browser.

    Our congregation's data charter · draft

      FORM YOUR OWN READ

      Five questions for a week.

      1. Which hours does our tradition set apart, and do our own apps and channels respect them?
      2. Do we send notifications on the day we ask people to rest?
      3. Who in our congregation cannot step away from a phone, and how do we keep them included?
      4. Is rest something we practice together, or something we tell individuals to manage?
      5. When did our congregation last stop, all at once, on purpose?
      THE END OF THE FAITH LAYER

      That is the last room. There is nothing here to press.
